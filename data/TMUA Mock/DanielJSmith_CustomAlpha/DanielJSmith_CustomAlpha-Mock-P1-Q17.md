## 题目
We say a real function $f(x)$ is *continuous* at a point $x_0\in\mathbb R$ if for all $\varepsilon>0$ there exists $\delta>0$ such that for all $x\in\mathbb R$, if $|x-x_0|<\delta$ then $|f(x)-f(x_0)|<\varepsilon$ Which one of the following statements is true if and only if $f$ is **not** continuous at $x_0$?

$$ \mathbf{A} \quad \text{There exists} \varepsilon>0 \text{such that for every} \delta>0 \text{there exists} x \text{with} |x-x_0|<\delta \text{and} |f(x)-f(x_0)|\ge\varepsilon $$
$$ \mathbf{B} \quad \text{For every} \varepsilon>0 \text{and every} \delta>0 \text{there exists} x \text{with} |x-x_0|<\delta \text{and} |f(x)-f(x_0)|\ge\varepsilon $$
$$ \mathbf{C} \quad \text{There exists} \varepsilon>0 \text{and} \delta>0 \text{such that for all} x \text{with} |x-x_0|<\delta \text{we have} |f(x)-f(x_0)|\ge\varepsilon $$
$$ \mathbf{D} \quad \text{There exists} \varepsilon>0 \text{such that for every} \delta>0 \text{we have} |x-x_0|\ge\delta \text{or} |f(x)-f(x_0)|\ge\varepsilon $$
$$ \mathbf{E} \quad \text{For every} \varepsilon>0 \text{and every} \delta>0 \text{there exists} x \text{with} |x-x_0|<\delta \text{and} |f(x)-f(x_0)|<\varepsilon $$
$$ \mathbf{F} \quad \text{There exists} \delta>0 \text{such that for all} \varepsilon>0 \text{and all} x \text{with} |x-x_0|<\delta \text{we have} |f(x)-f(x_0)|<\varepsilon $$
$$ \mathbf{G} \quad \text{For every} \varepsilon>0 \text{there exists} \delta>0 \text{such that there exists} x \text{with} |x-x_0|<\delta \text{and} |f(x)-f(x_0)|\ge\varepsilon $$
$$ \mathbf{H} \quad \text{There exists} \varepsilon>0 \text{such that for every} \delta>0 \text{if} |x-x_0|<\delta \text{then} |f(x)-f(x_0)|\ge\varepsilon $$
