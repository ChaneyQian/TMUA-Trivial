## 题目
A student attempts to prove the following statement. Consider the integers $a$ and $b$, where $a$ has remainder 1 when divided by 3, and $b$ has remainder 2 when divided by 3. Then $a+b$ is always divisible by 3. Consider the following attempt: $$\begin{aligned}&\text{Let } a=3n+1 \;\text{ and }\; b=3n+2 && \text{(I)}\\[4pt] &\text{then }\; a+b=3n+1+3n+2 && \text{(II)}\\[4pt] &\text{so }\; a+b=6n+3 && \text{(III)}\\[4pt] &\text{so }\; a+b=3(2n+1) && \text{(IV)}\\[4pt] &\text{therefore } a+b \text{ is always divisible by 3.} && \text{(V)}\end{aligned}$$ Which of the following best describes this proof?

$$ \mathbf{A} \quad \text{The statement is true and the proof is completely correct.} $$
$$ \mathbf{B} \quad \text{The statement is true but there is an error in the proof in line (I)} $$
$$ \mathbf{C} \quad \text{The statement is true but there is an error in the proof in line (II)} $$
$$ \mathbf{D} \quad \text{The statement is not true and there is an error in the proof in line (III)} $$
$$ \mathbf{E} \quad \text{The statement is not true and there is an error in the proof in line (IV)} $$
$$ \mathbf{F} \quad \text{The statement is not true and there is an error in the proof in line (V)} $$
