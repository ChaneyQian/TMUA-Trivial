## 题目
An equilateral triangle has centre $O$ and side length $2$. A straight line through $O$ intersects the triangle at two distinct points $P$ and $Q$. The minimum possible length of $PQ$ is
$$\mathbf{A} \quad \frac{2}{3}$$
$$\mathbf{B} \quad \frac{1}{2}$$
$$\mathbf{C} \quad \frac{2\sqrt{3}}{3}$$
$$\mathbf{D} \quad \frac{4}{3}$$
$$\mathbf{E} \quad \sqrt{3}$$
$$\mathbf{F} \quad \sqrt{2}$$

## 答案
D

## 解析
For an equilateral triangle of side $2$ the inradius is $\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$, the circumradius is $\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}$ and the altitude is $\sqrt{3}$. Take the chord parallel to a side: since the centroid divides each median in the ratio $2 : 1$ from the vertex, that chord sits at two thirds of the way down from the apex, where the triangle's width is $\frac{2}{3}$ of the base, giving length $\frac{2}{3} \times 2 = \frac{4}{3}$. Take instead the chord along a median: its length is the full altitude $\sqrt{3} \approx 1.732$. Sweeping the direction of the line through a full turn, the length varies continuously between these two extremes and is stationary exactly at the symmetric positions, so the smallest value is attained when the chord is parallel to a side:
$$PQ_{\min} = \frac{4}{3}.$$
The answer is D. Option C, $\frac{2\sqrt{3}}{3} = \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} \approx 1.155$, is the circumradius: it looks like a plausible short length but it is smaller than every chord through $O$, so it cannot be attained.
