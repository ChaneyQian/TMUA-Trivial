## 题目
How many real solutions are there to the equation
$$2\tan^4 x - 5\tan^2 x + 3 = 0$$
in the interval $0 \leq x < 2\pi$?
$$\mathbf{A} \quad 0$$
$$\mathbf{B} \quad 1$$
$$\mathbf{C} \quad 2$$
$$\mathbf{D} \quad 3$$
$$\mathbf{E} \quad 4$$
$$\mathbf{F} \quad 5$$
$$\mathbf{G} \quad 6$$
$$\mathbf{H} \quad 7$$
$$\mathbf{I} \quad 8$$

## 备注

### 我的备注

### AI备注


## 答案
I

## 解析
Put $t = \tan^2 x$, so the equation becomes $2t^2 - 5t + 3 = 0$, that is $(2t - 3)(t - 1) = 0$, with roots $t = 1$ and $t = \frac{3}{2}$. Both are positive, so each yields two values of $\tan x$, namely $\tan x = \pm 1$ and $\tan x = \pm\sqrt{\tfrac{3}{2}}$. Since $\tan$ has period $\pi$, each single value of $\tan x$ is attained exactly twice in an interval of length $2\pi$, so the total is
$$2 \times 2 \times 2 = 8.$$
The answer is I. Option E, $4$, is what you get by solving only $\tan^2 x = 1$ and discarding the root $t = \frac{3}{2}$ as though it were inadmissible.
