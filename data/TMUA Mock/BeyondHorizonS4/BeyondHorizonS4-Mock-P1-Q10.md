## 题目
In the expansion of $(a + bx)^4$ the coefficient of $x^3$ is 4 times the coefficient of $x^2$. Given that $a$ and $b$ are non-zero positive integers, what is the smallest possible value of $a + b$?
$$\mathbf{A} \quad 5$$
$$\mathbf{B} \quad 6$$
$$\mathbf{C} \quad 7$$
$$\mathbf{D} \quad 11$$
$$\mathbf{E} \quad 15$$
$$\mathbf{F} \quad 19$$

### 我的备注

### AI备注


## 答案
C

## 解析
In $(a + bx)^4$ the coefficient of $x^3$ is $\binom{4}{3}ab^3 = 4ab^3$ and the coefficient of $x^2$ is $\binom{4}{2}a^2b^2 = 6a^2b^2$. The stated condition is $4ab^3 = 4 \cdot 6a^2b^2$, and dividing through by the non-zero quantity $4ab^2$ leaves $b = 6a$. Hence $a + b = 7a$, and with $a$ a positive integer the smallest possible value is
$$a + b = 7.$$
The answer is C. Options A and B are smaller but unreachable, since $a + b$ is forced to be a multiple of $7$.
