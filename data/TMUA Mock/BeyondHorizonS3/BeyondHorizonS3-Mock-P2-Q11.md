## 题目
Consider the unit circle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$. The locus of a point $P$ such that the tangents $PA, PB$ at the points $A, B$ respectively of the circle are so that $\angle AOB = 60^\circ$, where $O$ is the origin, is
$$\mathbf{A} \quad \text{a circle of radius } \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} \text{ with centre } O$$
$$\mathbf{B} \quad \text{a circle of radius } \sqrt{3} \text{ with centre } O$$
$$\mathbf{C} \quad \text{a circle of radius 2 with centre } O$$
$$\mathbf{D} \quad \text{a pair of straight lines}$$

## 解析
Let $P$ be an external point with tangents touching the unit circle at $A$ and $B$. Since $OA$ and $OB$ are radii and $PA$, $PB$ are tangents, the triangles $OAP$ and $OBP$ are congruent right-angled triangles with the right angles at $A$ and $B$, and $OP$ bisects the angle $\angle AOB$.

Given $\angle AOB = 60^\circ$, the bisected angle is $\angle AOP = 30^\circ$. In the right-angled triangle $OAP$ the hypotenuse is $OP$ and the side adjacent to the $30^\circ$ angle is $OA = 1$, so
$$\cos 30^\circ = \frac{OA}{OP} \quad \Longrightarrow \quad OP = \frac{1}{\cos 30^\circ} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}/2} = \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}.$$
The distance $OP$ is the same constant for every such $P$ and the argument places no restriction on the direction of $OP$, so the locus is precisely the circle of radius $\tfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}}$ centred at $O$. The answer is A.
