## 题目
The difference between the roots of the equation $6x^2 + \alpha x + 1 = 0$ is $\frac{1}{6}$. $\alpha$ is a positive number. The value of $\alpha$ is
$$\mathbf{A} \quad 2$$
$$\mathbf{B} \quad 3$$
$$\mathbf{C} \quad 4$$
$$\mathbf{D} \quad 5$$
$$\mathbf{E} \quad \frac{8}{3}$$
$$\mathbf{F} \quad 8$$

## 备注

### 我的备注

### AI备注


## 答案
D

## 解析
If the roots are $p$ and $q$ then $p + q = -\frac{\alpha}{6}$ and $pq = \frac{1}{6}$, so
$$(p-q)^2 = (p+q)^2 - 4pq = \frac{\alpha^2}{36} - \frac{2}{3}$$
Setting this equal to $\left(\tfrac16\right)^2 = \tfrac{1}{36}$ gives $\frac{\alpha^2}{36} = \frac{1}{36} + \frac{24}{36}$, so $\alpha^2 = 25$ and, taking the positive value, $\alpha = 5$. The check is immediate: $6x^2 + 5x + 1 = (3x+1)(2x+1)$ has roots $-\tfrac13$ and $-\tfrac12$, whose difference is $\tfrac16$. The answer is D. Option A is the trap for anyone who uses $pq = 1$ instead of $pq = \tfrac16$.
