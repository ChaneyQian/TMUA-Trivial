## 题目
Evaluate the following integral (integration by parts is not needed)
$$\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1} \ln x\,dx$$
$$\mathbf{A} \quad \frac{\ln 2}{2}$$
$$\mathbf{B} \quad \frac{\ln 2-1}{2}$$
$$\mathbf{C} \quad \frac{1-\ln 2}{2}$$
$$\mathbf{D} \quad -\ln 2$$
$$\mathbf{E} \quad \frac{-\ln 2}{2}$$

## 备注

### 我的备注

### AI备注


## 答案
B

## 解析
An antiderivative of $\ln x$ is $x\ln x-x$, as differentiating gives $\ln x+1-1=\ln x$, so no integration by parts is required once that antiderivative is quoted. Evaluating at the upper limit gives $1\cdot 0-1=-1$, and at the lower limit gives $\tfrac12\ln\tfrac12-\tfrac12=-\tfrac{\ln 2}{2}-\tfrac12$. Subtracting yields
$$-1-\left(-\frac{\ln 2}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\right)=\frac{\ln 2}{2}-\frac{1}{2}=\frac{\ln 2-1}{2}.$$
The value is negative, around $-0.153$, which is consistent with $\ln x$ being negative throughout $\left[\tfrac12,1\right)$. The answer is B.
