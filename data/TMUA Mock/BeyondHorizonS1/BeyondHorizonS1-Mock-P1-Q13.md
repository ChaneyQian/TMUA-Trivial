## 题目
The line $x = y$ is tangent at $(0,0)$ to a circle of radius $1$. The centre of the circle is
$$\mathbf{A} \quad (1, 0)$$
$$\mathbf{B} \quad \text{either } \left( \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}, \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \right) \text{ or } \left( -\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}, -\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \right)$$
$$\mathbf{C} \quad \text{either } \left( \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}, -\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \right) \text{ or } \left( -\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}, \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \right)$$
$$\mathbf{D} \quad (0, 1)$$
$$\mathbf{E} \quad (0, 0)$$
$$\mathbf{F} \quad \text{None of the Above}$$

### AI备注


## 答案
C

## 解析
The radius drawn to the point of tangency is perpendicular to the tangent line, so the centre lies on the line through $(0,0)$ perpendicular to $y = x$, namely $y = -x$, at distance $1$ from the origin. A unit vector along that perpendicular is
$$\pm\left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}, -\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \right)$$
so the centre is either $\left( \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}, -\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2} \right)$ or $\left( -\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}, \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2} \right)$. The answer is C. Option B is the trap: those two points lie on the tangent line itself, so the line would be a diameter rather than a tangent.
