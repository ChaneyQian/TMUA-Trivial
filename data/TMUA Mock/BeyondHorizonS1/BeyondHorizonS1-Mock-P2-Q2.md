## 题目
If $m$ men can do a job in $d$ days, then the number of days in which $m+r$ men can do the job is:
$$\mathbf{A} \quad d+r$$
$$\mathbf{B} \quad \frac{d}{m+r}$$
$$\mathbf{C} \quad \frac{d}{m-r}$$
$$\mathbf{D} \quad \frac{md}{m+r}$$

## 备注

### 我的备注

### AI备注


## 答案
D

## 解析
The total amount of labour required is fixed, and measuring it in man-days it equals $md$. If $m+r$ men now work for $t$ days they supply $(m+r)t$ man-days, so $(m+r)t=md$ and therefore $t=\frac{md}{m+r}$. A sanity check confirms the form: taking $r=0$ returns $t=d$ as it must, and increasing $r$ decreases $t$, which is the expected inverse proportionality between the number of workers and the time taken. The answer is D.
