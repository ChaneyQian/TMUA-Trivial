## 题目
A student attempts to prove the statement For all primes $p>3$, $p^3-3p^2$ has at least 12 factors. Their proof is as follows. (I) $p^3-3p^2=p^2(p-3)$. (II) As $p>3$, $p$ is odd, therefore $p-3=2a$ for some integer $a\ne0$, therefore $p^2(p-3)=p^2\cdot2\cdot a$. (III) If $a=p$, then $p=-3$ which is impossible, so $a\ne p$. (IV) If $N$ has prime factorisation $p_1^{a_1}\cdot p_2^{a_2}\cdots p_n^{a_n}$, then each factor of $N$ has the form $p_1^{b_1}\cdot p_2^{b_2}\cdots p_n^{b_n}$, where $0\le b_i\le a_i$ (for $1\le i\le n$). (V) If $a$ is prime, then $p^2\cdot2^1\cdot a^1$ has $(2+1)(1+1)(1+1)=12$ factors. (VI) If $a$ is composite, then $p^2\cdot2^1\cdot a=p^2\cdot2^1\cdot q^1\cdot b$ for some prime $q$ and integer $b>1$, which has more than 12 factors. Which of the following best describes the proof?

$$ \mathbf{A} \quad \text{The proof is completely correct.} $$
$$ \mathbf{B} \quad \text{The first error occurs at line **(I)**.} $$
$$ \mathbf{C} \quad \text{The first error occurs at line **(II)**.} $$
$$ \mathbf{D} \quad \text{The first error occurs at line **(III)**.} $$
$$ \mathbf{E} \quad \text{The first error occurs at line **(IV)**.} $$
$$ \mathbf{F} \quad \text{The first error occurs at line **(V)**.} $$
$$ \mathbf{G} \quad \text{The first error occurs at line **(VI)**.} $$
