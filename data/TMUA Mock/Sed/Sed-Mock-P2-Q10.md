## 题目
Consider the following statement: A function $f(x)$ is *uniformly continuous* if and only if for all $\varepsilon>0$, there exists $\delta>0$ such that for all $u$ and $v$, if $|u-v|<\delta$, then $|f(u)-f(v)|<\varepsilon$. It follows that $f(x)$ is **not** uniformly continuous if and only if:

$$ \mathbf{A} \quad \text{For all} \varepsilon>0 \text{, there exist} \delta>0 \text{and} u,v \text{such that} |u-v|\ge\delta \text{and} |f(u)-f(v)|\ge\varepsilon \text{.} $$
$$ \mathbf{B} \quad \text{For all} \varepsilon>0 \text{, there exist} \delta>0 \text{and} u,v \text{such that} |u-v|<\delta \text{and} |f(u)-f(v)|\ge\varepsilon \text{.} $$
$$ \mathbf{C} \quad \text{For all} \varepsilon,\delta>0 \text{, there exist} u,v \text{such that} |u-v|<\delta \text{and} |f(u)-f(v)|<\varepsilon \text{.} $$
$$ \mathbf{D} \quad \text{For all} \varepsilon,\delta>0 \text{and} u,v \text{,} |u-v|\ge\delta \text{and} |f(u)-f(v)|\ge\varepsilon \text{.} $$
$$ \mathbf{E} \quad \text{There exists} \varepsilon>0 \text{where for all} \delta>0 \text{, there exist} u,v \text{such that} |u-v|\ge\delta \text{and} |f(u)-f(v)|\ge\varepsilon \text{.} $$
$$ \mathbf{F} \quad \text{There exists} \varepsilon>0 \text{where for all} \delta>0 \text{, there exist} u,v \text{such that} |u-v|<\delta \text{and} |f(u)-f(v)|\ge\varepsilon \text{.} $$
$$ \mathbf{G} \quad \text{There exist} \varepsilon,\delta>0 \text{and} u,v \text{such that} |u-v|\ge\delta \text{and} |f(u)-f(v)|<\varepsilon \text{.} $$
$$ \mathbf{H} \quad \text{There exist} \varepsilon,\delta>0 \text{such that for all} u \text{and} v \text{,} |u-v|\ge\delta \text{and} |f(u)-f(v)|\ge\varepsilon \text{.} $$
