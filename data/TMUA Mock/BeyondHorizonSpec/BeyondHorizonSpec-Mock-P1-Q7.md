## 题目
Find the general solution to
$$(\cos 2x)^2 - 2\cos 2x - 1 = 0$$
(Note that n is an integer here)
$$\mathbf{A} \quad x = \frac{1}{2}\cos^{-1}(1 + \sqrt{2}) + n\pi$$
$$\mathbf{B} \quad x = \frac{1}{2}\cos^{-1}(1 - \sqrt{2}) + n\pi$$
$$\mathbf{C} \quad x = \frac{1}{2}\cos^{-1}(2) + n\pi$$
$$\mathbf{D} \quad x = \frac{1}{2}\cos^{-1}(-1) + n\pi$$
$$\mathbf{E} \quad x = \frac{1}{2}\cos^{-1}(1) + n\pi$$
$$\mathbf{F} \quad x = \frac{1}{2}\cos^{-1}(-1 + \sqrt{2}) + n\pi$$

## 备注

### 我的备注

### AI备注


## 答案
B

## 解析
Put $c = \cos 2x$, so the equation is the quadratic $c^2 - 2c - 1 = 0$, whose roots are $c = \frac{2 \pm \sqrt{4+4}}{2} = 1 \pm \sqrt{2}$. The root $1 + \sqrt{2} \approx 2.414$ lies outside $[-1, 1]$ and is therefore rejected, leaving $\cos 2x = 1 - \sqrt{2} \approx -0.414$, which is admissible. Hence $2x = \cos^{-1}(1-\sqrt{2}) + 2n\pi$, and dividing by 2 gives $x = \frac{1}{2}\cos^{-1}(1-\sqrt{2}) + n\pi$. The answer is B.
