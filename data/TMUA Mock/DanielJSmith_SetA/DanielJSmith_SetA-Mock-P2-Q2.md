## 题目
A student is asked to prove the statement: **if** $u$ **and** $v$ are real numbers such that $u^2+v^2=0$, **then** $u=0$ **and** $v=0$ They write the following argument: I. For all real $w$, $w^2\ge 0$ II. So $u^2\ge 0$ **and** $v^2\ge 0$ III. If the sum of two real numbers is $0$ and each is **not** negative, then each number is $0$ IV. Therefore $u^2=0$ **and** $v^2=0$ V. Hence $u=0$ **and** $v=0$ Which of the following best describes this argument?

$$ \mathbf{A} \quad \text{It is a correct direct proof of the statement.} $$
$$ \mathbf{B} \quad \text{It is incorrect, because line I is false.} $$
$$ \mathbf{C} \quad \text{It is incorrect, because line III is false.} $$
$$ \mathbf{D} \quad \text{It is incorrect, because line IV does not follow from the previous lines.} $$
$$ \mathbf{E} \quad \text{It is incorrect, because the reasoning would only work if} u \text{**and**} v \text{were positive.} $$
$$ \mathbf{F} \quad \text{It is a correct proof of the converse, but **not** of the original statement.} $$
