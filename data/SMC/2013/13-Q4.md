## 题目
A route on the $3 \times 3$ board shown consists of a number of steps. Each step is from one square to an adjacent square of a different colour. How many different routes are there from square S to square T which pass through every other square exactly once?

![[Image/13-Q4-fig1.png|160]]

A 0 $\qquad$ B 1 $\qquad$ C 2 $\qquad$ D 3 $\qquad$ E 4

## 答案
C

## 解析
For convenience, label the other squares as in the left-hand figure. The first move of a route from $S$ to $T$ must be either $S \to M$ or $S \to Q$. It is easy to see that a route that visits all the squares must include both the sequence $M \to K \to L$ and the sequence $Q \to R \to P$. Hence, we see that there are just two routes that meet all the required conditions; these are shown in two figures on the the right.
