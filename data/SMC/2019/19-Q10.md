## 题目
Which of the following five values of $n$ is a counterexample to the statement in the box below?

> For a positive integer $n$, at least one of $6n - 1$ and $6n + 1$ is prime.

A 10 $\qquad$ B 19 $\qquad$ C 20 $\qquad$ D 21 $\qquad$ E 30

## 答案
C

## 解析
A counterexample to the statement in the box is a value of $n$ for which it is not true that at least one of $6n-1$ and $6n+1$ is prime. That is, it is a value of $n$ for which neither $6n-1$ nor $6n+1$ is prime.

We set out the values of $6n-1$ and $6n+1$ for $n = 10,\ 19,\ 20,\ 21$ and $30$ in the following table.

| $n$ | $6n-1$ | $6n+1$ |
| :-: | :----: | :----: |
| 10 | 59 | 61 |
| 19 | 113 | **115** |
| 20 | **119** | **121** |
| 21 | **165** | 167 |
| 30 | 179 | 181 |

The values of $6n-1$ and $6n+1$ that are not prime are shown in bold.

We therefore see that for $n = 20$, neither $6n-1$ nor $6n+1$ is prime. Therefore $n = 20$ provides the required counterexample.
