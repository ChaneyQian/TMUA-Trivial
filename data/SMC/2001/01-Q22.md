## 题目
For how many values of $n$ are both $n$ and $4^{\left(\frac{n-1}{n+1}\right)}$ integers?

A 1 $\qquad$ B 2 $\qquad$ C 3 $\qquad$ D 4 $\qquad$ E 5

### AI备注


## 答案
E

## 解析
$4^{\left(\frac{n-1}{n+1}\right)}$ is an integer if the value of $\dfrac{n-1}{n+1}$ is $0$, $\dfrac{1}{2}$, $1$, $\dfrac{3}{2}$, … i.e. $\dfrac{n-1}{n+1} = \dfrac{k}{2}$ where $k = 0$, $1$, $2$, $3$, …

Let $\dfrac{k}{2} = \dfrac{n-1}{n+1} = \dfrac{n+1-2}{n+1} = 1 - \dfrac{2}{n+1}$. Then $k = 2 - \dfrac{4}{n+1}$.

For $k$ to be an integer, the only possible values of $n + 1$ are $-4$, $-2$, $-1$, $1$, $2$, $4$; i.e. the possible values of $n$ are $-5$, $-3$, $-2$, $0$, $1$, $3$.

The corresponding values of $k$ are $3$, $4$, $6$, $-2$, $0$, $1$. However, $k$ cannot be negative and therefore $n = 0$ is not valid.

Therefore the five possible values of $n$ are $-5$, $-3$, $-2$, $1$, $3$.
