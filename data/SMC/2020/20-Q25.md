## 题目

## 解析
The sum of the internal angles of a polygon with $m$ sides is $(m-2)180^\circ$ [see Problem 25.1]. Hence each internal angle of the regular $m$-gon is $\left(\dfrac{m-2}{m}\right)180^\circ$, and similarly for the regular $n$-gon and the regular $p$-gon. Therefore, because the sum of the angles at a point is $360^\circ$, we have

$$\left(\frac{m-2}{m}\right)180+\left(\frac{n-2}{n}\right)180+\left(\frac{p-2}{p}\right)180=360.$$

It follows that

$$\frac{m-2}{m}+\frac{n-2}{n}+\frac{p-2}{p}=2.$$

We may rewrite this last equation as $\left(1-\dfrac{2}{m}\right)+\left(1-\dfrac{2}{n}\right)+\left(1-\dfrac{2}{p}\right)=2$. Hence

$$\frac1m+\frac1n+\frac1p=\frac12.\qquad(1)$$

We note that as $p$ is a positive integer, it follows from (1) that

$$\frac1m+\frac1n<\frac12.\qquad(2)$$

We seek a solution of (1), where $m$, $n$ and $p$ are positive integers and $p$ is as large as possible.

For $p$ to be as large as possible, $\dfrac1p$ needs to be as small as possible. Hence, by (1), $\dfrac1m+\dfrac1n$ needs to be as large as possible, subject to the inequality (2). Thus $m$ and $n$ need to be as small as possible. Without loss of generality we may assume that $m\le n$.

By (2), $m\ge 3$. If $m=3$, then $\dfrac1n<\dfrac12-\dfrac13=\dfrac16$, and hence $n>6$. When $m=3$ and $n=7$, we have $\dfrac1m+\dfrac1n=\dfrac13+\dfrac17=\dfrac{10}{21}$. Hence, by (1), $\dfrac1p=\dfrac12-\dfrac{10}{21}=\dfrac{1}{42}$ and so $p=42$. We show that this gives the largest possible value of $p$.

We cannot have $m=n=4$, as this is not compatible with the inequality (2).

If $n>m\ge 4$, we have $\dfrac1m+\dfrac1n\le\dfrac14+\dfrac15=\dfrac{9}{20}<\dfrac{10}{21}$ and so $\dfrac1m+\dfrac1n$ is not as large as possible.

Hence the largest possible value of $p$ is given by $m=3$, $n=7$ and $p=42$.
