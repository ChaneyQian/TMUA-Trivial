## 题目
The diagram shows two overlapping triangles; an isosceles triangle with an angle of $120^\circ$ and an equilateral triangle with area 36. Two of the vertices of the equilateral triangle are midpoints of the equal sides of the isosceles triangle.

![[Image/18-Q22-fig1.png|300]]

What is the total area of the shaded regions (inside the isosceles triangle but outside the equilateral triangle)?

A 24 $\qquad$ B 26 $\qquad$ C 28 $\qquad$ D 30 $\qquad$ E 32

## 解析
> **Commentary**
>
> This problem may be tackled in many different ways. We first give a full solution. We then sketch two more solutions, leaving the details to the reader. You may well find other methods.

**Method 1**

We let the vertices of the isosceles triangle with an angle of $120^\circ$ be $P$, $Q$ and $R$, and those of the equilateral triangle be $K$, $L$ and $M$, as shown in the diagram below.

We also let $N$ be the midpoint of $KM$ and $S$ and $T$ be the points where $QR$ meets the lines $KL$ and $ML$, respectively.

We also suppose that the equilateral triangle $KLM$ has sides of length $2s$

![[Image/18-Q22-sol1.png]]

The triangle $PQR$ is isosceles with $PQ = PR$, and $K$ and $M$ are the midpoints of $PQ$ and $PR$, respectively. Therefore $PK = PM$. Since $N$ is the midpoint of $KM$ we also have $KN = NM$. Therefore the triangles $PKN$ and $PMN$ have sides of the same lengths. Hence they are congruent. Therefore the angles of both these triangles are $90^\circ$, $60^\circ$ and $30^\circ$, as shown in the diagram.

Similarly, the triangles $KNL$ and $MNL$ have sides of the same lengths, and so are congruent. It follows that they also have angles of $90^\circ$, $60^\circ$ and $30^\circ$ as shown.

Because the triangle $PQR$ is isosceles with $PQ = PR$, it follows that $\angle PQR = \angle PRQ$. Therefore, as $\angle QPR = 120^\circ$ and the sum of the angles in a triangle is $180^\circ$, it follows that $\angle PQR = \angle PRQ = 30^\circ$.

The angles on the line $PQ$ at $K$ have sum $180^\circ$. It follows that $\angle QKS = 90^\circ$. Therefore $QKS$ is yet another triangle with angles $90^\circ$, $60^\circ$ and $30^\circ$. Similarly, the same is true of the triangle $RMT$. Because $QK = RM$, it follows that the triangles $QKS$ and $RMT$ are congruent.

We have therefore seen that $KPN$, $QSK$ and $LKN$ are similar triangles with angles $90^\circ$, $60^\circ$ and $30^\circ$.

By considering a triangle with these angles as half of an equilateral triangle, we see that in such a triangle the hypotenuse and other two sides are in the ratio $2 : \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} : 1$.

![[Image/18-Q22-sol2.png]]

Therefore, as $KN = s$, it follows that $KP = \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}}s$.

Hence $QK = KP = \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}}s$ and therefore $QS = \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}} \times \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}}s = \dfrac{4}{3}s$.

The ratio of the areas of similar triangles equals the ratio of the squares of their linear dimensions.

We have seen that the similar triangles $KPN$, $QSK$ and $LKN$ have hypotenuses of lengths $\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}}s$, $\dfrac{4}{3}s$ and $2s$, respectively. These are in the ratio $\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}} : \dfrac{4}{3} : 2$. Hence the areas of these triangles are in the ratio $\dfrac{4}{3} : \dfrac{16}{9} : 4$, which is equivalent to $\dfrac{1}{3} : \dfrac{4}{9} : 1$.

Therefore the areas of the triangles $KPN$ and $QSK$ are, respectively, $\dfrac{1}{3}$ and $\dfrac{4}{9}$ of the area of the triangle $LKN$. Now $\dfrac{1}{3} + \dfrac{4}{9} = \dfrac{7}{9}$. Hence the sum of the areas of the triangles $KPN$ and $QSK$ is $\dfrac{7}{9}$ of the area of the triangle $LKN$.

Similarly the sum of the areas of the triangles $MPN$ and $TRM$ is $\dfrac{7}{9}$ of the area of the triangle $LMN$.

It follows that the area of the shaded region is $\dfrac{7}{9}$ of the area of the equilateral triangle. Hence, the shaded area is $\dfrac{7}{9} \times 36 = 28$.

**Method 2**

Let $O$ be the midpoint of $QR$, and join $O$ to $K$, $L$ and $M$.

It can be checked that the triangles $PKM$, $KOM$, $LOK$ and $MOL$ are all congruent. It follows that the area of the triangle $PKM$ is one third of the area of $KLM$, and hence the triangle $PKM$ has area $12$.

It can also be proved that the length of $ST$ is $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of the length of $KM$. Hence the area of the triangle $LTS$ is $\dfrac{4}{9}$ of the area of $KLM$, that is $16$.

![[Image/18-Q22-sol3.png]]

It can also be checked that the triangles $KQS$, $MTR$, $SLO$ and $LTO$ are congruent.

Therefore the sum of the areas of the triangles $KQS$ and $MTR$ is equal to the area of the triangle $STL$, that is, $16$.

Hence the shaded area is $12 + 16 = 28$.

**Method 3**

*The third solution we give is more visual. It relies on properties of the diagrams which would need detailed justification if a full solution were required.*

Consider the diagram in Figure 3, below, which shows the original shape, outlined by heavy lines, together with two copies of it, rotated by $\pm 120^\circ$.

This shows that the hatched area in Figure 3 is equal to one third of the original equilateral triangle, that is, $12$. We also see that the large equilateral triangle has area $4 \times 36 = 144$.

Now consider Figure 4, which shows the same diagram, superimposed by several smaller equilateral triangles.

![[Image/18-Q22-sol4.png]]

This shows that the hatched area in Figure 4 is equal to one ninth of the large equilateral triangle, that is, $16$.

Hence the shaded area in the original figure is $12 + 16 = 28$.
