## 题目
Which of the following values of the positive integer $n$ is a counterexample to the statement: “If $n$ is not prime then $n - 2$ is not prime”?

A 6 $\qquad$ B 11 $\qquad$ C 27 $\qquad$ D 33 $\qquad$ E 51

## 答案
D

## 解析
A counterexample to the statement “If $n$ is not prime then $n - 2$ is not prime” is a positive integer $n$ for which this implication is false. An implication of the form “If $P$ then $Q$” is false if $P$ is true, but $Q$ is false.

Therefore a counterexample to the statement is a positive integer $n$ for which it is true that $n$ is not prime, but false that $n - 2$ is not prime. In other words, a counterexample is a positive integer $n$ such that $n$ is not prime but $n - 2$ is prime.

Since $11$ is prime, option B does not provide the required counterexample.

The options $6$, $27$, $33$ and $51$ are not primes. However $6 - 2 = 4$, $27 - 2 = 25$ and $51 - 2 = 49$ are also not primes. Therefore none of the options A, C and E provides the required counterexample.

This leaves $n = 33$. We see that $33$ is not prime, but $33 - 2 = 31$, is prime. Therefore $33$ is a counterexample. Hence the correct option is D.
