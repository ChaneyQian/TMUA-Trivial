## 题目
A square has its vertices on the edges of a regular hexagon. Two of the edges of the square are parallel to two edges of the hexagon, as shown in the diagram. The sides of the hexagon have length 1.

![[Image/23-Q24-fig1.png|202]]

What is the length of the sides of the square?

A $\dfrac{5}{4}$ $\qquad$ B $3 - \sqrt{3}$ $\qquad$ C $\dfrac{4}{3}$ $\qquad$ D $\sqrt{2}$ $\qquad$ E $\dfrac{3}{2}$

## 答案
B

## 解析
We label the vertices of the hexagon $P$, $Q$, $R$, $S$, $T$ and $U$, and the vertices of the square $V$, $W$, $X$ and $Y$, as shown in the diagram.

Also, we let $K$ and $L$ be the feet of the perpendiculars from $P$ and $U$, respectively, to $VY$, and we let $M$ be the point where $VW$ meets $QT$.

We let the length of the sides of the square be $s$.

The internal angles of the regular hexagon are $120^\circ$. The figure is symmetrical about the line $QT$. Therefore $\angle VQM = 60^\circ$, $\angle VMQ = 90^\circ$ and $VM = \dfrac{s}{2}$.

![[Image/23-Q24-sol1.png]]

From the right-angled triangle $VMQ$, we have $\dfrac{VM}{VQ} = \sin 60^\circ = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$. Therefore $VQ = \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}}VM = \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}} \times \dfrac{s}{2} = \dfrac{s}{\sqrt{3}}$.

$KLUP$ is a rectangle. Therefore $KL = PU = 1$. Therefore, $VK + LY = VY - KL = s - 1$. By symmetry, $VK = LY$. Hence $VK = \dfrac{s-1}{2}$.

$\angle PKV = 90^\circ$ and $\angle PVK = 60^\circ$. Therefore in the triangle $PVK$ we have $\dfrac{VK}{PV} = \cos 60^\circ = \dfrac{1}{2}$. Hence $PV = 2VK = s - 1$.

Since $PQ = 1$, we have $PV + VQ = 1$, and hence $s - 1 + \dfrac{s}{\sqrt{3}} = 1$. Hence $s\left(1 + \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\right) = 2$. That is, $s\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{3} + 1}{\sqrt{3}}\right) = 2$. Therefore $s = \dfrac{2\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3} + 1} = \dfrac{2\sqrt{3}(\sqrt{3} - 1))}{(\sqrt{3} + 1)(\sqrt{3} - 1)} = \dfrac{6 - 2\sqrt{3}}{2} = 3 - \sqrt{3}$.
