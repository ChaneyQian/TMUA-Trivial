## 题目
Jamie conducted a survey on the food preferences of pupils at a school and discovered that 70% of the pupils like pears, 75% like oranges, 80% like bananas and 85% like apples. What is the smallest possible percentage of pupils who like all four of these fruits?

A at least 10% $\qquad$ B at least 15% $\qquad$ C at least 20% $\qquad$ D at least 25% $\qquad$ E at least 70%

## 答案
A

## 解析
We use the idea that if at least $x\%$ of the pupils are in a category $X$ and at least $y\%$ are in a category $Y$, then at least $(x + y - 100)\%$ are in both categories. For suppose $z\%$ are in both categories. Then at least $(x-z)\%$ are in $X$ but not $Y$, and at least $(y-z)\%$ in $Y$ but not $X$. We must have that $(x-z)\% + z\% + (y-z)\% \le 100\%$, and hence $(x + y - 100)\% \le z\%$.

![[Image/11-Q17-sol1.png]]

Now, as $70\%$ of the pupils like pears and $75\%$ like oranges, at least $(70 + 75 - 100)\% = 45\%$ like both pears and oranges. Hence, as $80\%$ like bananas, at least $(45 + 80 - 100)\% = 25\%$ like pears, oranges and bananas. Therefore, as $85\%$ like apples, at least $(25 + 85 - 100)\% = 10\%$ like all four fruits.
