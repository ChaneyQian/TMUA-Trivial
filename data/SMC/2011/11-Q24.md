## 题目
Three circles and the lines $PQ$ and $QR$ touch as shown. The distance between the centres of the smallest and the biggest circles is 16 times the radius of the smallest circle. What is the size of $\angle PQR$?

![[Image/11-Q24-fig1.png|300]]

A $45^\circ$ $\qquad$ B $60^\circ$ $\qquad$ C $75^\circ$ $\qquad$ D $90^\circ$ $\qquad$ E $135^\circ$

## 解析
We let the centres of the circles be $S$, $T$ and $U$, and the points where they touch $PQ$ be $V$, $W$ and $X$, as shown. We let the radii of the circles be $a$, $b$ and $c$, with $a < b < c$ as shown, let $QS = d$, and let $\angle PQU = \alpha$.

![[Image/11-Q24-sol1.png]]

Then $QT = d + a + b$ and $QU = d + a + 2b + c$. Because $PQ$ is a tangent to all three circles, the triangles $QVS$, $QWT$ and $QXU$ have right angles at $V$, $W$ and $X$, respectively. Therefore

$$\sin\alpha = \frac{a}{d} = \frac{b}{d+a+b} = \frac{c}{d+a+2b+c}.$$

Since $\dfrac{a}{d} = \sin\alpha$, we have $a = d\sin\alpha$.

Therefore, as $\dfrac{b}{d+a+b} = \sin\alpha$, we have $b = (d + a + b)\sin\alpha$. Hence $b - b\sin\alpha = (d + d\sin\alpha)\sin\alpha$ and therefore $b = \dfrac{d\sin\alpha(1+\sin\alpha)}{1-\sin\alpha}$.

Hence, as $\dfrac{c}{d+a+2b+c} = \sin\alpha$, we have $c = (d + a + 2b + c)\sin\alpha$. Hence

$$c - c\sin\alpha = \left(d + d\sin\alpha + \frac{2d\sin\alpha(1+\sin\alpha)}{(1-\sin\alpha)}\right)\sin\alpha$$

and therefore

$$c = \frac{d\sin\alpha}{(1-\sin\alpha)}\left(1 + \sin\alpha + \frac{2\sin\alpha(1+\sin\alpha)}{(1-\sin\alpha)}\right) = \frac{d\sin\alpha}{(1-\sin\alpha)^2}\big((1+\sin\alpha)(1-\sin\alpha) + 2\sin\alpha(1+\sin\alpha)\big)$$

$$= \frac{d\sin\alpha(1+\sin\alpha)^2}{(1-\sin\alpha)^2}.$$

Now, as the distance between the centres of the smallest and largest circles is 16 times the radius of the largest circle, $a + 2b + c = 16a$, and thus

$$d\sin\alpha + \frac{2d\sin\alpha(1+\sin\alpha)}{(1-\sin\alpha)} + \frac{d\sin\alpha(1+\sin\alpha)^2}{(1-\sin\alpha)^2} = 16d\sin\alpha.$$

We can divide through by $d\sin\alpha$ as this cannot be 0. We then obtain

$$1 + 2\left(\frac{1+\sin\alpha}{1-\sin\alpha}\right) + \left(\frac{1+\sin\alpha}{1-\sin\alpha}\right)^2 = 1,$$

that is,

$$\left(1 + \left(\frac{1+\sin\alpha}{1-\sin\alpha}\right)\right)^2 = 16, \text{ or, equivalently, } \left(\frac{2}{1-\sin\alpha}\right)^2 = 16.$$

It follows that $\dfrac{2}{1-\sin\alpha} = \pm 4$, giving $1 - \sin\alpha = \pm\dfrac{1}{2}$, and hence that $\sin\alpha = \dfrac{1}{2}$, or $\sin\alpha = \dfrac{3}{2}$. The latter value is impossible, so $\sin\alpha = \dfrac{1}{2}$, and, as from the geometry of the problem, $0 < \alpha < 90^\circ$, we deduce that $\alpha = 30^\circ$. Therefore $\angle PQR = 2\alpha = 60^\circ$.
