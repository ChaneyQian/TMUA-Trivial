## 题目
The equation $x^2 + ax + b = 0$, where $a$ and $b$ are different, has solutions $x = a$ and $x = b$. How many such equations are there?

A 0 $\qquad$ B 1 $\qquad$ C 3 $\qquad$ D 4 $\qquad$ E an infinity

### AI备注


## 答案
B

## 解析
The equation $x^2 + ax + b = 0$ has the solutions $x = a$ and $x = b$ if and only if $x^2 + ax + b$ may be factorized as $(x-a)(x-b)$, that is, if and only if

$$x^2 + ax + b \equiv x^2 - (a+b)x + ab \qquad (1)$$

Now, equating the coefficients of $x$ and the constant terms, we see that (1) holds if and only if

$$a = -(a+b) \qquad (2)$$

and

$$b = ab \qquad (3)$$

From (3) we have that either $a = 1$ or $b = 0$. If $a = 1$ then, by (2), $b = -2$. If $b = 0$, then by (2) $a = 0$. Therefore, there is just one case, $a = 1$, $b = -2$, where (1) holds and $a \ne b$.
