## 题目
An equilateral triangle of side length $4\text{ cm}$ is divided into smaller equilateral triangles, all of which have side length equal to a whole number of centimetres. Which of the following cannot be the number of smaller triangles obtained?

A 4 $\qquad$ B 8 $\qquad$ C 12 $\qquad$ D 13 $\qquad$ E 16

## 解析
In the context of the SMC, it is enough to see that all the other options are possible. This is shown in the following diagrams:

![[Image/11-Q14-sol1.png]]

However, a complete mathematical solution requires a proof that option C is not possible.

We first note that the big triangle can only be divided into smaller triangles with side lengths $1\text{ cm}$, $2\text{ cm}$ and $3\text{ cm}$. There is room just for one triangle of side length $3\text{ cm}$, and we see, from the second diagram above, that if we have such a triangle in our subdivision, then there isn't room for a triangle of side length $2\text{ cm}$. So the remainder of the subdivision must consist of 7 triangles with side length $1\text{ cm}$. This gives a subdivision into 8 smaller triangles.

We now consider the possible subdivisions into, say, $a$, triangles with side length $1\text{ cm}$ and $b$ triangle with side length $2\text{ cm}$. By considering the areas of these triangles and that of the large triangle, we have that

$$a + 4b = 16, \text{ where } a \text{ and } b \text{ are non-negative integers.} \qquad (1)$$

There are just 5 solutions of (1), as shown in the table. This shows that the only possible values for $a + b$ are 4, 7, 10, 13 and 16. So a subdivision into 12 smaller triangles is not possible. Subdivisions into 4, 7, 10, 13 and 16 smaller triangles are all achievable. The cases 4, 13 and 16 are shown above.

| $a$ | $b$ | $a+b$ |
| --- | --- | ----- |
| 0 | 4 | 4 |
| 4 | 3 | 7 |
| 8 | 2 | 10 |
| 12 | 1 | 13 |
| 16 | 0 | 16 |
