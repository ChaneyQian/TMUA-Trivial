## 题目
A drinks carton is formed by arranging four congruent triangles as shown. $QP = RS = 4\text{ cm}$ and $PR = PS = QR = QS = 10\text{ cm}$.

![[Image/22-Q25-fig1.png|309]]

What is the volume, in $\text{cm}^3$, of the carton?

A $\dfrac{16}{3}\sqrt{23}$ $\qquad$ B $\dfrac{4}{3}\sqrt{2}$ $\qquad$ C $\dfrac{128}{25}\sqrt{6}$ $\qquad$ D $\dfrac{13}{2}\sqrt{23}$ $\qquad$ E $\dfrac{8}{3}\sqrt{6}$

## 解析
The lengths in this question are given in terms of centimetres. However, for convenience, we will ignore these units in the calculations until we reach the final answer.

The drinks carton is in the shape of a pyramid. We therefore use the fact that the volume of a pyramid is given by the formula

$$\text{volume} = \dfrac{1}{3}(\text{area of base} \times \text{height}).$$

We let $M$ be the midpoint of $PQ$ and let $L$ be the midpoint of $RS$.

![[Image/22-Q25-sol1.png]]

The carton may be thought of as made up of two pyramids, one with the triangle $RMS$ as its base and $P$ as its apex, and the other with $RMS$ as its base and $Q$ as its apex.

Since $PS = QS$, and $M$ is the midpoint of $QP$, the triangles $PMS$ and $QMS$ are congruent.

![[Image/22-Q25-sol2.png]]

It follows that the angles $\angle PMS$ and $\angle QMS$ are equal. Because they are angles on a line, it follows that they are both right angles.

Since $\angle PMS$ is a right angle, $PM$ is the height of the pyramid with base $RMS$ and apex $P$. Therefore the volume of this pyramid is $\dfrac{1}{3}(\text{area of } RMS \times PM)$.

Similarly the volume of the pyramid with base $RMS$ and apex $Q$ is $\dfrac{1}{3}(\text{area of } RMS \times MQ)$.

We let $V$ be the volume of the carton.

We now have that

$$\begin{aligned} V &= \dfrac{1}{3}(\text{area of } RMS \times PM) + \dfrac{1}{3}(\text{area of } RMS \times MQ) \\ &= \dfrac{1}{3}(\text{area of } RMS \times (PM + MQ)) \\ &= \dfrac{1}{3}(\text{area of } RMS \times PQ). \qquad (1) \end{aligned}$$

We therefore now calculate the area of the triangle $RMS$.

Since the triangles $PRQ$ and $PSQ$ are congruent it may be shown that $RM = SM$ (see Problem 25.3).

![[Image/22-Q25-sol3.png]]

It follows that the triangles $RML$ and $SML$ are congruent, and hence that $ML$ is at right angles to $RS$.

Therefore

$$\text{area of } RMS = \dfrac{1}{2}(RS \times ML). \qquad (2)$$

By Pythagoras theorem applied to the right-angled triangle $SMP$, we have $MS^2 = PS^2 - PM^2 = 10^2 - 2^2 = 100 - 4 = 96$. Therefore, by Pythagoras' Theorem applied to the right-angled triangle $SLM$, we have $ML^2 = MS^2 - SL^2 = 96 - 2^2 = 92$. Hence $ML = \sqrt{92} = 2\sqrt{23}$.

We can now deduce, by (1) and (2) that

$$\begin{aligned} V &= \dfrac{1}{3}\left(\dfrac{1}{2}(RS \times ML) \times PQ\right) \\ &= \dfrac{1}{6}(RS \times ML \times PQ) \\ &= \dfrac{1}{6}(4 \times 2\sqrt{23} \times 4) \\ &= \dfrac{16}{3}\sqrt{23}. \end{aligned}$$

Hence the volume of the carton is $\dfrac{16}{3}\sqrt{23}\text{ cm}^3$.
