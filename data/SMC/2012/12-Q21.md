## 题目
Which of the following numbers does *not* have a square root in the form $x + y\sqrt{2}$, where $x$ and $y$ are positive integers?

A $17 + 12\sqrt{2}$ $\qquad$ B $22 + 12\sqrt{2}$ $\qquad$ C $38 + 12\sqrt{2}$ $\qquad$ D $54 + 12\sqrt{2}$ $\qquad$ E $73 + 12\sqrt{2}$

## 答案
D

## 解析
Suppose $a + 12\sqrt{2} = (x + y\sqrt{2})^2$, where $a$, $x$ and $y$ are positive integers. It follows that $a + 12\sqrt{2} = x^2 + 2xy\sqrt{2} + 2y^2 = (x^2 + 2y^2) + 2xy\sqrt{2}$. Therefore $xy = 6$ and $x^2 + 2y^2 = a$. Since $x$ and $y$ are positive integers, the only possibilities for $x$ and $y$ are $x = 1, y = 6$; $x = 2, y = 3$; $x = 3, y = 2$ and $x = 6, y = 1$. Therefore the only possibilities for $a$ are $1^2 + 2(6^2) = 73$; $2^2 + 2(3^2) = 22$; $3^2 + 2(2^2) = 17$ and $6^2 + 2(1^2) = 38$. Therefore options A, B, C and E are numbers which have square roots of the required form, but not option D.
