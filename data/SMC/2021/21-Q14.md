## 题目
What is the smallest number of rectangles, each measuring $2\text{ cm}$ by $3\text{ cm}$, which are needed to fit together without overlap to form a rectangle whose sides are in the ratio $5 : 4$ ?

A 10 $\qquad$ B 15 $\qquad$ C 20 $\qquad$ D 30 $\qquad$ E 60

## 答案
D

## 解析
A rectangle whose sides are in the ratio $5 : 4$ has dimensions $5k\text{ cm} \times 4k\text{ cm}$, for some positive number $k$. Since we aim to cover this rectangle with $2\text{ cm} \times 3\text{ cm}$ rectangles without overlap, $k$ needs to be a positive integer.

The area of the $5k\text{ cm} \times 4k\text{ cm}$ rectangle is $(5k \times 4k)\text{ cm}^2 = 20k^2\text{ cm}^2$. The $2\text{ cm} \times 3\text{ cm}$ rectangles have area $6\text{ cm}^2$.

It follows that $20k^2$ needs to be a multiple of $6$. The least positive integer $k$ for which this is the case is $3$. In this case the larger rectangle has dimensions $15\text{ cm} \times 12\text{ cm}$ and area $(15 \times 12)\text{ cm}^2 = 180\text{ cm}^2$. Therefore the smallest number of $2\text{ cm} \times 3\text{ cm}$ rectangles that are needed would be $\dfrac{180}{6} = 30$, provided that it is possible to cover a $15\text{ cm} \times 12\text{ cm}$ with $30$ $2\text{ cm} \times 3\text{ cm}$ rectangles.
