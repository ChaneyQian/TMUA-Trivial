## 题目
The factorial of $n$, written $n!$, is defined by $n! = 1 \times 2 \times 3 \times \dots \times (n - 2) \times (n - 1) \times n$. Which of the following values of $n$ provides a counterexample to the statement:

“If $n$ is a prime number, then $n! + 1$ is also a prime number”?

A 1 $\qquad$ B 2 $\qquad$ C 3 $\qquad$ D 4 $\qquad$ E 5

## 备注

### 我的备注

### AI备注


## 答案
E

## 解析
It is necessary to test only $n = 2$, $n = 3$, $n = 5$ since the other two possible values are not prime. $2! + 1 = 3$, which is prime; $3! + 1 = 7$, which is prime; but $5! + 1 = 121$, which is not prime. So $n = 5$ provides the counterexample.
