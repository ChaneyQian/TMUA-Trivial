## 题目
The quadratic function shown passes through $(2,0)$ and $(q,0)$ , where $q > 2$.

![[Image/20-P1-Q11-fig1.jpg]]

What is the value of $q$ such that the area of region $R$ equals the area of region $S$ ?

\mathbf {A} \quad \sqrt{6}
$$

$$

$$

## 解析
At first sight, it seems that there may not be enough information to answer the question, as we only know two points on the quadratic. But if we stretch the graph parallel to the y-axis, though we modify the quadratic, we will not change q, and the areas of R and S will remain equal. So let us proceed. For simplicity, we will write R and S for the areas of these regions.

The quadratic has equation $y = k ( x - 2 ) ( x - q )$ . By the above comments, we may as well take $k = 1$ ; if we leave k in the equation, the areas will each be multiplied by $k$, and when we equate them, we can then divide by k.

The area of R is

$$
\begin{array}{l} R = \int_ {0} ^ {2} (x - 2) (x - q) \mathrm{d} x \\ \quad = \int_ {0} ^ {2} x ^ {2} - (q + 2) x + 2 q \mathrm{d} x \\ \quad = \left[ \frac {1}{3} x ^ {3} - \frac {1}{2} (q + 2) x ^ {2} + 2 q x \right] _ {0} ^ {2} \\ \quad = \frac {8}{3} - 2 (q + 2) + 4 q \\ \quad = 2 q - \frac {4}{3} \end{array}
$$

The area of S is as follows; note that the integral is negative, so we have to negate the integral to obtain the area.

$$
\begin{array}{l} S = - \int_ {2} ^ {q} (x - 2) (x - q) \mathrm{d} x \\ \quad = - \int_ {2} ^ {q} x ^ {2} - (q + 2) x + 2 q \mathrm{d} x \\ \quad = - \left[ \frac {1}{3} x ^ {3} - \frac {1}{2} (q + 2) x ^ {2} + 2 q x \right] _ {2} ^ {q} \\ \quad = - \left(\frac {1}{3} q ^ {3} - \frac {1}{2} (q + 2) q ^ {2} + 2 q ^ {2}\right) + \left(\frac {8}{3} - 2 (q + 2) + 4 q\right) \\ \quad = - \left(- \frac {1}{6} q ^ {3} + q ^ {2}\right) + \left(2 q - \frac {4}{3}\right) \\ \quad = \frac {1}{6} q ^ {3} - q ^ {2} + 2 q - \frac {4}{3} \end{array}
$$

As $R = S$ we have

$$
2 q - \frac {4}{3} = \frac {1}{6} q ^ {3} - q ^ {2} + 2 q - \frac {4}{3}
$$

and therefore $\begin{array} { r } { { \frac { 1 } { 6 } } q ^ { 3 } - q ^ { 2 } = 0 } \end{array}$ . As $q > 2$ , we cannot have $q = 0$ , so we can divide by $q ^ { 2 }$ to give $\begin{array} { r } { { \frac { 1 } { 6 } } q - 1 = 0 } \end{array}$ . It follows that $q = 6$ , which is option E.

You may have noticed that the value of the integral at $x = 2$ appeared in both expressions and then cancelled out when we equated them. Another way to think about this is that as the integral from 0 to 2 is positive and the integral from 2 to $q$ is negative, then if the areas $R$ and $S$ are the same, we must have the integral from 0 to q equal to 0. So we could have calculated:

$$
\begin{array}{r l} & 0 = \int_ {0} ^ {q} (x - 2) (x - q) \mathrm{d} x \\ & \quad = \int_ {0} ^ {q} x ^ {2} - (q + 2) x + 2 q \mathrm{d} x \\ & \quad = \left[ \frac {1}{3} x ^ {3} - \frac {1}{2} (q + 2) x ^ {2} + 2 q x \right] _ {0} ^ {q} \\ & \quad = - \frac {1}{6} q ^ {3} + q ^ {2} \end{array}
$$

giving $q = 6$ as before.
