## 题目
A student makes the following claim:

For all integers $n$ , the expression $4\left(\frac{9n + 1}{2} - \frac{3n - 1}{2}\right)$ is divisible by 3. Here is the student's argument:

$$
4 \left(\frac {9 n + 1}{2} - \frac {3 n - 1}{2}\right) = 2 \left(2 \left(\frac {9 n + 1}{2} - \frac {3 n - 1}{2}\right)\right)\tag{I}
$$

$$
= 2 (9 n + 1 - 3 n - 1)\tag{II}
$$

$$
= 2 (6 n)\tag{III}
$$

$$
= 1 2 n\tag{IV}
$$

$$
= 3 (4 n)\tag{V}
$$

$$
\text { which   is   always   a   multiple   of   3. }\tag{VI}
$$

So the expression $4\left(\frac{9n + 1}{2} - \frac{3n - 1}{2}\right)$ is always divisible by 3.

Which one of the following is true?

$$
\mathbf {A} \quad \text{The argument is correct.}
$$

$$
\mathbf {B} \quad \text{The argument is incorrect, and the first error occurs on line (I).}
$$

$$
\mathbf {C} \quad \text{The argument is incorrect, and the first error occurs on line (II).}
$$

$$
\mathbf {D} \quad \text{The argument is incorrect, and the first error occurs on line (III).}
$$

$$
\mathbf {E} \quad \text{The argument is incorrect, and the first error occurs on line (IV).}
$$

$$
\mathbf {F} \quad \text{The argument is incorrect, and the first error occurs on line (V).}
$$

$$
\mathbf {G} \quad \text{The argument is incorrect, and the first error occurs on line (VI).}
$$

## 答案
C

## 解析
Lines (I) to (V) are algebraic manipulations; we need to check whether each manipulation is correct.

Line (I) writes $4 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 .$ , which is correct.

Line (II) multiplies out the inner brackets, but does not do it correctly: the inner brackets should expand to $( 9 n + 1 ) - ( 3 n - 1 )$ , which simplifies to $9 n + 1 - 3 n + 1$

The remaining manipulations are correct.

Thus the first error occurs on line (II), and the correct option is C.
