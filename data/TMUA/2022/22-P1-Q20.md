## 题目
$n$ is the number of points of intersection of the graphs

$$
y = |x^2 - a^2| \quad \text{and} \quad y = a^2|x - 1|
$$

where $a$ is a real number.

What is the smallest value of $n$ that is **not** possible?

$$
\mathbf{A} \quad n = 1
$$

$$
\mathbf{B} \quad n = 2
$$

$$
\mathbf{C} \quad n = 3
$$

$$
\mathbf{D} \quad n = 4
$$

$$
\mathbf{E} \quad n = 5
$$

B

## 解析
The graph $y = | x ^ { 2 } - a ^ { 2 } |$ intersects the x-axis at $\pm a$ . The graph $y = a ^ { 2 } \left| x - 1 \right|$ intersects the x-axis at $x = 1$ . Replacing a with −a does not change the equations, so we may as well assume that $a \geq 0$

Here is a sketch of the graphs with $a = 2 ;$ we note that the graphs always intersect at $x = 0$ (and $y = a ^ { 2 }$ there):

![[Image/22-P1-Q20-sol1.jpg]]

We immediately see that when $a > 1$ , there will be 4 points of intersection: the graph $y = a ^ { 2 } | x - 1 |$ is below the $\mathrm { ^ { 6 6 } h i l l } ^ { \dag }$ at $( 1 , 0 )$ , so intersects it twice as x moves away from $x = 1$ , and then intersects the quadratic $y = x ^ { 2 } - a ^ { 2 }$ for some $x < - a$ and some $x > a$ as the quadratic grows faster than the linear $y = - a ^ { 2 } ( x - 1 )$ or $y = a ^ { 2 } ( x - 1 )$ in these regions.

What happens when $a = 1 2$ In this case, the equations become $y = | x ^ { 2 } - 1 |$ and $y = x - 1$ . The derivative of the quadratic $y = x ^ { 2 } - 1$ is 2x, so the gradient is just over 2 when x is just over 1. (There is no gradient at the point $x = 1 . )$ ) Therefore the graph looks like this:

![[Image/22-P1-Q20-sol2.jpg]]

We see that we have lost one point of intersection: there are now only three points of intersection.

What happens if $0 < a < 1 ?$ Now the vertex of $y = a ^ { 2 } | x - 1 |$ will be to the right of $x = a ,$ , where the quadratic meets the axis, and so there will be no points of intersection there – the quadratic grows too quickly to meet the linear function for $x > 1$ . (To be more precise: the gradient of the quadratic to the right of $x = a$ is $2 x > 2 a > a ^ { 2 }$ , so the quadratic will be growing faster than the linear function.)

On the left of $x = a ,$ , we have one point of intersection to the left of $x = - a$ as before. There is also a point of intersection at $( 0 , a ^ { 2 } )$ . Since this is the vertex of the quadratic, the linear function must go below the “hill”, giving another two points of intersection, yielding four points of intersection in total:

![[Image/22-P1-Q20-sol3.jpg]]

There is one remaining case to consider: $a = 0$ . In this case, the graphs become $y = | x ^ { 2 } | = x ^ { 2 }$ and $y = 0$ , and these graphs have a single point of intersection at the origin.

Therefore the possible numbers of points of intersection are 1, 3 and 4, and the smallest number not possible is $n = 2$ , option B.
