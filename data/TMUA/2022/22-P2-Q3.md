## 题目
Consider the following statement about the positive integer $n$

$$
\text{if } n \text{ is prime, } \textbf{then } n^2 + 2 \text{ is } \textbf{not} \text{ prime}
$$

Which of the following is a **counterexample** to this statement?

I. $n = 2$

II. $n = 3$

III. $n = 4$

$$
\mathbf{A} \quad \text{none of them}
$$

$$
\mathbf{B} \quad \text{I only}
$$

$$
\mathbf{C} \quad \text{II only}
$$

$$
\mathbf{D} \quad \text{III only}
$$

$$
\mathbf{E} \quad \text{I and II only}
$$

$$
\mathbf{F} \quad \text{I and III only}
$$

$$
\mathbf{G} \quad \text{II and III only}
$$

$$
\mathbf{H} \quad \text{I, II and III}
$$

## 答案
C

## 解析
I $n = 2$ is prime and $n ^ { 2 } + 2 = 6$ is not prime, so this case satisfies the statement and is not a counterexample.

II $n = 3$ is prime but $n ^ { 2 } + 2 = 1 1$ is prime, so this is a counterexample as it does not satisfy the statement.

III n = 4 is not prime, so it satisfies the statement and is not a counterexample.

Therefore only II provides a counterexample, which is option C.
