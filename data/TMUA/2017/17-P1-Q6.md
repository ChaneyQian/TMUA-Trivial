## 题目
A tangent to the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 144$ passes through the point $(20, 0)$ and crosses the positive $y$-axis.

What is the value of $y$ at the point where the tangent meets the $y$-axis?

$$
\mathbf{A} \quad 12
$$

$$
\mathbf{B} \quad 15
$$

$$
\mathbf{C} \quad \frac{49}{3}
$$

$$
\mathbf{D} \quad 20
$$

$$
\mathbf{E} \quad \frac{64}{3}
$$

$$
\mathbf{F} \quad \frac{80}{3}
$$

B

## 解析
This calls for a sketch. The circle $x ^ { 2 } + y ^ { 2 } = 1 4 4$ is centred on the origin and has radius 12:

![[Image/17-P1-Q6-sol1.jpg]]

It’s not exact, and it’s not drawn to scale, but it’s good enough to work with. The blue radius is perpendicular to the tangent line. We are trying to find the value of $k$.

## Approach 1

We could work out the equation of the tangent line. It has gradient $m$ (which we don’t know) and passes through (20, 0), so it has equation $y = m ( x - 2 0 )$ . Since it is tangent to the circle, solving the two equations simultaneously must give one repeated root.

We have $x ^ { 2 } + y ^ { 2 } = 1 4 4$ into which we can substitute $y = m ( x - 2 0 )$ giving

$$
\begin{array}{r l r} & & x ^ {2} + m ^ {2} (x - 2 0) ^ {2} = 1 4 4 \\ & \Longleftrightarrow & x ^ {2} + m ^ {2} (x ^ {2} - 4 0 x + 4 0 0) = 1 4 4 \\ & \Longleftrightarrow & (m ^ {2} + 1) x ^ {2} - 4 0 m ^ {2} x + 4 0 0 m ^ {2} - 1 4 4 = 0 \end{array}
$$

We want this to have exactly one root for x, so we need the discriminant to equal zero, giving

$$
(- 4 0 m ^ {2}) ^ {2} - 4 (m ^ {2} + 1) (4 0 0 m ^ {2} - 1 4 4) = 0.
$$

We can take out a factor of $4 ^ { 2 }$ :

$$
(1 0 m ^ {2}) ^ {2} - (m ^ {2} + 1) (1 0 0 m ^ {2} - 3 6) = 0
$$

and then expand the brackets to give

$$
1 0 0 m ^ {4} - 1 0 0 m ^ {4} + 3 6 m ^ {2} - 1 0 0 m ^ {2} + 3 6 = 0
$$

so $6 4 m ^ { 2 } = 3 6$ , or $8 m = \pm 6$ . Since $m < 0$ (as the line intersects the positive $y \mathrm { - a x i s ) }$ , we have $\begin{array} { r } { m = - \frac { 6 } { 8 } = - \frac { 3 } { 4 } } \end{array}$ . Thus the tangent line has equation

$$
y = - \frac {3}{4} (x - 2 0)
$$

Substituting in $( x , y ) = ( 0 , k )$ gives $k = 1 5$ , and hence the answer is B.

## Approach 2

We might notice from the sketch that we have some similar triangles, since the radius meets the tangent at right angles. If we label all of the vertices and equal angles, we will be able to describe things more precisely:

![[Image/17-P1-Q6-sol2.jpg]]

So the triangles $\triangle O Q R , \triangle P Q O$ and $\triangle P O R$ are all similar. We also know the lengths $O R = 2 0$ and $O Q = 1 2$ (as it is a radius). By Pythagoras’s Theorem, applied to $\triangle O Q R$ , we can calculate $Q R = 1 6$ (and we might note that $\triangle O Q R$ is a 3-4-5 triangle). We can then use the similarity of triangles $\triangle P Q O$ and $\triangle O Q R$ to find k:

$$
\frac {P O}{Q O} = \frac {O R}{Q R} \implies \frac {k}{1 2} = \frac {2 0}{1 6}
$$

giving $k = 1 5$ , as before.

Commentary: You might prefer one or other of these approaches. The second is shorter, but requires some confidence with geometry and recognising similar triangles.
