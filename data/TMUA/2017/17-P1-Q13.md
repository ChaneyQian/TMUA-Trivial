## 题目
In the expansion of $(a + bx)^5$ the coefficient of $x^4$ is 8 times the coefficient of $x^2$.

Given that $a$ and $b$ are non-zero **positive** integers, what is the smallest possible value of $a + b$?

$$
\mathbf{A} \quad 3
$$

$$
\mathbf{B} \quad 4
$$

$$
\mathbf{C} \quad 5
$$

$$
\mathbf{D} \quad 9
$$

$$
\mathbf{E} \quad 13
$$

$$
\mathbf{F} \quad 17
$$

## 答案
C

## 解析
The term in $x ^ { 4 }$ is $\binom { 5 } { 4 } a ( b x ) ^ { 4 } = 5 a b ^ { 4 } x ^ { 4 }$ , so the coefficient of $x ^ { 4 }$ is $5 a b ^ { 4 }$

The term in $x ^ { 2 }$ is ${ \binom { 5 } { 2 } } a ^ { 3 } ( b x ) ^ { 2 } = 1 0 a ^ { 3 } b ^ { 2 } x ^ { 2 }$ , so the coefficient of $x ^ { 2 }$ is $1 0 a ^ { 3 } b ^ { 2 }$

It follows, since the coefficient of $x ^ { 4 }$ is 8 times that of $x ^ { 2 }$ , that

$$
5 a b ^ {4} = 8 0 a ^ {3} b ^ {2}
$$

As $a$ and $b$ are non-zero, we can divide by $5 a b ^ { 2 }$ to get $b ^ { 2 } = 1 6 a ^ { 2 }$ . Since both $a$ and $b$ are positive, we can take square roots to get $b = 4 a$ . (If we didn’t know about their signs, we could only say that $b = \pm 4 a . )$ 1

So the smallest possible value of $a + b$ occurs when $a = 1 , b = 4$ , giving $a + b = 5$ , which is option C.
