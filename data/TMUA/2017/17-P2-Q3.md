## 题目
The first term of a geometric progression is $2\sqrt{3}$ and the fourth term is $\frac{9}{4}$

What is the sum to infinity of this geometric progression?

$$
\mathbf{A} \quad -2(2 - \sqrt{3})
$$

$$
\mathbf{B} \quad 4(2\sqrt{3} - 3)
$$

$$
\mathbf{C} \quad \frac{16(8\sqrt{3}+9)}{37}
$$

$$
\mathbf{D} \quad \frac{4(2\sqrt{3}-3)}{7}
$$

$$
\mathbf{E} \quad \frac{4(2\sqrt{3}+3)}{7}
$$

$$
\mathbf{F} \quad 2(2 + \sqrt{3})
$$

$$
\mathbf{G} \quad 4(2\sqrt{3} + 3)
$$

## 答案
G

## 解析
The first term is $a = 2 { \sqrt { 3 } }$ and the common ratio is r. So the fourth term is $\begin{array} { r } { a r ^ { 3 } = 2 \sqrt { 3 } r ^ { 3 } = \frac { 9 } { 4 } } \end{array}$ and hence

$$
r ^ {3} = \frac {9}{8 \sqrt {3}} = \frac {9 \sqrt {3}}{8 \times 3} = \frac {3 \sqrt {3}}{8}
$$

by rationalising the denominator.

We can write $3 \sqrt { 3 }$ as $\sqrt { 2 7 }$ or $3 ^ { \frac { 3 } { 2 } }$ (since we want to take the cube root of this expression), and so we have 3

$$
r ^ {3} = \frac {3 ^ {\frac {3}{2}}}{8} \implies r = \frac {3 ^ {\frac {1}{2}}}{2} = \frac {\sqrt {3}}{2}.
$$

(There is a unique real cube root of any real number, unlike the case for square roots.)

We can now find the sum to infinity of the geometric progression:

$$
S _ {\infty} = \frac {a}{1 - r}
$$

$$
{ \begin{array}{r l} & S _ {\infty} = {\frac {2 {\sqrt {3}}}{1 - {\frac {{\sqrt {3}}}{2}}}} \\ & \qquad = {\frac {4 {\sqrt {3}}}{2 - {\sqrt {3}}}} \\ & \qquad = {\frac {4 {\sqrt {3}} (2 + {\sqrt {3}})}{(2 - {\sqrt {3}}) (2 + {\sqrt {3}})}} \\ & \qquad = {\frac {4 (2 {\sqrt {3}} + 3)}{1}} \\ & \qquad = 4 (2 {\sqrt {3}} + 3) \end{array} }
$$

multiplying by 2

rationalising the denominator

paying attention to the options offered

and the correct answer is G.
