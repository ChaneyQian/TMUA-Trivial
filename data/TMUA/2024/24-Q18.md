## 题目
The expansion of the expression $\left(x^{p} + \frac{1}{x^{q}}\right)^{n}$ has a constant term.

Which of the following is/are necessary and sufficient conditions for this statement for some integer k?

	I: $kp + kq = n$
	II: $kp + kq = nq$
	III: $kp + kq = np$

$$\mathbf{A} \quad \text{Only I}$$

$$\mathbf{B} \quad \text{Only II}$$

$$\mathbf{C} \quad \text{Only III}$$

$$\mathbf{D} \quad \text{I and II}$$

$$\mathbf{E} \quad \text{I and III}$$

$$\mathbf{F} \quad \text{II and III}$$

$$\mathbf{G} \quad \text{All of them}$$

## 答案
F

## 解析
By the binomial theorem the general term of $\left(x^p+x^{-q}\right)^n$ is

$$\binom{n}{k}\left(x^p\right)^{n-k}\left(x^{-q}\right)^{k}=\binom nk x^{\,p(n-k)-qk},\qquad k=0,1,\dots,n .$$

There is a constant term exactly when some $k$ in that range makes the exponent zero:

$$p(n-k)-qk=0\iff k(p+q)=np,$$

which is statement **III**. Conversely, if an integer $k$ satisfies $k(p+q)=np$ then $k=\dfrac{np}{p+q}$ lies between $0$ and $n$ (as $p,q>0$), so the term really occurs. Hence III is necessary and sufficient.

**II** is the same condition counted from the other end. Writing $j=n-k$ for the number of factors contributing $x^{-q}$ — equivalently replacing $k$ by $n-k$ — the exponent condition becomes

$$kp-q(n-k)=0\iff k(p+q)=nq,$$

and $k$ is an integer in $[0,n]$ if and only if $n-k$ is. So II is also necessary and sufficient. (Directly: $k(p+q)=np$ for some integer $k$ iff $(n-k)(p+q)=n(p+q)-np=nq$.)

**I** is not equivalent. Take $p=q=2$ and $n=2$: then $\left(x^2+x^{-2}\right)^2=x^4+2+x^{-4}$ certainly has a constant term, and III reads $4k=4$ with $k=1$, but I reads $4k=2$, which has no integer solution. So I can fail when a constant term exists.

So II and III only. The answer is F.
