## 题目
Find the region satisfying both inequalities

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x+y)^2-8(x+y)+8&<0,\\
x^2+y^2-2xy+4x-4y+3&<0.
\end{aligned}
$$

## 备注

### 我的备注

### AI备注


## 答案
$4-2\sqrt2<x+y<4+2\sqrt2$ and $-3<x-y<-1$

## 解析
Both inequalities involve $x$ and $y$ only through $x+y$ and $x-y$, so set $s=x+y$ and $d=x-y$.

The first inequality is $s^2-8s+8<0$. Its roots are $s=\dfrac{8\pm\sqrt{64-32}}{2}=4\pm2\sqrt2$, and the quadratic is negative between them:

$$4-2\sqrt2<x+y<4+2\sqrt2.$$

For the second, note that $x^2+y^2-2xy=(x-y)^2$ and $4x-4y=4(x-y)$, so it reads

$$d^2+4d+3=(d+1)(d+3)<0\implies-3<x-y<-1.$$

Each condition describes an open strip, and the two families of lines $x+y=\text{const}$ and $x-y=\text{const}$ are perpendicular, so the solution set is the interior of a rectangle. Its sides lie along the four lines

$$x+y=4-2\sqrt2,\quad x+y=4+2\sqrt2,\quad x-y=-3,\quad x-y=-1;$$

its centre is the point with $s=4$, $d=-2$, namely $(1,3)$, and since a strip $c_1<x\pm y<c_2$ has perpendicular width $\dfrac{c_2-c_1}{\sqrt2}$, the side lengths are $\dfrac{4\sqrt2}{\sqrt2}=4$ and $\dfrac{2}{\sqrt2}=\sqrt2$, giving area $4\sqrt2$. The vertices are $\left(\tfrac12-\sqrt2,\tfrac72-\sqrt2\right)$, $\left(\tfrac32-\sqrt2,\tfrac52-\sqrt2\right)$, $\left(\tfrac32+\sqrt2,\tfrac52+\sqrt2\right)$, $\left(\tfrac12+\sqrt2,\tfrac72+\sqrt2\right)$, and the boundary is excluded because both inequalities are strict.

(The original question offered sketches of five candidate regions; those diagrams are missing from the recall source, so the region is described directly here.)

So the answer is the open rectangular region $4-2\sqrt2<x+y<4+2\sqrt2$ together with $-3<x-y<-1$.
