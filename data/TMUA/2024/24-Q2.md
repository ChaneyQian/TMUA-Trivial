## 题目
For positive integers $a$, $b$ and $c$, define three least common multiples: $L = \mathrm{lcm}(a, b)$, $M = \mathrm{lcm}(b, c)$, and $N = \mathrm{lcm}(a, c)$. Which of these is not possible?

$$\mathbf{A} \quad L < M < N$$

$$\mathbf{B} \quad L < M = N$$

$$\mathbf{C} \quad L = M < N$$

$$\mathbf{D} \quad L = M = N$$

$$\mathbf{E} \quad \text{All of them are possible}$$

## 答案
C

## 解析
The useful fact is that each of $a$, $b$, $c$ divides any lcm it appears in, and that $\mathrm{lcm}(a,c)$ is the *least* common multiple of $a$ and $c$: if some integer is a common multiple of $a$ and $c$, then $N=\mathrm{lcm}(a,c)$ divides it.

Suppose $L=M$. Then $a\mid L$ (because $L=\mathrm{lcm}(a,b)$) and $c\mid M=L$ (because $M=\mathrm{lcm}(b,c)$). So $L$ is a common multiple of $a$ and $c$, hence

$$N=\mathrm{lcm}(a,c)\ \big|\ L,\qquad\text{so}\qquad N\le L=M .$$

This rules out $L=M<N$ completely, so option C can never occur.

All the other patterns do occur:

$$\begin{aligned}
(a,b,c)&=(2,1,3): &L&=2,\ M=3,\ N=6 &&\Rightarrow L<M<N,\\
(a,b,c)&=(1,1,2): &L&=1,\ M=2,\ N=2 &&\Rightarrow L<M=N,\\
(a,b,c)&=(1,1,1): &L&=M=N=1.
\end{aligned}$$

So exactly one of the listed patterns is impossible. The answer is C.
