## 题目
Consider the following attempt to solve an equation. The steps have been numbered for reference.

Equation: $3\cos x = \sqrt{x}$, where $x$ is in radians.

Solving Steps:

	(1) Start with the equation: $3\cos x = \sqrt{x}$.
	(2) Square both sides of the equation: $9\cos^{2} x = x$.
	(3) Use the identity $\cos^{2} x = \frac{1}{2}(1 + \cos 2x)$ to rewrite the equation as $9 \cdot \frac{1}{2}(1 + \cos 2x) = x$.
	(4) Graph the functions $y = \frac{1}{2}(1 + \cos 2x)$ and $y = \frac{x}{9}$.
	(5) From the graph, observe that at $x = \pi$, $y = \pi/9 \approx 0.35$ is less than $y = \frac{1}{2}(1 + \cos 2\pi) = 1$, and at $x = 2\pi$, $y = 2\pi/9 \approx 0.70$ is less than 1. The graphs intersect at five points.
	(6) Conclude that the equation has five solutions.

Which one of the following statements is true?

$$\mathbf{A} \quad \text{All five solutions are solutions of the original equation.}$$

$$\mathbf{B} \quad \text{None of the five solutions is a solution of the original equation.}$$

$$\mathbf{C} \quad \text{Only three solutions are valid, and the incorrect solutions arise as a result of step (1).}$$

$$\mathbf{D} \quad \text{Only three solutions are valid, and the incorrect solutions arise as a result of step (2).}$$

$$\mathbf{E} \quad \text{Only three solutions are valid, and the incorrect solutions arise as a result of step (3).}$$

## 答案
D

## 解析
The original equation $3\cos x=\sqrt{x}$ forces $x\ge0$ **and** $\cos x\ge0$, because $\sqrt{x}$ is non-negative. Squaring in step (2) replaces this by

$$9\cos^2x=x,$$

which is satisfied by solutions of $3\cos x=-\sqrt{x}$ as well; this is the standard way squaring introduces extraneous roots. Steps (1) and (3) are harmless — step (1) is just the equation itself, and the double-angle identity $\cos^2x=\tfrac12(1+\cos2x)$ is a genuine identity — so the only possible source of error is step (2).

It remains to check that the loss is real. Since $9\cos^2x=x$ needs $0\le x\le9$, we compare $|\cos x|$ with $\tfrac{\sqrt{x}}{3}$ on $[0,9]$ and find five roots:

$$x\approx1.197,\;2.071,\;3.984,\;5.624,\;6.800,$$

in agreement with the five intersections reported in step (5). Their cosines are approximately

$$0.365,\;-0.480,\;-0.665,\;0.791,\;0.869,$$

so the two with $\cos x<0$ satisfy $3\cos x=-\sqrt{x}$ and are extraneous, while the three with $\cos x>0$ do satisfy $3\cos x=\sqrt{x}$ (for instance $3\cos1.197\approx1.094\approx\sqrt{1.197}$).

So exactly three of the five are solutions of the original equation, and the spurious ones were created by squaring in step (2).

The answer is D.
