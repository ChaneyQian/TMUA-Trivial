## 题目
The lines $x=1$, $y=1$, and the coordinate axes enclose a square. The parabolas

$$y=x^2\qquad\text{and}\qquad y=\frac12x^2+bx-\frac12$$

divide the square into three parts. Find the range of values of $b$.

## 答案
$0<b\le1$

## 解析
The square is $0\le x\le1$, $0\le y\le1$. The first parabola $y=x^2$ runs from the corner $(0,0)$ to the corner $(1,1)$, so on its own it already cuts the square into two parts. For exactly three parts the second parabola must cross the square, and it must not cross $y=x^2$ anywhere inside the square: each extra crossing would create a fourth part.

**The second parabola must cut the square.** Writing $y=\tfrac12x^2+bx-\tfrac12$, it passes through $\left(0,-\tfrac12\right)$, below the square, and meets $y=0$ where $x^2+2bx-1=0$, whose positive root is

$$x_0=-b+\sqrt{b^2+1}.$$

We need $x_0<1$, i.e. $\sqrt{b^2+1}<1+b$, which (squaring, valid only for $b>-1$) gives $b^2+1<1+2b+b^2$, i.e. $b>0$. So for $b\le0$ the curve stays at or below $y=0$ for $0\le x\le1$ (at $x=1$ its height is $b\le0$) and the square is left in two parts.

**The two parabolas must not meet inside the square.** Their intersections satisfy

$$x^2=\tfrac12x^2+bx-\tfrac12\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x^2-2bx+1=0,$$

with roots $x=b\pm\sqrt{b^2-1}$. These are real only when $b\ge1$ (taking $b>0$), and since their product is $1$ the smaller root $b-\sqrt{b^2-1}$ then lies in $(0,1]$. So for $b>1$ there is a genuine interior crossing and the square is cut into four parts, while for $0<b<1$ there is none.

The borderline case $b=1$ is admissible: there

$$\left(\tfrac12x^2+x-\tfrac12\right)-x^2=-\tfrac12(x-1)^2\le0,$$

with equality only at $x=1$, so the two parabolas touch only at the corner $(1,1)$ and the second runs from $(\sqrt2-1,0)$ up to that corner — still three parts. For $b$ slightly above $1$ the contact point moves strictly inside and a fourth part appears.

Hence the square is divided into three parts exactly when $0<b\le1$.

So the answer is $0<b\le1$.
