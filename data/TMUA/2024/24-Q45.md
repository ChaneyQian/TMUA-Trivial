## 题目
If $a + b, b + c, a - c$ are all even

What can be inferred about the parity of $a, b, c$?

$$\mathbf{A} \quad \text{$a$ is even, $b$ and $c$ are odd}$$

$$\mathbf{B} \quad \text{$a$ and $b$ are even, $c$ is odd}$$

$$\mathbf{C} \quad \text{$a$ is odd, $b$ is even, $c$ is odd}$$

$$\mathbf{D} \quad \text{Only one of } a, b, c \text{ is even}$$

$$\mathbf{E} \quad \text{$a$, $b$, $c$ are all odd or all even}$$

## 备注

### 我的备注

### AI备注


## 答案
E

## 解析
Work modulo $2$. A sum or difference of two integers is even exactly when the two integers have the same parity.

Since $a+b$ is even, $a\equiv b\pmod 2$; since $b+c$ is even, $b\equiv c\pmod 2$. Hence

$$a\equiv b\equiv c\pmod 2,$$

so $a,b,c$ are all odd or all even. The third condition adds nothing new: $a\equiv c$ already makes $a-c$ even, so it is automatically satisfied.

Conversely, if $a,b,c$ all have the same parity then $a+b$, $b+c$ and $a-c$ are all even, so this is exactly the right conclusion rather than merely a consequence.

Options A, B, C and D each mix parities, so each fails at least one of the conditions: for example A ($a$ even, $b,c$ odd) makes $a+b$ odd, and D requires $a,b,c$ not to share a parity, contradicting what we proved.

The answer is E.
