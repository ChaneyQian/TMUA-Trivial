## 题目
Find the number of real solutions to the equation $8 \cdot 3^{2x} + 27 \cdot 2^{2x} = 32 \cdot 6^{x}$.

$$\mathbf{A} \quad 1$$

$$\mathbf{B} \quad 2$$

$$\mathbf{C} \quad 4$$

$$\mathbf{D} \quad 0$$

### 我的备注

### AI备注


## 答案
B

## 解析
Every term is homogeneous of degree $2x$ in the bases $2$ and $3$, since $6^x=2^x3^x$. Dividing the whole equation by $2^{2x}>0$ and writing

$$t=\left(\frac32\right)^{x}>0$$

turns it into

$$8t^2-32t+27=0.$$

The discriminant is $32^2-4\cdot8\cdot27=1024-864=160>0$, so there are two distinct real roots

$$t=\frac{32\pm\sqrt{160}}{16}=2\pm\frac{\sqrt{10}}{4}.$$

Both are positive, because $\tfrac{\sqrt{10}}{4}\approx0.79<2$; numerically $t\approx2.791$ and $t\approx1.209$. Since $t\mapsto(\tfrac32)^x$ is a bijection from $\mathbb{R}$ onto $(0,\infty)$, each positive value of $t$ yields exactly one real $x$, namely $x=\log_{3/2}t$ (approximately $2.531$ and $0.469$).

Hence the equation has exactly $2$ real solutions. (A negative root for $t$ would have to be discarded, which is what the answer $1$ would require.)

The answer is B.
