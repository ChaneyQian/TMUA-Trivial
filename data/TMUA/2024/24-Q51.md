## 题目
Let

$$f(x)=p\sin x+q\cos x,$$

where $p$ and $q$ are positive. Given

$$f(x)^2=16\sin^2x+k\sin x\cos x+9,$$

find $k$.

## 备注

### 我的备注

### AI备注


## 答案
$30$

## 解析
Expand $f(x)^2$ and write it in the same form as the right-hand side, using $\cos^2x=1-\sin^2x$ so that only $\sin^2x$ and $\sin x\cos x$ appear:

$$\begin{aligned}
f(x)^2&=p^2\sin^2x+2pq\sin x\cos x+q^2\cos^2x\\
&=p^2\sin^2x+2pq\sin x\cos x+q^2\left(1-\sin^2x\right)\\
&=\left(p^2-q^2\right)\sin^2x+2pq\sin x\cos x+q^2.
\end{aligned}$$

Since $\sin^2x$, $\sin x\cos x$ and $1$ are independent as functions of $x$, comparing with $16\sin^2x+k\sin x\cos x+9$ gives

$$p^2-q^2=16,\qquad 2pq=k,\qquad q^2=9.$$

As $p$ and $q$ are positive, $q=3$ and $p^2=16+9=25$, so $p=5$. Hence

$$k=2pq=2\cdot5\cdot3=30.$$

Checking at $x=\tfrac{\pi}{4}$: $f=\tfrac{5+3}{\sqrt2}$, so $f^2=32$, while $16\cdot\tfrac12+30\cdot\tfrac12+9=32$.

So the answer is $30$.
