## 题目
Given

$$\frac{1}{1 - \sin\theta} = 5$$

find

$$\sum_{r=1}^{\infty} \cos^{r}\left(\theta + \frac{\pi}{2}\right)$$

$$\mathbf{A} \quad 1$$

$$\mathbf{B} \quad -\frac{1}{3}$$

$$\mathbf{C} \quad 3$$

$$\mathbf{D} \quad -\frac{4}{9}$$

$$\mathbf{E} \quad -\frac{2}{3}$$

## 备注

### 我的备注

### AI备注


## 答案
D

## 解析
The sum is a geometric series whose common ratio is $\cos\left(\theta+\tfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$, so all we need is that single value.

From $\dfrac{1}{1-\sin\theta}=5$ we get $1-\sin\theta=\tfrac15$, so $\sin\theta=\tfrac45$. The shift formula gives

$$\cos\left(\theta+\frac{\pi}{2}\right)=-\sin\theta=-\frac45.$$

Since $\left|-\tfrac45\right|<1$ the series converges, and with first term $r=-\tfrac45$ and ratio $-\tfrac45$,

$$\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\cos^{r}\left(\theta+\frac{\pi}{2}\right)=\frac{-\tfrac45}{1-\left(-\tfrac45\right)}=\frac{-\tfrac45}{\tfrac95}=-\frac49.$$

(Starting the sum at $r=0$ instead would give $\tfrac59$; using $+\sin\theta$ for the shift would give $4$.)

The answer is D.
