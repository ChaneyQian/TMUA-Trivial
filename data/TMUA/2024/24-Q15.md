## 题目
Laud Polygon: For any interior angle A, there exists an angle B such that $B = 2A$ or $B = \frac{A}{2}$.

A polygon is quiet if it is not a laud polygon.

Which of the following is/are quiet?

	I: $30^\circ$, $60^\circ$, $90^\circ$
	II: $40^\circ$, $80^\circ$, $80^\circ$, $160^\circ$
	III: $60^\circ$, $120^\circ$, $120^\circ$, $120^\circ$, $120^\circ$

$$\mathbf{A} \quad \text{Only I}$$

$$\mathbf{B} \quad \text{Only II}$$

$$\mathbf{C} \quad \text{Only III}$$

$$\mathbf{D} \quad \text{I and II}$$

$$\mathbf{E} \quad \text{I and III}$$

$$\mathbf{F} \quad \text{II and III}$$

$$\mathbf{G} \quad \text{All of them}$$

## 答案
A

## 解析
By the definition, a polygon is *laud* when **every** interior angle $A$ has a partner angle $B$ in the same polygon with $B=2A$ or $B=\tfrac A2$; it is *quiet* when this fails for at least one of its angles. So to show a polygon is quiet it is enough to exhibit one angle with no partner.

**I: $30^\circ,60^\circ,90^\circ$.** The angle $30^\circ$ is matched by $60^\circ$, and $60^\circ$ is matched by $30^\circ$. But $90^\circ$ would need $180^\circ$ or $45^\circ$, and neither occurs (indeed $180^\circ$ can never be an interior angle). So I is **quiet**.

**II: $40^\circ,80^\circ,80^\circ,160^\circ$.** Here $40^\circ$ is matched by $80^\circ$, each $80^\circ$ by $40^\circ$ (or by $160^\circ$), and $160^\circ$ by $80^\circ$. Every angle has a partner, so II is laud, hence **not quiet**.

**III: $60^\circ,120^\circ,120^\circ,120^\circ,120^\circ$.** Here $60^\circ$ is matched by $120^\circ$ and each $120^\circ$ by $60^\circ$. So III is laud, hence **not quiet**.

Only I is quiet. (Option F, "II and III", lists the **laud** polygons: it is the answer to the opposite question.)

The answer is A.
