## 题目
Given that $f'(x) < 0$ for $0 \leq x \leq 1$, which of the following statements must be true?

	I. $\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}} f(x)\,dx > \int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1} f(x)\,dx$
	II. $\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}} f(x)\,dx < \int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}} f(x^{2})\,dx$
	III. $f(0) < f(1)$

$$\mathbf{A} \quad \text{Only I}$$

$$\mathbf{B} \quad \text{Only II}$$

$$\mathbf{C} \quad \text{Only III}$$

$$\mathbf{D} \quad \text{I and II}$$

$$\mathbf{E} \quad \text{I and III}$$

$$\mathbf{F} \quad \text{II and III}$$

$$\mathbf{G} \quad \text{All of them}$$

## 答案
D

## 解析
The hypothesis $f'(x)<0$ on $[0,1]$ says only that $f$ is strictly decreasing there; nothing is assumed about its sign.

**I is true.** Substituting $x\mapsto x+\tfrac12$,

$$\int_{1/2}^{1}f(x)\,dx=\int_0^{1/2}f\!\left(x+\tfrac12\right)dx .$$

For $0\le x<\tfrac12$ we have $x<x+\tfrac12$, so $f(x)>f\!\left(x+\tfrac12\right)$ since $f$ is decreasing. Integrating this strict inequality over an interval of positive length gives

$$\int_0^{1/2}f(x)\,dx>\int_{1/2}^{1}f(x)\,dx .$$

**II is true.** For $0<x<\tfrac12$ we have $0<x^2<x$, so again $f(x^2)>f(x)$. Integrating over $\left[0,\tfrac12\right]$ gives

$$\int_0^{1/2}f(x)\,dx<\int_0^{1/2}f(x^2)\,dx .$$

**III is false.** A decreasing function satisfies $f(0)>f(1)$, the opposite inequality; for example $f(x)=-x$ has $f(0)=0>-1=f(1)$.

So I and II only. The answer is D.
