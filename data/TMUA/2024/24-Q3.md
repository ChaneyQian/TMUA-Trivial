## 题目
Given real numbers $p, q, r$ such that $p + q + r = 0$ and $0 < r < p$, find the number of solutions of the equation $p\sin^{2}\theta + q\sin\theta + r = 0$ within the interval $0 < \theta < \pi$.

$$\mathbf{A} \quad 0$$

$$\mathbf{B} \quad 1$$

$$\mathbf{C} \quad 2$$

$$\mathbf{D} \quad 3$$

$$\mathbf{E} \quad 4$$

$$\mathbf{F} \quad 5$$

## 答案
D

## 解析
Treat the equation as a quadratic in $s=\sin\theta$. For $0<\theta<\pi$ we have $0<s\le 1$, and each value $s\in(0,1)$ is attained by exactly two values of $\theta$ in this interval (one in $(0,\tfrac\pi2)$ and one in $(\tfrac\pi2,\pi)$), while $s=1$ is attained only at $\theta=\tfrac\pi2$.

Since $p+q+r=0$, substituting $s=1$ into $ps^2+qs+r$ gives $p+q+r=0$, so $s=1$ is a root. Note $p>r>0$, so $p\neq0$ and the quadratic is genuine; the product of its roots is $r/p$, hence the other root is

$$s=\frac{r}{p}.$$

Because $0<r<p$, this second root satisfies $0<\dfrac rp<1$, so it is a legitimate value of $\sin\theta$ and is different from $1$.

Counting solutions in $0<\theta<\pi$:

$$\begin{aligned}
\sin\theta=1 &\;\Rightarrow\; \theta=\tfrac{\pi}{2} &&\text{(1 solution)},\\
\sin\theta=\tfrac rp\in(0,1) &\;\Rightarrow\; \theta=\arcsin\tfrac rp,\ \pi-\arcsin\tfrac rp &&\text{(2 solutions)}.
\end{aligned}$$

Altogether there are $3$ solutions. (Answering $2$ comes from counting roots in $s$ rather than values of $\theta$.) The answer is D.
