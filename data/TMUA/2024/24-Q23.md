## 题目
For a cubic function $ax^{3} + bx^{2} + cx + d$, there are two local extrema, one at $x > 0$ and the other at $x < 0$. What is the necessary and sufficient condition for this to occur?

$$\mathbf{A} \quad b > 3a$$

$$\mathbf{B} \quad b < 3a$$

$$\mathbf{C} \quad ac > 0$$

$$\mathbf{D} \quad ac < 0$$

$$\mathbf{E} \quad b^{2} > 3ac$$

$$\mathbf{F} \quad b^{2} > 4ac$$

## 备注

### 我的备注

### AI备注


## 答案
D

## 解析
For a cubic we have $a\neq0$, and

$$f'(x)=3ax^2+2bx+c.$$

The two stationary points lie one on each side of the origin exactly when this quadratic has two real roots of opposite sign. The product of the roots is $\dfrac{c}{3a}$, so the condition is

$$\frac{c}{3a}<0\iff ac<0$$

(multiplying by $3a^2>0$). Note that no separate discriminant condition is needed: if $ac<0$ then $(2b)^2-4(3a)c=4b^2-12ac>0$ automatically, so the roots are real and distinct; and conversely roots of opposite sign are certainly real and distinct. Hence $ac<0$ is both necessary and sufficient.

Option E, $b^2>3ac$, is exactly the discriminant condition, so it guarantees two stationary points but not that they straddle the origin: $f(x)=x^3-6x^2+9x$ has $b^2=36>27=3ac$ yet both stationary points, $x=1$ and $x=3$, are positive. It is necessary but not sufficient.

The answer is D.
