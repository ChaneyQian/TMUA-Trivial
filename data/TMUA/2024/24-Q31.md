## 题目
A square with a side length of 1 contains an inscribed equilateral triangle.

Find the area of the equilateral triangle.

$$\mathbf{A} \quad \frac{2\sqrt{3} - 3}{4}$$

$$\mathbf{B} \quad \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}$$

$$\mathbf{C} \quad \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$$

$$\mathbf{D} \quad 2\sqrt{3} - 3$$

$$\mathbf{E} \quad \frac{3 - \sqrt{3}}{4}$$

$$\mathbf{F} \quad 1 - \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}$$

## 解析
"Inscribed" here means the largest equilateral triangle that fits in the square; by symmetry it has one vertex at a corner of the square and the other two on the two sides not through that corner, placed symmetrically about the diagonal.

Take the square with corners $(0,0)$, $(1,0)$, $(1,1)$, $(0,1)$ and put one vertex at $A=(0,0)$, the others at $B=(1,t)$ and $C=(t,1)$. Reflection in the line $y=x$ swaps $B$ and $C$ and fixes $A$, so $AB=AC$ automatically; we only need $AB=BC$:

$$AB^2=1+t^2,\qquad BC^2=(1-t)^2+(t-1)^2=2(1-t)^2.$$

Hence $1+t^2=2-4t+2t^2$, i.e. $t^2-4t+1=0$, giving $t=2\pm\sqrt3$. Only $t=2-\sqrt3\approx0.27$ lies in $[0,1]$. Then

$$AB^2=1+(2-\sqrt3)^2=1+(7-4\sqrt3)=8-4\sqrt3,$$

so the area is

$$\frac{\sqrt3}{4}AB^2=\frac{\sqrt3}{4}(8-4\sqrt3)=2\sqrt3-3\approx0.464.$$

This is comfortably less than the area $1$ of the square, as it must be, and larger than $\frac{\sqrt3}{4}\approx0.433$, the area of the unit-side equilateral triangle of option B, which is the triangle one gets by placing a side along a side of the square and which does not fit the square as tightly.

The answer is D.
