## 题目
If the statement P: "For any n such that $n \not\equiv 1 \pmod 6$, n is not prime" is false, then which of the following is true?

$$\mathbf{A} \quad \text{There exist a } n \not\equiv 1 \pmod 6 \text{ and } n \text{ is prime.}$$

$$\mathbf{B} \quad \text{There exist a } n \equiv 1 \pmod 6 \text{ and } n \text{ is prime.}$$

$$\mathbf{C} \quad \text{For any prime } n,\ n \equiv 1 \pmod 6$$

$$\mathbf{D} \quad \text{For any prime } n,\ n \not\equiv 1 \pmod 6$$

### 我的备注

### AI备注


## 答案
A

## 解析
Statement P has the form "for all $n$, if $n\not\equiv1\pmod 6$ then $n$ is not prime". Saying that P is false is exactly saying that its negation holds, and the negation of a universal implication is an existential conjunction:

$$\neg\Big(\forall n\,\big(n\not\equiv1\!\!\pmod 6\Rightarrow n\text{ is not prime}\big)\Big)
\equiv\exists n\,\big(n\not\equiv1\!\!\pmod 6\ \text{and}\ n\text{ is prime}\big).$$

That is option A — and indeed P really is false, since $n=2$ (or $3$, $5$, $11$, $\dots$) is prime and not congruent to $1$ modulo $6$.

The other options are not the negation. C ("every prime is $\equiv1$ mod $6$") is false, as $2$ shows; D ("no prime is $\equiv1$ mod $6$") is false, as $7$ shows. B concerns primes that *are* $\equiv1\pmod 6$; it is a true fact about the integers ($7$ is such a prime) but it is not what the falsity of P asserts, and it would remain true even if P were true.

The answer is A.
