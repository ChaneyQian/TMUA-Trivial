## 题目
Define the left branch of a quadratic function as the portion to the left of its vertex, and similarly, define the right branch. Let $f(x)$ and $g(x)$ be two quadratic functions.

Statement P: If $g(x)$ is a translation of $f(x)$, then the left branch of $g(x)$ and the left branch of $f(x)$ intersect at one point, and the right branch of $g(x)$ and the right branch of $f(x)$ intersect at one point.

Which of the following is correct?

$$\mathbf{A} \quad \text{Statement P is correct}$$

$$\mathbf{B} \quad \text{The converse of Statement P is correct}$$

$$\mathbf{C} \quad \text{The contrapositive of Statement P is correct}$$

$$\mathbf{D} \quad \text{None of them are correct}$$

## 答案
D

## 解析
Write $f(x)=a(x-\alpha)^2+\beta$. A translation of $f$ has the *same* leading coefficient, so $g(x)=a(x-\alpha-h)^2+\beta+k$ for some $h$ and $k$. Subtracting,

$$f(x)-g(x)=a\left[(x-\alpha)^2-(x-\alpha-h)^2\right]-k=2ah(x-\alpha)-ah^2-k,$$

which is linear in $x$ when $h\neq0$ and constant when $h=0$. So two distinct translates of the same parabola meet in **at most one point** — never in two.

**Statement P is false.** If $h=0$ and $k\neq0$ (a purely vertical translation) the graphs never meet at all, so neither pair of branches meets. Even when $h\neq0$ there is exactly one common point, so it is impossible for the left branches to meet *and* the right branches to meet. (The remaining case $h=k=0$ gives $g=f$, where the branches overlap completely rather than meeting in one point.)

**The contrapositive** of a statement is logically equivalent to it, so since P is false its contrapositive is false too.

**The converse is false** as well: take $f(x)=x^2$ and $g(x)=-x^2+2$. Both have their vertex on the $y$-axis, and the graphs meet at $(-1,1)$ and $(1,1)$ — one point on the two left branches and one point on the two right branches — yet $g$ is not a translation of $f$, since the parabolas open in opposite directions.

None of the three statements is correct.

The answer is D.
