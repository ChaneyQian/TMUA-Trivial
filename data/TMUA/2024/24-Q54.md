## 题目
Circle $C_{1}$ has the equation $(x - 3\sqrt{2})^{2} + (y - 3\sqrt{2})^{2} = 18$. A new circle, $C_{2}$, is drawn so that it passes through the point $(0, 3\sqrt{2})$ and is internally tangent to $C_{1}$. The difference between the area of $C_{1}$ and $C_{2}$ is $32\pi$. Which of the following is an equation for $C_{2}$?

$$\mathbf{A} \quad (x + 5\sqrt{2})^{2} + (y - 3\sqrt{2})^{2} = 25$$

$$\mathbf{B} \quad (x + 5\sqrt{2})^{2} + y^{2} = 50$$

$$\mathbf{C} \quad (x + 4\sqrt{2})^{2} + (y - 3\sqrt{2})^{2} = 32$$

$$\mathbf{D} \quad x^{2} + (y - 3\sqrt{2})^{2} = 18$$

$$\mathbf{E} \quad (x - 5\sqrt{2})^{2} + (y - 3\sqrt{2})^{2} = 50$$

## 答案
E

## 解析
The circle $C_1$ has centre $A\left(3\sqrt2,3\sqrt2\right)$ and radius $r_1=\sqrt{18}=3\sqrt2$. Note first that the given point $P\left(0,3\sqrt2\right)$ lies **on** $C_1$, since $AP=3\sqrt2=r_1$.

Use the area condition to fix $r_2$. The area of $C_1$ is $18\pi$, so the difference of areas being $32\pi$ means

$$\left|\pi r_2^2-18\pi\right|=32\pi\quad\Longrightarrow\quad r_2^2=50\ \ (\text{as } r_2^2=-14 \text{ is impossible}),$$

so $r_2=5\sqrt2>r_1$: it is $C_1$ that sits inside $C_2$.

For internal tangency the distance between the centres is $\left|r_2-r_1\right|=5\sqrt2-3\sqrt2=2\sqrt2$, and the point of tangency is the unique common point, so it must be $P$ — it is the only point known to lie on both circles. Centres and point of tangency are collinear: $P$ lies on $C_1$ in the direction $(-1,0)$ from $A$, so the centre $B$ of $C_2$ lies on the line $y=3\sqrt2$ at distance $r_2$ from $P$, on the same side as $A$:

$$B=P+5\sqrt2\,(1,0)=\left(5\sqrt2,\,3\sqrt2\right).$$

Indeed $AB=\left|5\sqrt2-3\sqrt2\right|=2\sqrt2=r_2-r_1$, confirming internal tangency, so

$$C_2:\ \left(x-5\sqrt2\right)^2+\left(y-3\sqrt2\right)^2=50.$$

The distractors fail on one of the three requirements: A and B do not pass through $P$ (for B, the distance from $\left(-5\sqrt2,0\right)$ to $P$ is $\sqrt{68}\neq5\sqrt2$); C passes through $P$ but has centre distance $7\sqrt2=r_1+r_2$, so it is *externally* tangent, and its area differs by only $14\pi$; D is $C_1$ reflected in the $y$-axis through $P$, with the same radius, so the areas are equal.

The answer is E.
