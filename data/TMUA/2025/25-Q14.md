## 题目
Given three sequences:

$$P_n = n^{2} - 3n - 2$$

$$Q_n = n^{2} + n - 4 \quad (n \in \mathbb{Z}^{+})$$

$$R_n = 4n^{2} - 2n - 4$$

$w$ is a term. Which of the following is true?

	I. $w \in P \implies w \in Q$
	II. $w \in Q \implies w \in R$
	III. $w \in R \implies w \in P$

$$\mathbf{A} \quad \text{none of them}$$

$$\mathbf{B} \quad \text{I only}$$

$$\mathbf{C} \quad \text{II only}$$

$$\mathbf{D} \quad \text{III only}$$

$$\mathbf{E} \quad \text{I and II only}$$

$$\mathbf{F} \quad \text{I and III only}$$

$$\mathbf{G} \quad \text{II and III only}$$

$$\mathbf{H} \quad \text{I, II and III}$$

## 答案
D

## 解析
**回忆答案:** III only（考场上算出来是 $R \subseteq Q \subseteq P$；具体细节数据与选项原稿已记不清）

![[Image/25-Q14-sol1.png]]
All three are quadratics in $n$, so the way to compare the sets of values they take is to look for an index substitution that turns one formula into another.

First compare $Q$ with $P$. Shifting the index of $P$ by $2$,

$$P_{n+2} = (n+2)^{2} - 3(n+2) - 2 = n^{2} + n - 4 = Q_n .$$

Since $n \in \mathbb{Z}^{+}$ gives $n + 2 \in \mathbb{Z}^{+}$, every term of $Q$ is a term of $P$, so $Q \subseteq P$.

Now compare $R$ with $Q$. Taking the odd-indexed terms of $Q$,

$$Q_{2n-1} = (2n-1)^{2} + (2n-1) - 4 = 4n^{2} - 2n - 4 = R_n ,$$

and $2n - 1 \in \mathbb{Z}^{+}$, so every term of $R$ is a term of $Q$. Altogether

$$R \subseteq Q \subseteq P .$$

**III** is therefore true: $w \in R \implies w \in Q \implies w \in P$.

**I** is false. Both $P_1$ and $P_2$ equal $-4$, while $Q_n = n^{2} + n - 4$ is increasing for $n \geq 1$ with least term $Q_1 = -2$. So $-4$ is a term of $P$ but not of $Q$.

**II** is false. $Q_2 = 2$, but $R_n = 4n^{2} - 2n - 4$ is increasing for $n \geq 1$ with $R_1 = -2$ and $R_2 = 8$, so $2$ is not a term of $R$.

(The first few terms make the inclusions visible: $P$ gives $-4, -4, -2, 2, 8, 16, 26, 38, 52, \ldots$, $Q$ gives $-2, 2, 8, 16, 26, 38, 52, \ldots$, and $R$ gives $-2, 8, 26, 52, \ldots$ — but note that checking a few terms only ever disproves an inclusion; the substitutions above are what prove $R \subseteq Q \subseteq P$.)

The answer is D.
