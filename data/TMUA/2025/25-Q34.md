## 题目
$f(x) = x^{2} - px + 1$. As $p$ varies, what is the locus of the stationary point of $f(x)$?

$$\mathbf{A} \quad y = 1 - 4x^{2}$$

$$\mathbf{B} \quad y = 1 - 2x^{2}$$

$$\mathbf{C} \quad y = 1 - x^{2}$$

$$\mathbf{D} \quad y = 1 - \dfrac{x^{2}}{4}$$

$$\mathbf{E} \quad y = 1 + x^{2}$$

$$\mathbf{F} \quad y = x^{2} - 1$$

## 答案
C

## 解析
**回忆答案:** $y = 1 - x^{2}$

A locus question of this type is solved by writing the coordinates of the moving point in terms of the parameter and then eliminating the parameter.

Differentiating, $f'(x) = 2x - p$, so the stationary point occurs at $x = \dfrac{p}{2}$, and there

$$y = \left(\frac{p}{2}\right)^{2} - p\left(\frac{p}{2}\right) + 1 = \frac{p^{2}}{4} - \frac{p^{2}}{2} + 1 = 1 - \frac{p^{2}}{4}.$$

(The same thing comes out of completing the square: $f(x) = \left(x - \tfrac{p}{2}\right)^{2} + 1 - \tfrac{p^{2}}{4}$, so the vertex is $\left(\tfrac{p}{2},\, 1 - \tfrac{p^{2}}{4}\right)$.)

Now eliminate $p$. The stationary point has $x = \dfrac{p}{2}$, so $p = 2x$, and substituting into the $y$-coordinate gives

$$y = 1 - \frac{(2x)^{2}}{4} = 1 - x^{2}.$$

As $p$ runs over all real values, $x = \tfrac{p}{2}$ runs over all real values too, so the locus is the whole parabola $y = 1 - x^{2}$. A sanity check: $p = 0$ gives the vertex $(0, 1)$, and $p = 2$ gives $f(x) = x^2 - 2x + 1$ with vertex $(1, 0)$; both lie on $y = 1 - x^{2}$.

The common slip is to substitute $p = x$ rather than $p = 2x$, which gives $y = 1 - \tfrac{x^{2}}{4}$, or to keep the $4$ in the wrong place and get $y = 1 - 4x^{2}$.

The answer is C.
