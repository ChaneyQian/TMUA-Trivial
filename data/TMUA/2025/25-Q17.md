## 题目
$x$ is a constant number. $a, b \in \mathbb{Z}^{+}$, $a + b = 100$.

$$f(x) = \frac{1}{x+a} + \frac{1}{x+b}, \quad x > 0$$

For what values of $a$ does $f(x)$ reach maximum?

$$\mathbf{A} \quad a = 1 \text{ only}$$

$$\mathbf{B} \quad a = 50 \text{ only}$$

$$\mathbf{C} \quad a = 1 \text{ or } a = 99$$

$$\mathbf{D} \quad a = 49 \text{ or } a = 51$$

$$\mathbf{E} \quad a = 0 \text{ or } a = 100$$

$$\mathbf{F} \quad \text{Every value of } a \text{ gives the same value of } f(x)$$

## 答案
C

## 解析
**回忆答案:** $a = 1$ or $a = 99$

Here $x$ is fixed and it is $a$ that varies, so combine the two fractions and use $a + b = 100$ to see what actually depends on $a$:

$$f(x) = \frac{1}{x+a} + \frac{1}{x+b} = \frac{(x+b) + (x+a)}{(x+a)(x+b)} = \frac{2x + 100}{x^{2} + 100x + ab} .$$

For $x > 0$ the numerator $2x + 100$ and the term $x^{2} + 100x$ are both positive and independent of $a$, and the denominator is positive. So $f(x)$ is largest exactly when the denominator is smallest, i.e. when the product $ab$ is as small as possible.

With $a + b = 100$ we have $ab = a(100 - a)$, a downward parabola in $a$ with maximum at $a = 50$ and decreasing towards the ends of the range. Since $a, b \in \mathbb{Z}^{+}$, the admissible values are $a = 1, 2, \ldots, 99$, and the least product is

$$ab = 1 \times 99 = 99 \quad \text{at } a = 1 \text{ or } a = 99 .$$

(The symmetry $a \leftrightarrow b$ in $f$ is why the two extreme values both occur; $a = 0$ or $a = 100$ would give $ab = 0$ but is excluded by $a, b \in \mathbb{Z}^{+}$.) Note also that $a = b = 50$ gives the *largest* product $ab = 2500$ and hence the *minimum* of $f(x)$, which is the trap for anyone who reaches for AM–GM out of habit.

The answer is C.
