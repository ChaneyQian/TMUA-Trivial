## 题目
Geometric sequence $\{a_n\}$ has sum to infinity $S_\infty = 1$. All the terms are positive. What is the maximum value of the $3^{\text{rd}}$ term, $a_3$?

$$\mathbf{A} \quad \frac{1}{8}$$

$$\mathbf{B} \quad \frac{4}{27}$$

$$\mathbf{C} \quad \frac{1}{4}$$

$$\mathbf{D} \quad \frac{1}{3}$$

$$\mathbf{E} \quad \frac{4}{9}$$

$$\mathbf{F} \quad \frac{2}{3}$$

$$\mathbf{G} \quad 1$$

## 答案
B

## 解析
**回忆答案:** $\dfrac{4}{27}$

Let the first term be $a$ and the common ratio be $r$. For the sum to infinity to exist we need $|r| < 1$, and since every term is positive we need $a > 0$ and $0 < r < 1$. The sum to infinity condition gives

$$\frac{a}{1-r} = 1 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad a = 1 - r.$$

Hence the third term is a function of $r$ alone:

$$a_3 = ar^{2} = (1-r)r^{2} = r^{2} - r^{3}, \qquad 0 < r < 1.$$

Differentiating,

$$\frac{\mathrm{d}a_3}{\mathrm{d}r} = 2r - 3r^{2} = r(2 - 3r),$$

which vanishes on $(0,1)$ only at $r = \frac{2}{3}$. The derivative is positive for $r < \frac{2}{3}$ and negative for $r > \frac{2}{3}$, so this is a maximum. Its value is

$$a_3 = \left(1 - \frac{2}{3}\right)\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{2} = \frac{1}{3} \cdot \frac{4}{9} = \frac{4}{27}.$$

(The endpoints give $a_3 \to 0$ as $r \to 0^{+}$ or $r \to 1^{-}$, confirming that the interior stationary point is the maximum.)

Note $\frac{2}{3}$ is the maximising value of $r$, not the maximum of $a_3$, and $\frac{1}{3}$ is the corresponding first term.

The answer is B.
