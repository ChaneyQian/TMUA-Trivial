## 题目
Quadratic function $16x^{2} + kx + 9$ can be factorised into $(ax + b)(cx + d)$, where $a, c > 0$ and $a, b, c, d \in \mathbb{Z}$. How many distinct values can $k$ take?

$$\mathbf{A} \quad 4$$

$$\mathbf{B} \quad 8$$

$$\mathbf{C} \quad 16$$

$$\mathbf{D} \quad 18$$

$$\mathbf{E} \quad 30$$

$$\mathbf{F} \quad \text{Infinitely many}$$

## 答案
C

## 解析
**回忆答案:** $16$

Expanding and comparing coefficients in $16x^{2} + kx + 9 = (ax+b)(cx+d)$ gives

$$ac = 16, \qquad bd = 9, \qquad k = ad + bc .$$

Since $a, c$ are positive integers with $ac = 16$, the ordered pairs $(a,c)$ are $(1,16), (2,8), (4,4), (8,2), (16,1)$. Since $bd = 9 > 0$, the integers $b, d$ have the same sign, so $(b,d)$ is one of $(1,9), (3,3), (9,1)$ or their negatives $(-1,-9), (-3,-3), (-9,-1)$.

Two symmetries cut the work down. Swapping the two brackets, $(a,b,c,d) \mapsto (c,d,a,b)$, leaves $ad + bc$ unchanged; and replacing $(b,d)$ by $(-b,-d)$ replaces $k$ by $-k$. So it is enough to list $k = ad + bc$ for the $5 \times 3 = 15$ combinations with $b, d > 0$ and then double the count by sign:

$$\begin{aligned}
(a,c) = (1,16) &: \ 25, \ 51, \ 145 \\
(a,c) = (2,8) &: \ 26, \ 30, \ 74 \\
(a,c) = (4,4) &: \ 40, \ 24, \ 40 \\
(a,c) = (8,2) &: \ 74, \ 30, \ 26 \\
(a,c) = (16,1) &: \ 145, \ 51, \ 25
\end{aligned}$$

(each row taking $(b,d) = (1,9), (3,3), (9,1)$ in turn). The distinct positive values are

$$\{24, \ 25, \ 26, \ 30, \ 40, \ 51, \ 74, \ 145\},$$

eight of them. Adding the eight negatives obtained from $b, d < 0$, and noting $k = 0$ is impossible (it would need $ad = -bc$ with all of $ad$, $bc$ of the same sign), there are

$$8 \times 2 = 16$$

possible values of $k$. (Counting the $5 \times 6 = 30$ ordered combinations without removing duplicates gives $30$; forgetting that $b$ and $d$ may both be negative gives $8$.)

The answer is C.
