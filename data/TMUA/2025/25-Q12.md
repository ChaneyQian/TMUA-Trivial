## 题目
$N \in \mathbb{Z}^{+}$, $\dfrac{1}{N}$ is recurring. Which of the following is/are correct?

	I. $\dfrac{1}{N} + \dfrac{1}{2}$ must be recurring.
	II. $\dfrac{1}{N} + \dfrac{1}{3}$ must be recurring.
	III. $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{N}}$ can't be recurring.

$$\mathbf{A} \quad \text{none of them}$$

$$\mathbf{B} \quad \text{I only}$$

$$\mathbf{C} \quad \text{II only}$$

$$\mathbf{D} \quad \text{III only}$$

$$\mathbf{E} \quad \text{I and II only}$$

$$\mathbf{F} \quad \text{I and III only}$$

$$\mathbf{G} \quad \text{II and III only}$$

$$\mathbf{H} \quad \text{I, II and III}$$

## 答案
B

## 解析
**回忆答案:** I only

A fraction in lowest terms has a terminating decimal exactly when its denominator has no prime factors other than $2$ and $5$; otherwise it recurs. So "$\frac{1}{N}$ is recurring" means $N$ has some prime factor $p \notin \{2, 5\}$.

**I.** $\dfrac{1}{N} + \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{N+2}{2N}$. Take the prime $p \notin \{2,5\}$ with $p \mid N$. Then $p \mid 2N$, while $p \nmid N + 2$ (since $p \mid N$ would force $p \mid 2$). So $p$ survives into the denominator after cancelling, and the fraction still recurs. **True.**

**II.** The same argument breaks down for $p = 3$, because then $p$ can divide $N + 3$. Concretely, take $N = 6$: $\frac{1}{6} = 0.1\dot{6}$ is recurring, but

$$\frac{1}{6} + \frac{1}{3} = \frac{1}{2} = 0.5,$$

which terminates. **False.**

**III.** Take $N = 9$: $\frac{1}{9} = 0.\dot{1}$ is recurring, and

$$\frac{1}{\sqrt{9}} = \frac{1}{3} = 0.\dot{3},$$

which is recurring too. **False.** (The statement would be plausible if $N$ were never a perfect square, but nothing in the question prevents that.)

So only statement I holds.

The answer is B.
