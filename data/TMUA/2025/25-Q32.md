## 题目
$f(x)$ is a 4th-degree polynomial with respect to $10^{x}$. How many distinct real solutions does $f(x) = 0$ have?

$$\mathbf{A} \quad 0$$

$$\mathbf{B} \quad 1$$

$$\mathbf{C} \quad 2$$

$$\mathbf{D} \quad 3$$

$$\mathbf{E} \quad 4$$

## 答案
C

## 解析
**回忆答案:** $2$

原稿注：四次函数可因式分解，求出 $a$ 有 4 个解，两正两负；但 $10^{x} > 0$，所以最终只有 2 个解。

The point of the question is the substitution together with the range of the exponential. Put

$$a = 10^{x},$$

so that $f(x) = 0$ becomes a quartic equation in $a$. The recalled quartic factorises completely and has four real roots, two positive and two negative.

Now translate back. The map $x \mapsto 10^{x}$ is a bijection from $\mathbb{R}$ onto $(0, \infty)$: it is strictly increasing, so each admissible value of $a$ comes from **at most one** $x$, and it is never zero or negative, so

- each of the two positive roots $a$ contributes exactly one solution $x = \log_{10} a$;
- each of the two negative roots $a$ contributes none, since $10^{x} > 0$ for every real $x$.

Hence $f(x) = 0$ has $2 + 0 = 2$ distinct real solutions. Note that the positive roots need not be powers of $10$ — any positive number is $10^{x}$ for a (generally irrational) $x$ — so both of them really do count.

The trap is to solve the quartic in $a$, find four roots, and answer $4$; the other way to go wrong is to notice $10^{x} > 0$ and discard too much.

The answer is C.
