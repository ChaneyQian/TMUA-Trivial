## 题目
$y = x^{2} - 2x$ is rotated about $(0,0)$ through $180^\circ$, and then transformed by $\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$. What is the final function?

$$\mathbf{A} \quad y = x^{2} - 2x$$

$$\mathbf{B} \quad y = x^{2} - 6x + 8$$

$$\mathbf{C} \quad y = -x^{2} - 2x$$

$$\mathbf{D} \quad y = -x^{2} + 2x$$

$$\mathbf{E} \quad y = -x^{2} + 6x - 8$$

$$\mathbf{F} \quad y = -x^{2} - 6x - 8$$

## 答案
D

## 解析
**回忆答案:** $y = -x^{2} + 2x$

Handle the two transformations one at a time, each time asking what equation the image points satisfy.

A rotation of $180^\circ$ about the origin sends $(x, y)$ to $(-x, -y)$. So a point $(X, Y)$ lies on the image exactly when $(-X, -Y)$ lies on $y = x^{2} - 2x$, i.e. when

$$-Y = (-X)^{2} - 2(-X) = X^{2} + 2X,$$

which gives the intermediate curve

$$y = -x^{2} - 2x.$$

The translation by $\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$ moves every point $2$ to the right, so we replace $x$ by $x - 2$:

$$y = -(x-2)^{2} - 2(x-2) = -\left(x^{2} - 4x + 4\right) - 2x + 4 = -x^{2} + 2x.$$

A quick check with the roots confirms this. The original parabola has roots $0$ and $2$ and a minimum at $(1, -1)$. Rotating about the origin sends these to roots $0$ and $-2$ with a maximum at $(-1, 1)$; translating by $2$ gives roots $2$ and $0$ with a maximum at $(1, 1)$, and $y = -x^{2} + 2x = -x(x-2)$ has exactly those features.

Note the coincidence that makes the distractors tempting: for this particular curve the net effect is the same as a reflection in the $x$-axis. Omitting the translation leaves $y = -x^{2} - 2x$, and omitting the rotation leaves $y = x^{2} - 6x + 8$; translating the wrong way (or translating before rotating) gives $y = -x^{2} - 6x - 8$.

The answer is D.
