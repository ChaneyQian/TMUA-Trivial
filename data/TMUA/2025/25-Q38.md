## 题目
$p(x)$ is a cubic polynomial. It is divisible by $(x-1)$ and $(x+2)$. When it is divided by $(x+1)$ the remainder is $2$.

	I. $p(x) = 0$ has 2 roots
	II. $p(0) < 0$
	III. $x \cdot p(x)$ passes $(-1, 2)$

$$\mathbf{A} \quad \text{none of them}$$

$$\mathbf{B} \quad \text{I only}$$

$$\mathbf{C} \quad \text{II only}$$

$$\mathbf{D} \quad \text{III only}$$

$$\mathbf{E} \quad \text{I and II only}$$

$$\mathbf{F} \quad \text{I and III only}$$

$$\mathbf{G} \quad \text{II and III only}$$

$$\mathbf{H} \quad \text{I, II and III}$$

## 解析
**回忆答案:** II only

（`TAGS: 待补答案`：下面的推导与回忆答案不符，结论未定，详见末段。）

Since $p$ is a cubic divisible by $(x-1)$ and by $(x+2)$, the remaining factor is linear:

$$p(x) = (x-1)(x+2)(ax+b), \qquad a \neq 0.$$

The remainder theorem turns the last condition into $p(-1) = 2$, and

$$p(-1) = (-2)(1)(-a+b) = -2(b-a) = 2 \implies b = a - 1.$$

So the data determine a one-parameter family

$$p(x) = (x-1)(x+2)\big(ax + a - 1\big), \qquad a \neq 0,$$

and a statement is "correct" only if it holds for **every** member of this family.

**I is not forced.** The third root is $x = \dfrac{1-a}{a}$, which coincides with $1$ only when $a = \tfrac12$ and with $-2$ only when $a = -1$. For any other $a$ there are three distinct roots: for instance $a = 1$ gives $p(x) = x(x-1)(x+2)$, with roots $-2, 0, 1$. So "$p(x) = 0$ has $2$ roots" fails for $a = 1$. (Read instead as "at least two distinct roots", I would be true for every $a$, since $1$ and $-2$ are always roots.)

**II is not forced.** Here

$$p(0) = (-1)(2)(b) = -2b = -2(a-1) = 2 - 2a,$$

whose sign depends on $a$. With $a = 2$ we get $p(0) = -2 < 0$, but with $a = \tfrac12$ we get $p(x) = \tfrac12(x-1)^{2}(x+2)$ and $p(0) = 1 > 0$, and with $a = 1$ we get $p(0) = 0$. So $p(0) < 0$ need not hold.

**III is false for every member.** At $x = -1$,

$$x\,p(x)\big|_{x=-1} = (-1) \cdot p(-1) = (-1)(2) = -2,$$

so $y = x\,p(x)$ passes through $(-1, -2)$, not $(-1, 2)$.

Hence, on the stem exactly as recalled, none of I, II, III must be true, which points to option A — and under the alternative reading of I ("at least two roots") it would point to B. Neither matches the recalled answer "II only", and nothing in the family makes $p(0) < 0$ compulsory, so the recalled stem is very probably missing a condition (something that would pin down $a$, or restrict its sign). Worth noting: if the remainder on division by $(x+1)$ were $-2$ instead of $2$, then $b = a+1$ and III *would* become true for every $a$ (since $(-1)\cdot(-2) = 2$), giving "III only"; II is still not forced in that version either, because $p(0) = -2(a+1)$ changes sign with $a$.

So, on the stem as recalled, the answer would be A (or B under the alternative reading of I). This conflicts with the recalled "II only", so no answer is recorded until the missing condition is recovered.
