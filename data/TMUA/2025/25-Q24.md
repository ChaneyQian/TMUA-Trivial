## 题目
A student wants to prove $x + y = 180^\circ$. His proof is as follows:

![[Image/25-Q24-fig1.png]]
	I. If $O$ is on $BD$, then $BD$ is the diameter of $\odot O$.
	II. Thus $x = y = 90^\circ$, $x + y = 180^\circ$.
	III. If $O$ is inside $\triangle ABD$, then $\angle BOD = 2y$.
	IV. As $C$ bisects the arc $BD$, ...

Which step does the first error occur in?

$$\mathbf{A} \quad \text{The proof is correct; there is no error.}$$

$$\mathbf{B} \quad \text{Step I}$$

$$\mathbf{C} \quad \text{Step II}$$

$$\mathbf{D} \quad \text{Step III}$$

$$\mathbf{E} \quad \text{Step IV}$$

## 答案
E

## 解析
**回忆答案:** Incorrect. The first error occurs in step IV.

In the diagram $ABCD$ is a cyclic quadrilateral with centre $O$, and $y = \angle BAD$, $x = \angle BCD$ are a pair of opposite angles; the claim $x + y = 180^\circ$ is the cyclic quadrilateral theorem. The student splits into cases according to where $O$ lies relative to $BD$, so each step has to be checked on its own terms.

**Step I is correct.** $O$ lies on the chord $BD$, and a chord through the centre is by definition a diameter, so $BD$ is a diameter.

**Step II is correct.** With $BD$ a diameter, $\angle BAD$ and $\angle BCD$ are both angles in a semicircle, so $x = y = 90^\circ$ and $x + y = 180^\circ$. This settles that case completely.

**Step III is correct.** If $O$ lies inside $\triangle ABD$ then $O$ and $A$ are on the same side of the chord $BD$, so the arc $BD$ not containing $A$ is the minor arc and the angle subtended by it at the centre is the non-reflex angle $\angle BOD$. The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference standing on the same arc, so $\angle BOD = 2\angle BAD = 2y$.

**Step IV is where the proof first breaks down.** "As $C$ bisects the arc $BD$" is simply not given. The only information about $C$ is that it is a point of the circle on the opposite side of $BD$ from $A$, making $ABCD$ a quadrilateral; its position on that arc is arbitrary, and in the figure it is plainly not the midpoint. The student has imported an extra hypothesis that would make the result true only for a special quadrilateral — a classic unstated assumption, and it invalidates the general argument from this point on.

The answer is E.
