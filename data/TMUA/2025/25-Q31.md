$f(x)$ is a 4th-degree polynomial. What can be the graph of the derivative of $f(x)$, $f'(x)$?

## 解析
**回忆答案:** $f'(x)$ 与 $x$ 轴在 $(-2, 0)$ 相切，且过 $(0, 0)$；凭这两点即可选出。

![[Image/25-Q31-sol1.png]]
（本题原卷的候选图像未被回忆下来，`TAGS: 待补答案`；以下说明判别所用的全部依据。）

If $f$ has degree $4$, then $f'$ has degree exactly $3$, so the graph being sought must be that of a cubic: it has either one or three real zeros counted with multiplicity turning points, it tends to $+\infty$ at one end and $-\infty$ at the other, and it has exactly one point of inflection. Any graph that is a parabola, a quartic-shaped curve with two "ends" pointing the same way, or a curve with more than two turning points can be eliminated at once. Conversely every cubic is the derivative of some quartic, so no further restriction comes from $f$ alone; the choice is settled by reading the zeros off the picture.

The recalled graph meets the $x$-axis twice: it is tangent to the axis at $(-2, 0)$ and crosses it at the origin. A tangency is a repeated zero and a crossing is a simple zero, so

$$f'(x) = kx(x+2)^{2}$$

for some constant $k \neq 0$. In the recalled picture the curve comes down from the upper left and falls away to the lower right, so $f'(x) \to +\infty$ as $x \to -\infty$; since $x(x+2)^{2} \to -\infty$ there, this forces $k < 0$.

This is consistent with a genuine quartic. Integrating,

$$f(x) = k\left(\frac{x^{4}}{4} + \frac{4x^{3}}{3} + 2x^{2}\right) + c,$$

which has degree $4$ with leading coefficient $\tfrac{k}{4} < 0$. Reading the sign of $f'$ back, $f' > 0$ for $x < 0$ (apart from $x = -2$) and $f' < 0$ for $x > 0$, so $f$ has a maximum at $x = 0$ and a stationary point of inflection at $x = -2$ — exactly the behaviour a repeated zero of the derivative produces.

So the answer is the graph of a cubic with a double zero at $x = -2$ and a simple zero at $x = 0$, descending to the right, i.e. $f'(x) = kx(x+2)^{2}$ with $k < 0$.
