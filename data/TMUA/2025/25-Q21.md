## 题目
There are red or blue, square or circular tokens. Exactly $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of the red tokens are square. Exactly half of the square tokens are red. Which of the following is correct?

	I. Blue tokens more than circular.
	II. Square tokens more than circular.
	III. Blue circular tokens more than red circular.

$$\mathbf{A} \quad \text{none of them}$$

$$\mathbf{B} \quad \text{I only}$$

$$\mathbf{C} \quad \text{II only}$$

$$\mathbf{D} \quad \text{III only}$$

$$\mathbf{E} \quad \text{I and II only}$$

$$\mathbf{F} \quad \text{I and III only}$$

$$\mathbf{G} \quad \text{II and III only}$$

$$\mathbf{H} \quad \text{I, II and III}$$

## 答案
B

## 解析
**回忆答案:** I only

Set up a two-way table. Let $T$ be the total number of tokens, $R$ the number of red ones and $S$ the number of square ones. The two conditions say that the number of red squares is both $\tfrac{2}{3}R$ and $\tfrac{1}{2}S$, so

$$\frac{2}{3}R = \frac{1}{2}S \implies S = \frac{4}{3}R .$$

The four cells are then: red squares $\tfrac{2}{3}R$, red circles $\tfrac{1}{3}R$, blue squares $S - \tfrac{2}{3}R = \tfrac{2}{3}R$, blue circles $T - \tfrac{5}{3}R$. Note that $T$ is *not* determined by the data — only the relation between $R$ and $S$ is — so any statement whose truth depends on $T$ cannot be guaranteed.

**I is true.** The number of blue tokens is $T - R$ and the number of circular tokens is $T - S = T - \tfrac{4}{3}R$, so

$$(\text{blue}) - (\text{circular}) = (T - R) - \left(T - \frac{4}{3}R\right) = \frac{1}{3}R > 0 .$$

The unknown total cancels, so blue tokens always outnumber circular ones.

**II is false.** Here $S - (\text{circular}) = \tfrac{4}{3}R - \left(T - \tfrac{4}{3}R\right) = \tfrac{8}{3}R - T$, which depends on $T$. Take $R = 3$ (so $2$ red squares, $1$ red circle), $S = 4$ (so $2$ blue squares) and $3$ blue circles: then $T = 8$ with $4$ squares and $4$ circles, so squares do **not** outnumber circles. Adding yet more blue circles makes circles strictly the majority.

**III is false.** Nothing forces any blue circles to exist. Take $R = 3$ ($2$ red squares, $1$ red circle), $S = 4$ ($2$ blue squares) and $0$ blue circles. All conditions hold ($\tfrac{2}{3}$ of the $3$ red tokens are square, and $2$ of the $4$ squares are red), yet blue circles $= 0 <  1 =$ red circles.

The answer is B.
