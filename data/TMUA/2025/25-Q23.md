## 题目
$f(x) = ax^{4} - bx^{3} + cx^{2} + d$. $f(x) = 0$ has 2 distinct real roots. Which of the following is true?

	I. $f(-x) = 2d$ has at least 2 roots.
	II. $f(x + 2d) = 0$ has exactly 2 roots.
	III. $f(x) - 2d = 0$ has at most 2 roots.

$$\mathbf{A} \quad \text{none of them}$$

$$\mathbf{B} \quad \text{I only}$$

$$\mathbf{C} \quad \text{II only}$$

$$\mathbf{D} \quad \text{III only}$$

$$\mathbf{E} \quad \text{I and II only}$$

$$\mathbf{F} \quad \text{I and III only}$$

$$\mathbf{G} \quad \text{II and III only}$$

$$\mathbf{H} \quad \text{I, II and III}$$

## 答案
C

## 解析
**回忆答案:** II only

Throughout, "roots" means distinct real roots, as in the stem. Two general facts do most of the work: replacing $x$ by $-x$ or by $x + k$ is a bijection of $\mathbb{R}$, so it cannot change how many distinct real roots an equation has; but changing the *right-hand side* from $0$ to $2d$ moves the horizontal line and can change the number of intersections completely.

**II is true.** The graph of $y = f(x + 2d)$ is the graph of $y = f(x)$ translated horizontally by $-2d$, and $x \mapsto x + 2d$ is a bijection of $\mathbb{R}$. So the solutions of $f(x + 2d) = 0$ are exactly the solutions of $f(u) = 0$ shifted by $-2d$: there are exactly $2$ of them.

**I is false.** Since $x \mapsto -x$ is a bijection, $f(-x) = 2d$ has the same number of distinct real solutions as $f(x) = 2d$, and that equation can have none. Take $a = 1$, $b = c = 0$, $d = -1$, i.e.

$$f(x) = x^{4} - 1 .$$

Then $f(x) = 0$ has exactly the two distinct real roots $x = \pm 1$, as required. But $2d = -2$, and $x^{4} - 1 = -2$ gives $x^{4} = -1$, which has no real solutions at all. So I fails, let alone "at least $2$".

**III is false.** The horizontal line $y = 2d$ can cut a quartic four times. Take $a = 1$, $b = 0$, $c = -5$, $d = -1$, i.e.

$$f(x) = x^{4} - 5x^{2} - 1 .$$

Putting $u = x^{2}$ in $f(x) = 0$ gives $u^{2} - 5u - 1 = 0$, so $u = \dfrac{5 \pm \sqrt{29}}{2}$; only the root $u \approx 5.19$ is positive, giving exactly $2$ distinct real roots $x \approx \pm 2.28$, as required. Now $2d = -2$, and $f(x) = -2$ becomes

$$x^{4} - 5x^{2} + 1 = 0 \implies u = \frac{5 \pm \sqrt{21}}{2} \approx 4.79 \ \text{or} \ 0.21 ,$$

both positive, so there are $4$ distinct real roots $x \approx \pm 2.19, \pm 0.46$. "At most $2$" is therefore not guaranteed.

The answer is C.
