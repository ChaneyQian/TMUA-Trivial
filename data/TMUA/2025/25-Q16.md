## 题目
Quadrilateral $ABCD$, $A, B, C, D$ are in order. $AB \parallel CD$.

Which of the following is necessary but not sufficient for $ABCD$ to be a parallelogram?

![[Image/25-Q16-fig1.png]]
	I. $AC = BD$
	II. $AB = CD$
	III. $AD = BC$

$$\mathbf{A} \quad \text{none of them}$$

$$\mathbf{B} \quad \text{I only}$$

$$\mathbf{C} \quad \text{II only}$$

$$\mathbf{D} \quad \text{III only}$$

$$\mathbf{E} \quad \text{I and II only}$$

$$\mathbf{F} \quad \text{I and III only}$$

$$\mathbf{G} \quad \text{II and III only}$$

$$\mathbf{H} \quad \text{I, II and III}$$

## 答案
D

## 解析
**回忆答案:** III only

A condition is *necessary but not sufficient* only if it passes both tests: every parallelogram must satisfy it, and some non-parallelogram with $AB \parallel CD$ must satisfy it too. So each statement has to be checked in both directions.

**I is not even necessary.** In a parallelogram the diagonals bisect each other but need not be equal; $AC = BD$ holds precisely when the parallelogram is a rectangle. The slanted parallelogram in the figure already has $AC \neq BD$, so $AC = BD$ fails the necessity test and is out.

**II is necessary, but it is also sufficient.** Opposite sides of a parallelogram are equal, so $AB = CD$ is necessary. Conversely, if $AB \parallel CD$ *and* $AB = CD$, then one pair of opposite sides is both equal and parallel, which forces $ABCD$ to be a parallelogram. Being necessary *and* sufficient, II is not what the question asks for.

**III is necessary but not sufficient.** Opposite sides $AD$ and $BC$ of a parallelogram are equal, so $AD = BC$ is necessary. It is not sufficient: with $AB \parallel CD$ the condition $AD = BC$ is satisfied by every isosceles trapezium, for instance $A = (0,2)$, $B = (4,2)$, $C = (5,0)$, $D = (-1,0)$, where $AB \parallel CD$ and $AD = BC = \sqrt{5}$ but $AB = 4 \neq 6 = CD$. This is exactly the "necessary but not sufficient" situation.

The answer is D.
