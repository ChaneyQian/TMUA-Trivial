|x| + |y| = 1

## 解析
We first need to work out what the graph looks like. There are four cases for x and y depending on whether there are positive or negative. We list them in a table:

$$
\begin{array}{c c c c c c c} \text {quadrant} & x & y & | x | & | y | & \text {equation} & \text {rearranged to} y = \dots \\ \hline 1 \mathrm{st} & + & + & x & y & x + y = 1 & y = - x + 1 \\ 2 \mathrm{nd} & - & + & - x & y & - x + y = 1 & y = x + 1 \\ 3 \mathrm{rd} & - & - & - x & - y & - x - y = 1 & y = - x - 1 \\ 4 \mathrm{th} & + & - & x & - y & x - y = 1 & y = x - 1 \end{array}
$$

We therefore have (at most) four lines making up this graph, and we can sketch these. We need to recall that we only draw the part of each line that appears within the relevant quadrant; for example, $y = x + 1$ only appears in the 2nd quadrant.

![[Image/21-P1-Q9-sol1.jpg]]

This shape is a square. We can think of it as made up of 4 right angled triangles, each with base and height equal to 1, so each has area $\begin{array} { r } { \frac { 1 } { 2 } \times 1 \times 1 = \frac { 1 } { 2 } } \end{array}$ , and hence the square has area 2. Thus the correct option is C.
