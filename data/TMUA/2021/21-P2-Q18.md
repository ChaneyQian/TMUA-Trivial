## 题目
A student chooses two distinct real numbers $x$ and $y$ with $0 < x < y < 1$.

The student then attempts to draw a triangle $ABC$ with:

$$
AB = 1
$$

$$
\sin A = x
$$

$$
\sin B = y
$$

Which of the following statements is/are correct?

I For some choice of $x$ and $y$, there is exactly **one** triangle the student could draw.

II For some choice of $x$ and $y$, there are exactly **two** different triangles the student could draw.

III For some choice of $x$ and $y$, there are exactly **three** different triangles the student could draw.

(Note that congruent triangles are considered to be the same.)

$$
\mathbf{A} \quad \text{none of them}
$$

$$
\mathbf{B} \quad \text{I only}
$$

$$
\mathbf{C} \quad \text{II only}
$$

$$
\mathbf{D} \quad \text{III only}
$$

$$
\mathbf{E} \quad \text{I and II only}
$$

$$
\mathbf{F} \quad \text{I and III only}
$$

$$
\mathbf{G} \quad \text{II and III only}
$$

$$
\mathbf{H} \quad \text{I, II and III}
$$

## 答案
C

## 解析
Since we are only given a condition on sin A, the angle A could be acute or obtuse, and similarly for angle B.

Let us write $\theta = \sin ^ { - 1 }$ x and $\phi = \sin ^ { - 1 } y$ for the acute angles; the respective obtuse angles are then $1 8 0 ^ { \circ } - \theta$ and $1 8 0 ^ { \circ } - \phi$

As we are given $x < y$ , we have $\theta < \phi$

We can now consider the four possible cases.

Case 1: both $A$ and $B$ are acute. Then we have this situation:

![[Image/21-P2-Q18-sol1.jpg]]

We therefore know the three angles of the triangle ABC (as given two, we automatically know the third) and one side, and this determines the triangle uniquely (regarding congruent triangles as the same).

So we always obtain exactly one triangle from this case.

Case 2: A is obtuse and B is acute. Then $A = 1 8 0 ^ { \circ } - \theta$ and we have this situation:

![[Image/21-P2-Q18-sol2.jpg]]

Since $\theta < \phi$, , the two lines (rays) from A and B shown in this diagram will never meet. Alternatively, we could note that $A + B = 1 8 0 ^ { \circ } - \theta + \phi { \mathrm { ~ } }$ , and since $\phi - \theta > 0$ , this means that the angles A and B sum to more than $180^{\circ}$.

Therefore this case can never produce a triangle.

Case 3: A is acute and B is obtuse. Then $B = 1 8 0 ^ { \circ } - \phi$ and we have this situation:

![[Image/21-P2-Q18-sol3.jpg]]

Since $\theta < \phi$ , the two lines (rays) from A and B shown will eventually meet, and so we get a unique triangle. Alternatively, we have $A + B = \theta + 1 8 0 ^ { \circ } - \phi = 1 8 0 ^ { \circ } - ( \phi - \theta )$ , and since $\phi - \theta > 0$ , this means that the angles A and B sum to less than $1 8 0 ^ { \circ }$ , so they can be two angles of a triangle.

So we always obtain exactly one triangle from this case.

Case 4: $A$ and $B$ are both obtuse. This is impossible in a triangle.

Therefore this case can never produce a triangle.

In summary, cases 1 and 3 always produce a unique triangle, and cases 2 and 4 never produce a triangle, so the student can always draw exactly two different triangles, regardless of the values of $x$ and y.

Therefore the correct answer is C.
