## 题目
An arithmetic progression and a convergent geometric progression each have first term $\frac{1}{2}$.

The sum of the second terms of the two progressions is $\frac{1}{2}$.

The sum of the third terms of the two progressions is $\frac{1}{8}$.

What is the sum to infinity of the geometric progression?

$$
\mathbf{A} \quad -2
$$

$$
\mathbf{B} \quad -1
$$

$$
\mathbf{C} \quad -\frac{1}{2}
$$

$$
\mathbf{D} \quad -\frac{1}{3}
$$

$$
\mathbf{E} \quad \frac{1}{3}
$$

$$
\mathbf{F} \quad \frac{1}{2}
$$

$$
\mathbf{G} \quad 1
$$

$$
\mathbf{H} \quad 2
$$

## 解析
Let us write the arithmetic progression as

$$
\frac {1}{2}, \quad \frac {1}{2} + d, \quad \frac {1}{2} + 2 d, \quad \frac {1}{2} + 3 d, \quad \dots
$$

and the geometric progression as

$$
\frac {1}{2}, \quad \frac {1}{2} r, \quad \frac {1}{2} r ^ {2}, \quad \frac {1}{2} r ^ {3}, \quad \dots
$$

Then the given information about the second terms and third terms can be written algebraically as

$$
\begin{array}{c} \frac {1}{2} + d + \frac {1}{2} r = \frac {1}{2} \\ \frac {1}{2} + 2 d + \frac {1}{2} r ^ {2} = \frac {1}{8} \end{array}
$$

The first equation simplifies to give $\begin{array} { r } { d + \frac { 1 } { 2 } r = 0 , \mathrm { ~ s o ~ } d = - \frac { 1 } { 2 } r } \end{array}$ . We can then substitute this into the second equation to give

$$
\frac {1}{2} + \frac {1}{2} r ^ {2} - r = \frac {1}{8}
$$

(We could equivalently have eliminated d by subtracting twice the first equation from the second.) Multiplying by 8 and rearranging slightly gives

$$
4 r ^ {2} - 8 r + 3 = 0.
$$

We can solve this by factorising or using the formula, for example; this gives

$$
r = \frac {8 \pm \sqrt {(- 8) ^ {2} - 4 \times 4 \times 3}}{2 \times 4} = \frac {1}{2} \mathrm{or} \frac {3}{2}
$$

Since the geometric progression is convergent, we must have $| r | < 1$ , so $\begin{array} { r } { r = \frac { 1 } { 2 } } \end{array}$ and the sum to infinity is

$$
S _ {\infty} = \frac {a}{1 - r} = \frac {(\frac {1}{2})}{1 - \frac {1}{2}} = 1
$$

so the answer is option G.
