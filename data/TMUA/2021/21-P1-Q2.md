## 题目
The curve $y = x^{3} - 6x + 3$ has turning points at $x = \alpha$ and $x = \beta$ , where $\beta > \alpha$ .
Find $\int_{\alpha}^{\beta} x^{3} - 6x + 3 \, dx$

$$
\mathbf {A} \quad -8\sqrt{2}
$$

$$
\mathbf {B} \quad -10
$$

$$
\mathbf {C} \quad -10 + 6\sqrt{2}
$$

$$
\mathbf {D} \quad 0
$$

$$
\mathbf {E} \quad 12 - 8\sqrt{2}
$$

$$
\mathbf {F} \quad 6\sqrt{2}
$$

$$
\mathbf {G} \quad 12
$$

## 答案
F

## 解析
We first find the turning points by differentiation. We have

$$
{\frac {\mathrm{d} y}{\mathrm{d} x}} = 3 x ^ {2} - 6 = 0 {\mathrm{ifandonlyif}} x ^ {2} = 2
$$

so $\alpha = - \sqrt { 2 }$ and $\beta = { \sqrt { 2 } } .$

We can now compute the integral:

$$
\begin{array}{r l} & {\int_ {\alpha} ^ {\beta} x ^ {3} - 6 x + 3 \mathrm{d} x = \left[ \frac {1}{4} x ^ {4} - 3 x ^ {2} + 3 x \right] _ {- \sqrt {2}} ^ {\sqrt {2}}} \\ & {\qquad = \left(\frac {1}{4} (\sqrt {2}) ^ {4} - 3 (\sqrt {2}) ^ {2} + 3 (\sqrt {2})\right) - \left(\frac {1}{4} (- \sqrt {2}) ^ {4} - 3 (- \sqrt {2}) ^ {2} + 3 (- \sqrt {2})\right)} \\ & {\qquad = 6 \sqrt {2}} \end{array}
$$

where to get the last line, we note that the first terms in each bracket cancel with each other, and similarly for the second terms, so we are left with just $3 { \sqrt { 2 } } - ( - 3 { \sqrt { 2 } } )$ . This is option F.
