## 题目
[4 marks] A regular hexagon is centred at the origin with vertices including $(1, 0)$ and $(-1, 0)$. Find the equation of the **downward-opening** parabola of the form $y = kx^2 + c$ that passes through **four** of the hexagon's vertices.

(a) $y = -\sqrt{3}\,(x^2 - 1)$
(b) $y = -\dfrac{2\sqrt{3}}{3}(x^2 - 1)$
(c) $y = -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}(x^2 - 1)$
(d) $y = -2(x^2 - 1)$
(e) $y = -x^2 + 1$

## 答案
B

## 解析
The circumradius is $1$, so the vertices are $(\pm 1, 0)$ and $\left(\pm\dfrac{1}{2}, \pm\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)$.
A curve $y = kx^{2} + c$ is symmetric about the $y$-axis, so it takes vertices in $\pm x$ pairs;
opening downwards the only possible four are $(\pm 1, 0)$ together with the two upper vertices.

$$k + c = 0, \qquad \frac{k}{4} + c = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \;\Longrightarrow\; -\frac{3k}{4} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, \quad k = -\frac{2\sqrt{3}}{3},\quad c = \frac{2\sqrt{3}}{3}.$$
