## 题目
[4 marks] A function $f$ satisfies $f(x) = |x|$ for $-1 \le x \le 1$ and $f(x) = f(x + 2)$ for all real $x$. For which values of $k$ does $f(x) = kx$ have **exactly four** distinct real solutions?

(a) $k \in \left(0, \dfrac{1}{3}\right)$
(b) $k \in \left(-\dfrac{1}{3}, -\dfrac{1}{5}\right) \cup \left(\dfrac{1}{5}, \dfrac{1}{3}\right)$
(c) $k \in \left(-\dfrac{1}{3}, 0\right) \cup \left(0, \dfrac{1}{3}\right)$
(d) $k \in \left(-\infty, -\dfrac{1}{3}\right) \cup \left(\dfrac{1}{3}, \infty\right)$
(e) $k = \dfrac{1}{3}$ only

## 答案
B

## 解析
$f$ is the triangular wave of period $2$: zeros at the even integers, peaks $f(2m+1) = 1$.
It is even, so $k$ and $-k$ give mirror-image solution sets and the answer must be symmetric.
Take $k > 0$; then every solution has $x \ge 0$, and $x = 0$ is always one of them.

For $m \ge 1$ the line $y = kx$ meets the tooth over $[2m, 2m+2]$ only if it is still no higher than
that peak, i.e. $k(2m+1) \le 1$: twice when $k < \dfrac{1}{2m+1}$, and once (at the peak itself) when
$k = \dfrac{1}{2m+1}$. The tooth over $[0,2]$ gives $x = 0$ and one further point whenever $0 < k < 1$.

So exactly four solutions means the $m=1$ tooth is cut twice and the $m=2$ tooth is missed entirely:

$$\frac{1}{5} < k < \frac{1}{3}.$$

At $k = \dfrac{1}{3}$ the line runs through $(3,1)$ and only three solutions survive; at $k = \dfrac{1}{5}$
it reaches $(5,1)$ and there are five. Reflecting gives the negative half.

The answer is (b).
