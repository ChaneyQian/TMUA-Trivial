## 题目
[4 marks] How many real solutions does the equation

$$\frac{5}{3}x^3 - x^5 = 1$$

have?

(a) $0$
(b) $1$
(c) $2$
(d) $3$
(e) $5$

## 答案
B

## 解析
Let $f(x) = \dfrac{5}{3}x^{3} - x^{5}$, so that $f'(x) = 5x^{2} - 5x^{4} = 5x^{2}(1-x^{2})$.
Thus $f$ increases on $[-1,1]$ and decreases on $(-\infty,-1)$ and on $(1,\infty)$, with
turning values $f(\pm 1) = \pm\dfrac{2}{3}$.

On $[-1,\infty)$ the curve never rises above $f(1) = \dfrac{2}{3} < 1$, so no root there.
On $(-\infty,-1)$ the function decreases from $+\infty$ to $-\dfrac{2}{3}$ and therefore
crosses $y = 1$ exactly once.

The answer is (b).
