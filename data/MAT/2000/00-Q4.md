## 题目
**(a)**

It is known that differentiation satisfies the four rules
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1)&\quad \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}(\text{constant}) = 0, \\
(2)&\quad \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}(x) = 1, \\
(3)&\quad \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\bigl(af(x) + bg(x)\bigr) = a\dfrac{\mathrm{d}f}{\mathrm{d}x} + b\dfrac{\mathrm{d}g}{\mathrm{d}x}
\quad\text{for any constants } a \text{, } b \text{, and} \\
(4)&\quad \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\bigl(f(x)\cdot g(x)\bigr) = \dfrac{\mathrm{d}f}{\mathrm{d}x}\, g(x) + f(x)\, \dfrac{\mathrm{d}g}{\mathrm{d}x}.
\end{aligned}
$$
From these rules alone show that, for $n = 1,2,3,\ldots$,
$$
\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}(x^n) = n x^{n-1}.
$$
Use Rule (4) to find the derivative of the function
$$
f(x) = \dfrac{1}{x^n}.
$$
(It will help you to notice that $x^n \cdot \dfrac{1}{x^n} = 1$.)

**(b)**

A careless calculus student remembers Rules (1), (2) and (3) correctly, but thinks that Rule (4) says
$$
(4')\quad \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\bigl(f(x)\cdot g(x)\bigr) = \dfrac{\mathrm{d}f}{\mathrm{d}x} + f(x)\, \dfrac{\mathrm{d}g}{\mathrm{d}x}.
$$
What will he or she compute for
$$
\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\bigl(x^4\bigr)\,?
$$

## 解析
**(a)** Argue by induction on $n$. For $n = 1$, Rule (2) gives

$$\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\left(x^1\right) = 1 = 1 \cdot x^{0}.$$

Suppose the result holds for some $n \geqslant 1$. Writing $x^{n+1} = x \cdot x^{n}$ and applying Rule (4) with $f(x) = x$ and $g(x) = x^{n}$,

$$\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\left(x^{n+1}\right) = 1 \cdot x^{n} + x \cdot n x^{n-1} = x^{n} + n x^{n} = (n+1)x^{n}.$$

That is the result for $n+1$, so by induction $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\left(x^{n}\right) = nx^{n-1}$ for every positive integer $n$.

Now let $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{x^{n}}$, so that $x^{n} f(x) = 1$. Differentiating both sides, using Rule (4) on the left and Rule (1) on the right,

$$n x^{n-1} f(x) + x^{n} \frac{\mathrm{d}f}{\mathrm{d}x} = 0.$$

Since $f(x) = x^{-n}$, the first term is $n x^{n-1} \cdot x^{-n} = \dfrac{n}{x}$, and therefore

$$\frac{\mathrm{d}f}{\mathrm{d}x} = -\frac{1}{x^{n}} \cdot \frac{n}{x} = -\frac{n}{x^{n+1}}.$$

**(b)** Write $D$ for the student's (incorrect) operation. Rule (2) still gives $D(x) = 1$, and the faulty rule $(4')$ gives $D(fg) = \dfrac{\mathrm{d}f}{\mathrm{d}x} + f \dfrac{\mathrm{d}g}{\mathrm{d}x}$, so building up one factor at a time:

$$D\left(x^2\right) = D(x\cdot x) = D(x) + x\,D(x) = 1 + x,$$

$$D\left(x^3\right) = D\left(x \cdot x^2\right) = D(x) + x\,D\left(x^2\right) = 1 + x(1+x) = 1 + x + x^2,$$

$$D\left(x^4\right) = D\left(x \cdot x^3\right) = D(x) + x\,D\left(x^3\right) = 1 + x\left(1+x+x^2\right).$$

So the student computes

$$\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\left(x^4\right) = 1 + x + x^2 + x^3.$$

(The same answer comes out of splitting $x^4$ as $x^2 \cdot x^2$: $D\left(x^2\right) + x^2 D\left(x^2\right) = (1+x) + x^2(1+x) = 1+x+x^2+x^3$.)
