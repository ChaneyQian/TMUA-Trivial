## 题目
The curves $y = \frac{1}{2}\pi \cos x$ and $x = \frac{1}{2}\pi \cos y$ intersect at the three points $(0, \frac{1}{2}\pi)$, $(a, b)$, $(\frac{1}{2}\pi, 0)$, as shown in the figure below.

![[Image/97-Q4-fig1.jpg]]

**(a)**

Explain why $a = b = \frac{1}{2}\pi \cos b$.

**(b)**

Show that $\pi \sin b = \sqrt{\pi^2 - 4b^2}$.

**(c)**

Show that the area of the shaded region is
$$
\sqrt{\pi^2 - 4b^2} - \frac{\pi}{2} - b^2
$$.

## 解析
**(a)** The equation of the second curve is obtained from the first by interchanging $x$ and $y$, so the two curves are reflections of one another in the line $y = x$. Consequently, if $(a,b)$ is an intersection point then so is $(b,a)$.

The two points $\left(0,\tfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$ and $\left(\tfrac{\pi}{2},0\right)$ form such a reflected pair. Since we are told there are exactly three intersection points, the remaining point $(a,b)$ must be its own reflection, so $a = b$. It lies on the first curve, so

$$a = b = \frac{\pi}{2}\cos b.$$

**(b)** From (a), $\cos b = \dfrac{2b}{\pi}$. From the figure $0 < b < \tfrac{\pi}{2}$, so $\sin b > 0$ and

$$\sin b = \sqrt{1 - \cos^2 b} = \sqrt{1 - \frac{4b^2}{\pi^2}} = \frac{\sqrt{\pi^2 - 4b^2}}{\pi}.$$

Multiplying by $\pi$,

$$\pi \sin b = \sqrt{\pi^2 - 4b^2}.$$

**(c)** The shaded region runs from $x = 0$ to $x = a\ (= b)$, bounded above by $y = \tfrac{\pi}{2}\cos x$ and below by the other curve, which on this range is $y = \arccos\!\left(\tfrac{2x}{\pi}\right)$. So

$$\text{Area} = \underbrace{\int_0^{b} \frac{\pi}{2}\cos x \,\mathrm{d}x}_{U} \; - \; \underbrace{\int_0^{b} \arccos\!\left(\frac{2x}{\pi}\right)\mathrm{d}x}_{V}.$$

The first is immediate:

$$U = \left[\frac{\pi}{2}\sin x\right]_0^{b} = \frac{\pi}{2}\sin b.$$

For $V$, split the region under the lower curve at the height $y = b$. The part below that height is the rectangle $0 \leqslant x \leqslant b$, $0 \leqslant y \leqslant b$, of area $b^2$. The part above it is

$$\left\{(x,y) : b \leqslant y \leqslant \tfrac{\pi}{2},\; 0 \leqslant x \leqslant \tfrac{\pi}{2}\cos y \right\},$$

because for $y \geqslant b$ we have $\tfrac{\pi}{2}\cos y \leqslant \tfrac{\pi}{2}\cos b = b$. Integrating in the $y$-direction,

$$\int_{b}^{\pi/2} \frac{\pi}{2}\cos y \,\mathrm{d}y = \frac{\pi}{2}\left(1 - \sin b\right).$$

Hence $V = b^2 + \dfrac{\pi}{2} - \dfrac{\pi}{2}\sin b$, and

$$\text{Area} = \frac{\pi}{2}\sin b - b^2 - \frac{\pi}{2} + \frac{\pi}{2}\sin b = \pi\sin b - \frac{\pi}{2} - b^2.$$

Finally, substituting $\pi \sin b = \sqrt{\pi^2-4b^2}$ from part (b),

$$\text{Area} = \sqrt{\pi^2 - 4b^2} - \frac{\pi}{2} - b^2.$$

(Numerically $b \approx 0.9340$ and the area is about $0.0827$.)
