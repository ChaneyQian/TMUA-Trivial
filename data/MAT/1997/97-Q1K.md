## 题目
The simultaneous equations
$$
\begin{aligned}
x - 2y + 3z &= 1, \\
2x + 3y - z &= 4, \\
4x - y + 5z &= 6
\end{aligned}
$$
have
(i) no solutions,
(ii) exactly one solution,
(iii) exactly three solutions,
(iv) infinitely many solutions.

### AI备注


## 答案
(iv)

## 解析
We can eliminate variables to solve the system. Multiplying the first equation by $2$ and subtracting it from the second gives $7y - 7z = 2$. Multiplying the first equation by $4$ and subtracting it from the third also gives $7y - 7z = 2$. 
Since the last two equations reduce to the exact same condition, the third equation provides no new information. We are left with two independent linear equations in three variables, which defines a line of intersection. Therefore, there are infinitely many solutions. The answer is (iv).
