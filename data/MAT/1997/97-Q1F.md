## 题目
As the integer $n$ becomes very large and positive,
$$
\frac{\sqrt{n} + (-1)^n}{\sqrt{n}}
$$
(i) approaches (that is, converges to) $0$,
(ii) approaches (that is, converges to) $1$,
(iii) approaches infinity,
(iv) oscillates, but does not converge.

## 备注

### 我的备注

### AI备注


## 答案
(ii)

## 解析
We can rewrite the given expression by dividing both terms in the numerator by $\sqrt{n}$:

$$\frac{\sqrt{n} + (-1)^n}{\sqrt{n}} = 1 + \frac{(-1)^n}{\sqrt{n}}$$

As $n$ becomes very large, the numerator $(-1)^n$ oscillates between $-1$ and $1$, while the denominator $\sqrt{n}$ grows to infinity. Thus, the fraction $\frac{(-1)^n}{\sqrt{n}}$ approaches $0$. The overall expression converges to $1$. The answer is (ii).
