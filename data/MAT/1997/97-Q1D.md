## 题目
For the following statements
$$
P:\ \frac{x(x - 2)}{1 - x} > 0,\qquad Q:\ 1 < x < 2
$$
about a real number $x$,
(i) $P$ implies $Q$, but $Q$ does not imply $P$,
(ii) $Q$ implies $P$, but $P$ does not imply $Q$,
(iii) $P$ implies $Q$, and $Q$ implies $P$,
(iv) $P$ and $Q$ contradict each other.

## 备注

### 我的备注

### AI备注


## 答案
(ii)

## 解析
We solve the inequality $P$ by finding the sign of the expression $\frac{x(x - 2)}{1 - x}$ in the intervals separated by the critical points $x=0, 1, 2$. 
For $x > 2$, the sign is $(+)(+)/(-) = -$. 
For $1 < x < 2$, the sign is $(+)(-)/(-) = +$. 
For $0 < x < 1$, the sign is $(+)(-)/(+) = -$. 
For $x < 0$, the sign is $(-)(-)/(+) = +$. 
Thus, statement $P$ is true for $x < 0$ or $1 < x < 2$. Statement $Q$ is strictly $1 < x < 2$. Therefore, if $Q$ is true, then $P$ must be true, so $Q$ implies $P$. However, $P$ can be true for negative values of $x$, so $P$ does not imply $Q$. The answer is (ii).
