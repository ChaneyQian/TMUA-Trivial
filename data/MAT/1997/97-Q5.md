## 题目
Songs of the Martian classical period had just two notes (let us call them $x$ and $y$) and were constructed according to rigorous rules:

I. the sequence consisting of no notes was deemed to be a song (perhaps the most pleasant);

II. a sequence starting with $x$, followed by two repetitions of an existing song and ending with $y$ was also a song;

III. the sequence of notes obtained by interchanging $x$'s and $y$'s in a song was also a song.

All songs were constructed using those rules.

**(a)**

Write down four songs of length $6$ (that is, songs with exactly $6$ notes).

**(b)**

Show that if there are $k$ songs of length $m$ then there are $2k$ songs of length $2m+2$. Deduce that for each natural number $n$ there are $2^n$ songs of length $2^{n+1}-2$.

Songs of the Martian later period were constructed using also the rule:

IV. if a song ended in $y$ then the sequence of notes obtained by omitting that $y$ was also a song.

**(c)**

What lengths do songs of the later period have? That is, for which natural numbers $n$ is there a song with exactly $n$ notes? Justify your answer.

## 解析
Write $\varnothing$ for the empty song of rule I, and for a song $s$ write $\bar{s}$ for the song obtained by interchanging $x$'s and $y$'s (rule III).

**(a)** Rule II applied to $\varnothing$ gives $x\varnothing\varnothing y = xy$, and rule III gives $yx$; these are the two songs of length $2$. Applying rule II to each of them, and then rule III, gives four songs of length $6$:

$$xxyxyy, \qquad xyxyxy, \qquad yyxyxx, \qquad yxyxyx.$$

(The first two are $x\,s\,s\,y$ with $s = xy$ and $s = yx$; the last two are their letter-interchanges.)

**(b)** Rule II is the only rule that changes the length of a song, and it turns a song of length $m$ into one of length $2m+2$; rule III preserves length. So every song of length $2m+2$ is either

$$x\,s\,s\,y \quad \text{for some song } s \text{ of length } m, \qquad \text{or the interchange } y\,\bar{s}\,\bar{s}\,x \text{ of such a song.}$$

Distinct songs $s$ give distinct songs $x\,s\,s\,y$ (the middle portion recovers $s$), so if there are $k$ songs of length $m$ there are $k$ songs of the first kind and $k$ of the second. The two families are disjoint, since songs of the first kind begin with $x$ and songs of the second begin with $y$. Hence there are exactly

$$2k \text{ songs of length } 2m+2.$$

Now define $m_0 = 0$ and $m_{n+1} = 2m_n + 2$, with $k_n$ the number of songs of length $m_n$. By rule I, $k_0 = 1$ (the empty song), and by the above $k_{n+1} = 2k_n$, so $k_n = 2^n$. Also

$$m_{n+1} + 2 = 2\left(m_n + 2\right),$$

so $m_n + 2 = 2^{n}(m_0+2) = 2^{n+1}$, giving $m_n = 2^{n+1} - 2$. Therefore there are $2^n$ songs of length $2^{n+1}-2$ for every natural number $n$.

**(c)** In the later period there is a song of **every** length $n$; that is, every natural number occurs.

First note that from any song $w$ of length $L \geqslant 1$ one can obtain a song of length $L-1$. If $w$ ends in $y$, rule IV deletes that $y$. If $w$ ends in $x$, then $\bar{w}$ is a song (rule III) of the same length ending in $y$, and rule IV applied to $\bar{w}$ gives a song of length $L-1$.

By part (b) there are songs of length $2^{n+1}-2$ for every $n$, and these lengths are unbounded. So, given any natural number $N$, choose $n$ with $2^{n+1} - 2 \geqslant N$, start from a song of that length, and apply the step above repeatedly until the length has come down to $N$. Every length is therefore achieved, and the empty song covers $N=0$.
