## 题目
A square made of cardboard has sides of unit length and corners marked $A, B, C, D$.

**(a)**

Show that there are eight different ways of placing the cardboard square so that it completely covers the square region in the $(x, y)$ plane with corners at the points $(0, 0)$, $(0, 1)$, $(1, 1)$, $(1, 0)$.

**(b)**

Initially the square is positioned so that $A, B, C, D$ are over the points $(0, 0), (0, 1), (1, 1), (1, 0)$, respectively. You may move the square by either
(i) rotating it in the plane by $90^{\circ}$ about one of the corners, or
(ii) turning it over keeping one of the edges fixed in contact with the plane.

Show that after two moves it is possible to return the square to its initial position but with the corners $B$ and $D$ interchanged.

**(c)**

Show that four of the eight configurations in (a) can be achieved from the initial position and moves described in (b).

## 解析
**(a)** A covering placement sends the four corners $A,B,C,D$ of the cardboard to the four grid corners $(0,0),(0,1),(1,1),(1,0)$, and it is determined completely by that assignment. Corner $A$ may go to any of the $4$ grid corners. Once $A$ is fixed, the neighbours $B$ and $D$ of $A$ must go to the two grid corners adjacent to $A$'s position, and there are $2$ ways round; $C$ is then forced into the remaining corner. So there are

$$4 \times 2 = 8$$

placements. (Equivalently: $4$ rotations with the printed side up, and the same $4$ with the square turned over.)

**(b)** Label the grid corners as in the question, so initially $A,B,C,D$ sit at $(0,0),(0,1),(1,1),(1,0)$.

*Move 1.* Rotate the square through $90^{\circ}$ clockwise about the corner $A$, which is at the origin. This is the map $(x,y) \mapsto (y,-x)$, so

$$A(0,0) \mapsto (0,0), \quad B(0,1) \mapsto (1,0), \quad C(1,1) \mapsto (1,-1), \quad D(1,0) \mapsto (0,-1),$$

and the square now covers $0 \leqslant x \leqslant 1$, $-1 \leqslant y \leqslant 0$.

*Move 2.* Turn the square over, keeping the edge along the $x$-axis fixed. This is the map $(x,y) \mapsto (x,-y)$, so

$$A \mapsto (0,0), \quad B \mapsto (1,0), \quad C \mapsto (1,1), \quad D \mapsto (0,1).$$

The square is back on the original region, with $A$ and $C$ where they started and $B$ and $D$ interchanged, as required. (The net effect is reflection in the diagonal $AC$.)

**(c)** The two moves of (b) achieve reflection in the diagonal $AC$. The same pair of moves performed at the corner $B$ — rotate through $90^{\circ}$ anticlockwise about $B$, i.e. $(x,y) \mapsto (1-y,\,1+x)$, then turn over about the edge lying along $y=1$, i.e. $(x,y)\mapsto(x,2-y)$ — sends

$$A(0,0) \mapsto (1,1), \quad B \mapsto (0,1), \quad C(1,1) \mapsto (0,0), \quad D \mapsto (1,0),$$

which is reflection in the diagonal $BD$. Performing the $AC$-pair and then the $BD$-pair gives the composition of the two reflections, which is rotation through $180^{\circ}$. So the following four of the eight configurations are all achievable:

* $A,B,C,D$ at $(0,0),(0,1),(1,1),(1,0)$ — the initial position, using no moves;
* $A,B,C,D$ at $(0,0),(1,0),(1,1),(0,1)$ — reflection in $AC$, in $2$ moves;
* $A,B,C,D$ at $(1,1),(0,1),(0,0),(1,0)$ — reflection in $BD$, in $2$ moves;
* $A,B,C,D$ at $(1,1),(1,0),(0,0),(0,1)$ — rotation through $180^{\circ}$, in $4$ moves.

In fact these are the only four that can be reached. Colour each lattice point according to the parity of $x+y$. A rotation through $90^{\circ}$ about a lattice point $(u,v)$ sends $(x,y)$ to a point whose coordinate sum is $2v + x - y$, and a turn over a horizontal or vertical lattice line sends $(x,y)$ to a point with coordinate sum $x+y \pm 2k$; in both cases the parity of the coordinate sum of each corner is unchanged. Since $A$ starts on an even point, $A$ can only ever land on $(0,0)$ or $(1,1)$, which rules out the other four configurations.
