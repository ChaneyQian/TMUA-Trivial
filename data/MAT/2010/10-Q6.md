## 题目
For **APPLICANTS IN** $\left\{\begin{array}{l}\textbf{COMPUTER SCIENCE} \\ \textbf{MATHEMATICS \& COMPUTER SCIENCE}\end{array}\right\}$ **ONLY**.

In the questions below, the people involved make statements about each other. Each person is either a saint (S) who always tells the truth or a liar (L) who always lies.

(i) Six people, $P_{1} , P_{2} , \ldots , P_{6}$ sit in order around a circular table with $P_{1}$ sitting to $P_{6} \mathrm { ^{3} s }$ right, as shown in the diagram below.

![[Image/10-Q6-fig1.jpg]]

(a) Suppose all six people say "the person directly opposite me is telling the truth". One possibility is that all six are lying. But, in total, how many different possibilities are there? Explain your reasoning.

(b) Suppose now that all six people say "the person to my left is lying". In how many different ways can this happen? Explain your reasoining.

(ii) Now n people $Q_{1} , Q_{2} , \ldots , Q_{n}$ sit in order around a circular table with $Q_{1}$ sitting to $Q_{n} \mathrm { { ^{~\small{s} } } }$ right.

(a) Suppose that all n people make the statement "the person on my left is lying and the person on my right is telling the truth". Explain why everyone is lying.

(b) Suppose now that every person makes the statement "either the people to my left and right are both lying $o r$ both are telling the truth". If at least one person is lying, show that n is a multiple of three.

## 解析
6. (i) (a) [3 marks] The 6 people splits into 3 pairs sat opposite one another. For consistency each pair has to be both lying or both telling the truth. Hence there are $2 ^{3} = 8$ possible ways in which the statements can be made.

(i) (b) [3 marks] If $P_{1}$ is telling the truth then $P_{6}$ is lying and $P_{5}$ is telling the truth, etc. So the only ways that the statements can be made is as SLSLSL or LSLSLS. That is there are two ways.

(ii) (a) [4 marks] If a particular person is a saint we have an LSS situation, counting left-to-right the saint and neighbours; if not then the possibilities are LLL, SLS, SLL. If we had a LSS situation the neighbour would have to be in a SS? situation, but none such is permitted. Likewise SLS is impossible as there is no permitted LS?. And SLL is impossible as it only propagates by LLL and there is no means to complete the circle. The only possibility is LL . . . L (n times).

(ii) (b) [5 marks] If a person is a saint we are in a SSS or LSL situation; if not we are in an LLS or SLL situation. If we had an SSS situation this could only consistently propagate as $S S S \ldots S$ and no-one would be lying. But a LLS situation can propagate to a LSL, then to a SLL, then to LLS etc. etc. So the situation around the table can be

$$
L L S L L S L L S \dots L L S,
$$

that is any number of repeats of LLS (or equivalently of LSL or SLL), provided of course that n is a multiple of 3.
