## 题目
The function $f$, defined for whole positive numbers, satisfies $f(1) = 1$ and also the rules

$$
\begin{array}{c} f (2 n) = 2 f (n), \\ f (2 n + 1) = 4 f (n), \end{array}
$$

for all values of n. How many numbers n satisfy $f \left( n \right) = 16 ?$

(a) $3,$
(b) $4,$
(c) $5,$
(d) $6.$

## 解析
The function f satisfies $f \left( 1 \right) = 1$ and the two rules

$$
f (2 n) = 2 f (n), \qquad f (2 n + 1) = 4 f (n).
$$

For any positive integer value of k, we can work out $f ( k )$ by considering whether k is even or odd, then using either the first rule or the second rule to work our way downwards until we reach 1, when we can apply the fact that $f ( 1 ) = 1$ . On the way down, we'll gain factors of 2 and 4, and we would like the product of these to be 16.

Let's write $g ( n ) = 2 n$ and $h ( n ) = 2 n + 1$ to represent the expressions inside the brackets. So we have $f ( g ( n ) ) = 2 f ( n )$ and $f ( h ( n ) ) = 4 f ( n )$

The challenge is to apply g and/or h several times (starting from the number 1), so that we make $16 = 2 ^{4}$ . We gain a factor of 2 each time we apply g, and a factor of $2 ^{2}$ each time we apply h. So we could use g four times, or g twice and h once, or h twice. The order that we apply the functions matters, so that middle case gives us three values of n for which $f ( n ) = 16$ . Concretely;

$$
\begin{array}{r l} g (g (g (g (1)))) = 16 & \text {so} \qquad f (16) = 16, \\ g (g (h (1))) = 12 & \text {so} \qquad f (12) = 16, \\ g (h (g (1))) = 10 & \text {so} \qquad f (10) = 16, \\ h (g (g (1))) = 9 & \text {so} \qquad f (9) = 16, \\ h (h (1)) = 7 & \text {so} \qquad f (7) = 16. \end{array}
$$

There are 5 possible solutions and the answer is (c).
