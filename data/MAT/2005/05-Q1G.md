## 答案
C

## 解析
The distance $d(t)$ is the second integral of the acceleration $a(t)$.
The velocity $v(t)$ is the integral of $a(t)$ (the area under the acceleration curve). Assuming the car starts from rest, $v(0) = 0$.
Looking at the graph of $a(t)$, it is periodic and its average value over one period is positive (it spends more time in the positive region or has larger positive peaks). Wait, the graph of $a(t)$ appears to be completely symmetric about the $x$-axis, meaning the integral over a full period is zero. 
Because $a(t)$ starts positive, $v(t)$ increases from $0$ to a maximum, then as $a(t)$ becomes negative, $v(t)$ decreases back to $0$. This implies $v(t) \geqslant 0$ for all time $t$, and $v(t) = 0$ periodically. 
Since $v(t) \geqslant 0$, the distance $d(t)$ (the integral of velocity) must be a monotonically increasing function. At the points where $v(t) = 0$, the slope of the distance graph $d'(t) = 0$, meaning the graph of $d(t)$ will momentarily flatten out (horizontal tangents). 
Graph (c) shows a function that is always increasing but periodically flattens out, creating a "staircase" shape. This perfectly matches the physical behavior described. The answer is (c).
