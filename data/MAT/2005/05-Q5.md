## 题目
**(a)**

Three points $P$, $A$, $B$ lie on a circle which has centre $O$. The point $C$ is where $PO$ extends to meet $AB$ as shown in the diagram below.

![[Image/05-Q5-fig1.jpg]]

Show that $\angle AOC = 2\angle APC$ and $\angle BOC = 2\angle BPC$. Why does this mean that $\angle APB$ is independent of the choice of the point $P$?

**(b)**

Four points $K$, $L$, $M$, $N$ lie on a circle and the lines $LK$ and $MN$ meet outside the circle at a point $S$, as shown in the diagram below.

![[Image/05-Q5-fig2.jpg]]

Using part (a) and the Sine Rule show that
$$
\frac{KS}{NS} = \frac{SM}{SL}.
$$

[You may also assume that part (b) holds true in the special case when $M = N$ in which case the line $SM$ is the tangent to the circle at $M$.]

**(c)**

A tower has height $h$. Assuming the earth to be a perfect sphere of radius $r$, determine the greatest distance $x$ from the top of the tower at which an observer can still see it.

## 解析
**(a)** $OA$ and $OP$ are both radii, so the triangle $OAP$ is isosceles and

$$\angle OAP = \angle OPA.$$

The point $C$ lies on $PO$ produced, so $\angle AOC$ is the exterior angle of triangle $OAP$ at $O$, and an exterior angle equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles:

$$\angle AOC = \angle OAP + \angle OPA = 2\,\angle OPA = 2\,\angle APC,$$

the last step because $C$ is on the ray $PO$, so $\angle APC$ and $\angle APO$ are the same angle. Exactly the same argument applied to the isosceles triangle $OBP$ gives $\angle BOC = 2\,\angle BPC$.

Adding the two results,

$$\angle APB = \angle APC + \angle BPC = \tfrac12\left(\angle AOC + \angle BOC\right) = \tfrac12 \angle AOB.$$

The right-hand side depends only on $A$, $B$ and the centre $O$, and not at all on $P$; so $\angle APB$ is the same wherever $P$ is chosen on the arc.

**(b)** $K$, $L$, $M$, $N$ lie on a circle, so $KNML$ is a cyclic quadrilateral, and by part (a) its opposite angles add up to $180^{\circ}$:

$$\angle KNM + \angle MLK = 180^{\circ}, \qquad \angle NKL + \angle LMN = 180^{\circ}.$$

In the configuration shown, $K$ lies between $S$ and $L$, and $N$ lies between $S$ and $M$. Hence $\angle KNS$ and $\angle KNM$ are supplementary, and likewise $\angle NKS$ and $\angle NKL$, so

$$\angle KNS = 180^{\circ} - \angle KNM = \angle MLK = \angle SLM, \qquad \angle NKS = 180^{\circ} - \angle NKL = \angle LMN = \angle SML,$$

using that the ray $LK$ is the ray $LS$ and the ray $MN$ is the ray $MS$.

Now apply the Sine Rule in the two triangles $SKN$ and $SML$:

$$\frac{KS}{\sin \angle KNS} = \frac{NS}{\sin\angle NKS}, \qquad \frac{SM}{\sin\angle SLM} = \frac{SL}{\sin\angle SML}.$$

Rearranging each,

$$\frac{KS}{NS} = \frac{\sin\angle KNS}{\sin\angle NKS} = \frac{\sin\angle SLM}{\sin\angle SML} = \frac{SM}{SL}.$$

**(c)** Let $T$ be the top of the tower and $O$ the centre of the earth, so $OT = r + h$. The observer can just see the top of the tower when the line of sight from $T$ grazes the surface, i.e. along the tangent from $T$ to the circle; beyond that point the earth blocks the view. So $x$ is the length of the tangent from $T$.

Apply part (b) with $S = T$, taking the line through $T$ and $O$ to meet the circle at $K$ (near) and $L$ (far), and taking the tangent case $M = N$ with $M$ the point of contact. Then

$$TK = OT - r = h, \qquad TL = OT + r = h + 2r,$$

and (b) gives $\dfrac{TK}{TM} = \dfrac{TM}{TL}$, i.e. $TM^2 = TK \cdot TL$. Therefore

$$x^2 = h(h+2r) \qquad \text{and} \qquad x = \sqrt{h(h+2r)} = \sqrt{h^2 + 2rh}.$$

(Equivalently, by Pythagoras in the right-angled triangle $OMT$: $x^2 = (r+h)^2 - r^2 = h^2 + 2rh$. Since $h$ is tiny compared with $r$, in practice $x \approx \sqrt{2rh}$.)
