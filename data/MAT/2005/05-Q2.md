## 题目
**(a)**

Show, with working, that
$$
x^{3} - (1 + \cos\theta + \sin\theta)x^{2} + (\cos\theta\sin\theta + \cos\theta + \sin\theta)x - \sin\theta\cos\theta, \tag{1}
$$
equals
$$
(x - 1)\bigl(x^{2} - (\cos\theta + \sin\theta)x + \cos\theta\sin\theta\bigr).
$$
Deduce that the cubic in (1) has roots
$$
1,\qquad \cos\theta,\qquad \sin\theta.
$$

**(b)**

Give the roots when $\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$.

**(c)**

Find all values of $\theta$ in the range $0 \leqslant \theta < 2\pi$ such that two of the three roots are equal.

**(d)**

What is the greatest possible difference between two of the roots, and for what values of $\theta$ in the range $0 \leqslant \theta < 2\pi$ does this greatest difference occur?

Show that for each such $\theta$ the cubic (1) is the same.

## 解析
Write $c = \cos\theta$ and $s = \sin\theta$ throughout.

**(a)** Expanding the given product,

$$(x-1)\left(x^2 - (c+s)x + cs\right) = x^3 - (c+s)x^2 + csx - x^2 + (c+s)x - cs,$$

and collecting terms,

$$= x^3 - (1+c+s)x^2 + \left(cs + c + s\right)x - cs,$$

which is exactly the cubic (1). Now $x^2 - (c+s)x + cs = (x-c)(x-s)$, since $c$ and $s$ have sum $c+s$ and product $cs$. Hence

$$(1) = (x-1)(x-\cos\theta)(x-\sin\theta),$$

so the roots of the cubic are $1$, $\cos\theta$ and $\sin\theta$.

**(b)** With $\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$ we have $\cos\theta = \dfrac12$ and $\sin\theta = \dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$, so the roots are

$$1, \qquad \frac12, \qquad \frac{\sqrt3}{2}.$$

**(c)** Two of the roots $1$, $\cos\theta$, $\sin\theta$ coincide in exactly the following cases.

* $\cos\theta = 1$: this gives $\theta = 0$.
* $\sin\theta = 1$: this gives $\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$.
* $\cos\theta = \sin\theta$, i.e. $\tan\theta = 1$: this gives $\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$ or $\theta = \dfrac{5\pi}{4}$.

So in $0 \leqslant \theta < 2\pi$ the values are

$$\theta = 0, \quad \frac{\pi}{4}, \quad \frac{\pi}{2}, \quad \frac{5\pi}{4}.$$

**(d)** Every root lies in $[-1,1]$, since $|\cos\theta| \leqslant 1$, $|\sin\theta| \leqslant 1$ and the third root is $1$. Hence the difference between any two roots is at most $1 - (-1) = 2$.

This is attained only when some root equals $-1$, which needs $\cos\theta = -1$ or $\sin\theta = -1$, i.e.

$$\theta = \pi \qquad \text{or} \qquad \theta = \frac{3\pi}{2}.$$

At $\theta = \pi$ the roots are $1, -1, 0$; at $\theta = \tfrac{3\pi}{2}$ they are $1, 0, -1$. So the greatest possible difference is

$$2,$$

occurring at $\theta = \pi$ and $\theta = \tfrac{3\pi}{2}$.

In both cases $\{\cos\theta, \sin\theta\} = \{-1, 0\}$, so $\cos\theta + \sin\theta = -1$ and $\cos\theta\sin\theta = 0$; substituting into (1),

$$x^3 - (1 - 1)x^2 + (0 - 1)x - 0 = x^3 - x.$$

The cubic is $x^3 - x$ for each such $\theta$, so it is indeed the same cubic in both cases.
