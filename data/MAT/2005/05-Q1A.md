## 题目
The area of the region bounded by the curves $y = x^{2}$ and $y = x + 2$ equals
(a) $\dfrac{9}{2}$
(b) $\dfrac{7}{3}$
(c) $\dfrac{7}{2}$
(d) $\dfrac{11}{2}$

### AI备注


## 答案
A

## 解析
To find the area bounded by the curves $y = x^{2}$ and $y = x + 2$, we first find their points of intersection by setting the equations equal to each other:

$$x^{2} = x + 2 \implies x^{2} - x - 2 = 0 \implies (x - 2)(x + 1) = 0$$

The curves intersect at $x = -1$ and $x = 2$. Between these points, the line $y = x + 2$ is above the parabola $y = x^{2}$. The area $A$ is given by the integral:

$$A = \int_{-1}^{2} \left( (x + 2) - x^{2} \right) \mathrm{d}x$$

Integrating term by term:

$$A = \left[ \frac{1}{2}x^{2} + 2x - \frac{1}{3}x^{3} \right]_{-1}^{2}$$

Evaluating at the upper limit $x = 2$:

$$\frac{1}{2}(4) + 2(2) - \frac{1}{3}(8) = 2 + 4 - \frac{8}{3} = \frac{10}{3}$$

Evaluating at the lower limit $x = -1$:

$$\frac{1}{2}(1) + 2(-1) - \frac{1}{3}(-1) = \frac{1}{2} - 2 + \frac{1}{3} = -\frac{7}{6}$$

Subtracting the two values:

$$A = \frac{10}{3} - \left(-\frac{7}{6}\right) = \frac{20}{6} + \frac{7}{6} = \frac{27}{6} = \frac{9}{2}$$

The answer is (a).
