## 题目
**(a)**

Find the co-ordinates of the turning points of
$$
f(x) = e^{x}\bigl(2x^{2} - x - 1\bigr).
$$

**(b)**

Sketch the graph of $y = f(x)$ on the axes below for the range $-4 \leqslant x \leqslant 2$.

**(c)**

Now consider
$$
g(x) =
\begin{cases}
e^{x}\bigl(2x^{2} - x - 1\bigr) & \text{if } x < 1; \\
\sin(x - 1) & \text{if } x \geqslant 1.
\end{cases}
$$
Determine, with explanations, the maximum and minimum values of $g(x)$ as $x$ varies over the real numbers.

## 解析
**(a)** By the product rule,

$$f'(x) = \mathrm{e}^{x}\left(2x^2-x-1\right) + \mathrm{e}^{x}(4x-1) = \mathrm{e}^{x}\left(2x^2+3x-2\right) = \mathrm{e}^{x}(2x-1)(x+2).$$

Since $\mathrm{e}^{x} > 0$, the stationary points are at $x = -2$ and $x = \tfrac12$. Their $y$-values are

$$f(-2) = \mathrm{e}^{-2}(8+2-1) = \frac{9}{\mathrm{e}^{2}}, \qquad f\left(\tfrac12\right) = \mathrm{e}^{1/2}\left(\tfrac12 - \tfrac12 - 1\right) = -\sqrt{\mathrm{e}}.$$

So the turning points are

$$\left(-2,\; \frac{9}{\mathrm{e}^{2}}\right) \approx (-2,\, 1.22) \qquad \text{and} \qquad \left(\frac12,\; -\sqrt{\mathrm{e}}\right) \approx (0.5,\, -1.65).$$

The sign of $(2x-1)(x+2)$ is positive, then negative, then positive, so the first is a (local) maximum and the second a (local) minimum.

**(b)** Since $2x^2-x-1 = (2x+1)(x-1)$, the curve crosses the $x$-axis at $x = -\tfrac12$ and $x = 1$. As $x \to -\infty$ the exponential factor dominates the quadratic, so $f(x) \to 0$ from above. On $-4 \leqslant x \leqslant 2$ the curve therefore rises from $f(-4) = 35\mathrm{e}^{-4} \approx 0.64$ to the local maximum $\left(-2, 9\mathrm{e}^{-2}\right)$, falls through the zero at $x=-\tfrac12$ to the local minimum $\left(\tfrac12, -\sqrt{\mathrm{e}}\right)$, and then climbs steeply through the zero at $x=1$ up to $f(2) = 5\mathrm{e}^{2} \approx 36.9$.

**(c)** Note first that $g$ is continuous at $x=1$: the upper formula tends to $\mathrm{e}(2-1-1) = 0$ and $\sin(1-1) = 0$.

*On $x \geqslant 1$.* Here $g(x) = \sin(x-1)$, whose values fill out the whole of $[-1,1]$ as $x-1$ runs over $[0,\infty)$. So on this half-line the largest value is $1$ and the smallest is $-1$, both attained.

*On $x < 1$.* Here $g = f$. From (a), $f$ increases on $(-\infty,-2]$, decreases on $[-2,\tfrac12]$ and increases on $[\tfrac12,1)$. Since $f \to 0$ as $x \to -\infty$ and $f(x) \to 0$ as $x \to 1^{-}$, the largest value on this half-line is the local maximum $9\mathrm{e}^{-2}$ at $x=-2$ and the smallest is the local minimum $-\sqrt{\mathrm{e}}$ at $x = \tfrac12$.

Comparing the two halves, and using $9\mathrm{e}^{-2} \approx 1.218 > 1$ and $\sqrt{\mathrm{e}} \approx 1.649 > 1$:

$$\text{maximum of } g = \frac{9}{\mathrm{e}^{2}} \; \text{ attained at } x = -2, \qquad \text{minimum of } g = -\sqrt{\mathrm{e}} \; \text{ attained at } x = \frac12.$$
