## 题目
The numbers $x$ and $y$ satisfy
$$
(x - 1)^{2} + y^{2} \leqslant 1.
$$
The largest that $x + y$ can be is
(a) $2$
(b) $1 + \sqrt{2}$
(c) $3$
(d) $2 + \sqrt{2}$

### 我的备注

### AI备注


## 答案
B

## 解析
The inequality $(x - 1)^{2} + y^{2} \leqslant 1$ describes a filled circular disk of radius $1$ centered at the point $(1, 0)$ in the $xy$-plane. 
We want to maximize the quantity $k = x + y$. This is equivalent to finding the largest value of $k$ such that the line $x + y - k = 0$ intersects the disk. The maximum value of $k$ occurs when the line is tangent to the circle. 
The perpendicular distance from the center of the circle $(1, 0)$ to the tangent line $x + y - k = 0$ must equal the radius of the circle, which is $1$. Using the point-to-line distance formula:

$$\frac{|1(1) + 1(0) - k|}{\sqrt{1^{2} + 1^{2}}} = 1 \implies \frac{|1 - k|}{\sqrt{2}} = 1 \implies |1 - k| = \sqrt{2}$$

This gives $1 - k = \sqrt{2}$ or $1 - k = -\sqrt{2}$. Since we want to maximize $k$, we choose the solution that yields a larger positive value:

$$k - 1 = \sqrt{2} \implies k = 1 + \sqrt{2}$$

The answer is (b).
