## 题目
[4 marks] Find the area of the region that lies **below** the curve $y = x^3 - 2x$ **and inside** the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 9$.

(a) $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$
(b) $3\pi$
(c) $\dfrac{9\pi}{2}$
(d) $9\pi$
(e) $\dfrac{27\pi}{2}$

## 答案
C

## 解析
No integration is needed: $x^3-2x$ is odd, so both the curve and the circle are carried to themselves by the half-turn $(x,y)\mapsto(-x,-y)$, and that half-turn exchanges "below the curve" with "above the curve".

The curve cuts the disc into exactly two pieces, since putting $u=x^2$ in $x^2+(x^3-2x)^2=9$ gives

$$u^3-4u^2+5u-9=0,$$

whose derivative $(3u-5)(u-1)$ shows it has a single root $u\approx 3.31$, i.e. just two intersection points $(\pm 1.82,\pm 2.39)$.

The two pieces are swapped by the half-turn, so each is half the disc: $\frac12\pi\cdot 3^2=\frac{9\pi}{2}$.

The answer is (c).
