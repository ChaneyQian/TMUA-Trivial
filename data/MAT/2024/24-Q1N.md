## 题目
[4 marks] Consider $$y = \frac{2e^{\cos x + 1} - e^2 - 1}{e^2 - 1}.$$ Which of the following statements is **not** true?

(a) $y(x) = y(-x)$
(b) $y$ has a maximum at $x = 2\pi$ with $y = 1$
(c) $y$ has a minimum at $x = \pi$ with $y = -1$
(d) $y(x) = y(\pi + x)$
(e) The range of $y$ is $[-1,\,1]$

## 答案
D

## 解析
Put $t=\cos x\in[-1,1]$. Then $e^{t+1}$ runs over $[1,e^2]$ and $y=\dfrac{2e^{t+1}-e^2-1}{e^2-1}$ is an increasing function of $t$ with $y=-1$ at $t=-1$ and $y=1$ at $t=1$. So $y$ is just an increasing reparametrisation of $\cos x$: it inherits evenness, period $2\pi$, its maxima where $\cos x=1$ and its minima where $\cos x=-1$, and its range is $[-1,1]$. That makes (a), (b), (c), (e) all true.

Not (d): it would force $\cos x=\cos(\pi+x)=-\cos x$, i.e. $\cos x\equiv0$. The period is $2\pi$, not $\pi$, and a shift by $\pi$ carries the peaks onto the troughs.
