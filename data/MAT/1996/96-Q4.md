## 题目
**(a)**

Find the values of

(i) $\int_{-1}^{1}(x^2 - x)\,\mathrm{d}x$,

(ii) $\int_{-1}^{1}\left(x^3 + x^2 - 2x\right)\,\mathrm{d}x$.

**(b)**

Sketch the graph of $y = x^2 - x$ and indicate which difference in areas is represented by your answer to (a)(i).

**(c)**

Find the total area (measured positively) that lies between the graphs of $y = x^2 - x$ and $y = x^3 + x^2 - 2x$ between $x = -1$ and $x = 1$.

**(d)**

The answers to (a)(i) and (a)(ii) are related in a particular way. Explain how the relationship can be seen without working out any integrals.

## 解析
**(a)(i)**

$$\int_{-1}^{1}\left(x^2-x\right)\,\mathrm{d}x = \left[\frac{x^3}{3} - \frac{x^2}{2}\right]_{-1}^{1} = \left(\frac13 - \frac12\right) - \left(-\frac13 - \frac12\right) = \frac{2}{3}.$$

**(a)(ii)** The terms $x^3$ and $-2x$ are odd functions, so they contribute nothing over the symmetric interval $[-1,1]$, and

$$\int_{-1}^{1}\left(x^3+x^2-2x\right)\,\mathrm{d}x = \int_{-1}^{1} x^2\,\mathrm{d}x = \left[\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_{-1}^{1} = \frac{2}{3}.$$

**(b)** Since $x^2 - x = x(x-1)$, the curve is a $\cup$-shaped parabola meeting the $x$-axis at $x=0$ and $x=1$, with minimum $-\tfrac14$ at $x = \tfrac12$. On $-1 \leqslant x \leqslant 0$ the curve lies above the axis; on $0 \leqslant x \leqslant 1$ it lies below.

The integral in (a)(i) is therefore the area above the axis minus the area below it:

$$\int_{-1}^{0}\left(x^2-x\right)\mathrm{d}x = \frac{5}{6}, \qquad -\int_{0}^{1}\left(x^2-x\right)\mathrm{d}x = \frac{1}{6}, \qquad \frac{5}{6} - \frac{1}{6} = \frac{2}{3}.$$

**(c)** The gap between the two curves is

$$\left(x^3+x^2-2x\right) - \left(x^2-x\right) = x^3 - x = x(x-1)(x+1),$$

which is $\geqslant 0$ on $[-1,0]$ and $\leqslant 0$ on $[0,1]$. So the curves cross only at $x = -1, 0, 1$ and the total area is

$$\int_{-1}^{1}\left|x^3-x\right|\mathrm{d}x = \int_{-1}^{0}\left(x^3-x\right)\mathrm{d}x - \int_{0}^{1}\left(x^3-x\right)\mathrm{d}x.$$

Since $\left[\dfrac{x^4}{4} - \dfrac{x^2}{2}\right]_{-1}^{0} = \dfrac14$ and $\left[\dfrac{x^4}{4} - \dfrac{x^2}{2}\right]_{0}^{1} = -\dfrac14$, the total area is

$$\frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{2}.$$

**(d)** The two integrands differ by $x^3 - x$, which is an odd function. Its integral over the symmetric interval $[-1,1]$ is zero, because the contribution from $[0,1]$ cancels that from $[-1,0]$. Hence the two integrals must be equal, without either being evaluated.
