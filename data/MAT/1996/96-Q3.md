## 题目
**(a)**

Write down the equation of the straight line through the point $(1,2)$ with slope $-1$.

**(b)**

Let $l$ be a line with equation

$$
y = (2 - a) + ax,
$$

where $a$ is a constant. Show that, for any $a$, the line passes through the point $(1,2)$. Find the equation of the line perpendicular to this line which also passes through the point $(1,2)$.

**(c)**

Find the equations of the lines which pass through the point $(1,2)$ and have perpendicular distance $1$ from the origin.

## 解析
**(a)** Using $y - y_0 = m(x - x_0)$ with $m = -1$ and $(x_0,y_0) = (1,2)$:

$$y - 2 = -(x-1), \qquad \text{that is} \qquad y = 3 - x.$$

**(b)** Putting $x = 1$ into $y = (2-a) + ax$ gives

$$y = (2-a) + a = 2,$$

independently of $a$, so the line passes through $(1,2)$ for every $a$.

The line $l$ has gradient $a$. For $a \neq 0$ the perpendicular through $(1,2)$ has gradient $-1/a$, so

$$y - 2 = -\frac{1}{a}(x-1), \qquad \text{that is} \qquad y = 2 - \frac{x-1}{a}.$$

(When $a = 0$ the line $l$ is horizontal and the perpendicular through $(1,2)$ is the vertical line $x = 1$.)

**(c)** First take a line of gradient $m$ through $(1,2)$:

$$y = m(x-1) + 2, \qquad \text{that is} \qquad mx - y + (2-m) = 0.$$

Its perpendicular distance from the origin is

$$\frac{|2-m|}{\sqrt{m^2+1}} = 1 \implies (2-m)^2 = m^2 + 1 \implies 4 - 4m = 1,$$

since the $m^2$ terms cancel. Hence $m = \tfrac34$ and the line is

$$y = \frac{3}{4}x + \frac{5}{4}, \qquad \text{that is} \qquad 3x - 4y + 5 = 0.$$

Its distance from the origin is $|5|/\sqrt{3^2+4^2} = 1$, as required.

This argument misses lines of undefined gradient, so the vertical line through $(1,2)$ must be checked separately: $x = 1$ is at distance $1$ from the origin, so it also qualifies. The two lines are

$$x = 1 \qquad \text{and} \qquad 3x - 4y + 5 = 0.$$
