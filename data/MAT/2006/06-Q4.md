In the diagram below are drawn the circle $x^{2} + (y - 1)^{2} = 1$ and the parabola $y = -\dfrac{1}{4}x^{2}$.

![[Image/06-Q4-fig1.jpg]]

**(a)**

Find the equation of the tangent to the parabola $y = -\dfrac{1}{4}x^{2}$ at the point $\bigl(t, -\dfrac{1}{4}t^{2}\bigr)$.

**(b)**

Show that, when $t = 2\sqrt{3}$, this tangent is also a tangent to the circle $x^{2} + (y - 1)^{2} = 1$.
By symmetry the tangent to the parabola at $(-2\sqrt{3}, -3)$ is also a tangent to the circle. These tangents are also shown on the diagram above.

**(c)**

The first and second tangents meet the $x$-axis at $A$ and $B$ respectively; the two tangents intersect at $C$. Find the angle $CAB$.

Deduce that the area of the shaded region $R$, bounded by the circle and the two tangents, is
$$
\sqrt{3} - \frac{\pi}{3}.
$$

## 解析
**(a)** Differentiating $y = -\tfrac14 x^2$ gives $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} = -\dfrac{x}{2}$, so the tangent at $\left(t, -\tfrac14 t^2\right)$ has gradient $-\tfrac{t}{2}$ and equation

$$y + \frac{t^2}{4} = -\frac{t}{2}(x-t), \qquad \text{that is} \qquad y = -\frac{t}{2}x + \frac{t^2}{4}.$$

**(b)** Putting $t = 2\sqrt3$ gives $\dfrac{t}{2} = \sqrt3$ and $\dfrac{t^2}{4} = 3$, so the tangent is

$$y = -\sqrt3\,x + 3, \qquad \text{that is} \qquad \sqrt3\,x + y - 3 = 0.$$

The circle $x^2+(y-1)^2 = 1$ has centre $(0,1)$ and radius $1$, and the distance from the centre to this line is

$$\frac{\left|\sqrt3 \cdot 0 + 1 - 3\right|}{\sqrt{\left(\sqrt3\right)^2 + 1^2}} = \frac{2}{2} = 1,$$

which equals the radius. So the line is also a tangent to the circle.

**(c)** The first tangent meets $y=0$ where $\sqrt3 x = 3$, so $A = \left(\sqrt3, 0\right)$. By symmetry the second tangent is $y = \sqrt3 x + 3$, meeting the $x$-axis at $B = \left(-\sqrt3, 0\right)$; the two meet where $-\sqrt3 x + 3 = \sqrt3 x + 3$, i.e. at $C = (0,3)$.

At $A$ the vector along $AB$ is $(-1,0)$ and the vector along $AC$ is $\left(-\sqrt3, 3\right)$, of length $\sqrt{3+9} = 2\sqrt3$. Hence

$$\cos \angle CAB = \frac{\sqrt3}{2\sqrt3} = \frac12 \implies \angle CAB = 60^{\circ}.$$

By symmetry $\angle CBA = 60^{\circ}$ too, so $ABC$ is equilateral with side $AB = 2\sqrt3$.

Now observe that the circle is the inscribed circle of this triangle: it touches both tangents by (b), and it touches the $x$-axis at the origin, since the distance from $(0,1)$ to $y=0$ is $1$.

The shaded region $R$ is what is left of the corner at $C$ after the circle is removed, so it is bounded by the two tangent segments from $C$ and the near arc of the circle. Let $O' = (0,1)$ be the centre and let the tangents touch the circle at $P_1$ and $P_2$. Then $CO' = 2$ and, since $O'P_i \perp CP_i$,

$$CP_1 = CP_2 = \sqrt{2^2-1^2} = \sqrt3, \qquad \tan\angle P_1O'C = \frac{CP_1}{O'P_1} = \sqrt3 \implies \angle P_1 O' C = 60^{\circ}.$$

So the kite $CP_1O'P_2$ consists of two right-angled triangles of area $\tfrac12 \times \sqrt3 \times 1$ each, total area $\sqrt3$, and the angle $\angle P_1O'P_2 = 120^{\circ} = \tfrac{2\pi}{3}$. Removing the circular sector of that angle,

$$\text{Area of } R = \sqrt3 - \frac12 r^2 \theta = \sqrt3 - \frac12 \times 1^2 \times \frac{2\pi}{3} = \sqrt3 - \frac{\pi}{3},$$

as required. (Numerically this is about $0.685$.)
