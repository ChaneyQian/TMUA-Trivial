## 题目
**For APPLICANTS IN COMPUTER SCIENCE ONLY.**

The game of ABC involves creating "words" formed from the letters $\mathbf{A}$, $\mathbf{B}$, $\mathbf{C}$, according to certain rules. All forms of the game start with the word $\mathbf{AB}$.

In the simple form of the game, there is a single rule:

If the current word is of the form $\mathbf{A}x$, for some word $x$, then it may be replaced with the word $\mathbf{A}xx$. \hfill (1)

For example the word $\mathbf{ABC}$ could be replaced by $\mathbf{ABCBC}$.

**(a)**

Show how to produce the word $\mathbf{ABBBB}$.

**(b)**

Describe precisely all the words that can be produced in the simple form of the game. You may want to write $\mathbf{B}^{n}$ as a shorthand for the word formed from $n$ copies of $\mathbf{B}$; e.g. $\mathbf{AB}^{4} = \mathbf{ABBBB}$.

In the intermediate form of the game, there is a second rule:

If the current word is of the form $x\mathbf{BBB}$, for some word $x$, then it may be replaced by the word $\mathbf{C}x$. \hfill (2)

**(c)**

Show how to produce the word $\mathbf{CCABB}$.

**(d)**

Describe precisely all the words that can be produced in the intermediate form of the game. Explain your answer.

In the advanced form of the game, there is a third rule:

If the current word is of the form $x\mathbf{B}$, for some word $x$, then it may be replaced by the word $x$. \hfill (3)

**(e)**

Describe precisely all the words that can be produced in the advanced form of the game. Explain your answer.

## 解析
Every word that can arise consists of a block of $\mathbf{C}$'s, then a single $\mathbf{A}$, then a block of $\mathbf{B}$'s: the starting word $\mathbf{AB}$ has this shape, and each of the three rules preserves it. So write a word as $\mathbf{C}^{j}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}^{n}$ and record it as the pair $(j,n)$; the start is $(0,1)$. In this notation the rules read

$$(1)\; (0,n) \to (0,2n), \qquad (2)\; (j,n) \to (j+1,\,n-3) \text{ when } n \geqslant 3, \qquad (3)\; (j,n) \to (j,\,n-1) \text{ when } n \geqslant 1 .$$

Rule (1) needs the word to begin with $\mathbf{A}$, which is why it applies only when $j = 0$; there $x = \mathbf{B}^{n}$ and $\mathbf{A}xx = \mathbf{AB}^{2n}$.

**(a)** Apply rule (1) twice:

$$\mathbf{AB} \to \mathbf{ABB} \to \mathbf{ABBBB},$$

taking $x = \mathbf{B}$ and then $x = \mathbf{BB}$.

**(b)** In the simple game only rule (1) is available, so from $\mathbf{AB}^{n}$ one can only pass to $\mathbf{AB}^{2n}$. Starting from $n=1$, the reachable values of $n$ are exactly $1, 2, 4, 8, \ldots$, so the words that can be produced are precisely

$$\mathbf{AB}^{2^{k}} \qquad \text{for } k = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$$

**(c)** Apply rule (1) three times and then rule (2) twice:

$$\mathbf{AB} \to \mathbf{AB}^{2} \to \mathbf{AB}^{4} \to \mathbf{AB}^{8} \to \mathbf{CAB}^{5} \to \mathbf{CCAB}^{2},$$

the last two steps taking $x = \mathbf{AB}^{5}$ and then $x = \mathbf{CAB}^{2}$ in rule (2). The final word is $\mathbf{CCABB}$.

**(d)** The words producible in the intermediate game are exactly

$$\mathbf{C}^{j}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}^{n} \quad \text{with } j, n \geqslant 0 \text{ and } n + 3j \text{ a power of } 2$$

(counting $2^{0}=1$ as a power of $2$).

*These are all producible.* Given such $j$ and $n$, say $n + 3j = 2^{k}$, apply rule (1) $k$ times to reach $(0, 2^{k})$, then rule (2) $j$ times. Each application of rule (2) is legal, because at the $i$-th step the number of $\mathbf{B}$'s is $2^{k}-3(i-1) \geqslant 2^{k}-3(j-1) \geqslant 3$.

*Nothing else is producible.* The quantity $n + 3j$ is unchanged by rule (2) and doubled by rule (1), and it starts at $1$. So $n+3j$ is always a power of $2$.

**(e)** In the advanced game, rule (3) removes a single trailing $\mathbf{B}$, so it decreases $n$ by $1$ and leaves $j$ alone. Now **every** word of the form

$$\mathbf{C}^{j}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}^{n} \quad \text{with } j, n \geqslant 0$$

can be produced, and (as noted at the start) no other words can.

To see that all of them occur, first note that $(0,N)$ is reachable for every $N \geqslant 0$: choose $k$ with $2^{k} \geqslant N$, use rule (1) $k$ times to reach $(0,2^{k})$, then use rule (3) $2^{k}-N$ times. Now, given any $j$ and $n$, reach $(0,\, n+3j)$ this way and apply rule (2) $j$ times; as in (d) each application is legal, and it ends at $(j,n)$.
