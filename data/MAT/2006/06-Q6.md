## 题目
**For APPLICANTS IN**
$\left\{\begin{array}{l}\text{MATHS \& COMPUTER SCIENCE}\\\text{COMPUTER SCIENCE}\end{array}\right\}$
**ONLY.**

Portia has three boxes made from gold, silver and lead. She has placed a prize in one of these boxes and challenges a friend, Bassanio, to find the prize.

She explains that on each box there is a message which may be true or false. On the basis of these messages Bassanio should be able to choose the box with the prize in.

**(a)**

Initially suppose that there are only two boxes, gold and silver, one of which contains the prize. The messages read:

**Gold:** The prize is not in here.

**Silver:** Exactly one of these messages is true.

Which box contains the prize? Explain your answer. [Hint: consider separately the cases where the message on the silver box is true or false.]

**(b)**

Now suppose that there are all three boxes and that Portia has left the following messages on them:

**Gold:** The prize is in here.

**Silver:** The prize is in here.

**Lead:** At least two of these messages are false.

Which box should Bassanio choose? Explain your answer. [Hint: show that the message on the lead box cannot be false.]

**(c)**

In this version of the challenge, Portia puts a dagger into one of the boxes. Bassanio must choose a box that does **not** contain the dagger. The messages on the boxes now read as follows:

**Gold:** The dagger is in this box.

**Silver:** The dagger is not in this box.

**Lead:** At most one of these messages is true.

Which box should Bassanio choose? Explain your answer.

## 解析
**(a)** The prize is in the **gold** box. Consider the two cases for the silver message.

*Suppose the silver message is true.* Then exactly one of the two messages is true, and since silver's is true, gold's is false. "The prize is not in here" being false means the prize **is** in the gold box. This is consistent: gold false, silver true is indeed exactly one true message.

*Suppose the silver message is false.* Then it is not the case that exactly one message is true, so either both are true or neither is. Both cannot be true, since silver's is false; so neither is true, and in particular gold's message is false — again the prize is in the gold box. This too is consistent, with no true messages.

Either way the prize is in the gold box.

**(b)** Bassanio should choose the **lead** box.

First show the lead message cannot be false. If it were false, then it is *not* the case that at least two of the three messages are false, so at most one is false; that one is lead's own message, and the gold and silver messages would both have to be true. But then the prize would be in the gold box and in the silver box at once, which is impossible.

So the lead message is true. Being true, at least two of the three messages are false; since lead's is true, the other two must be the false ones. So the prize is not in the gold box and not in the silver box, and therefore it is in the lead box.

**(c)** Bassanio should choose the **lead** box: the dagger can never be in it.

Suppose the dagger were in the lead box. Then the gold message ("the dagger is in this box", i.e. in gold) is false, and the silver message ("the dagger is not in this box", i.e. not in silver) is true. Now look at the lead message.

* If the lead message were true, then at most one of the three would be true; but silver's and lead's would both be true, giving two. Contradiction.
* If the lead message were false, then more than one of the three would be true; but only silver's is true. Contradiction.

Either way we reach a contradiction, so the dagger is not in the lead box.

(For completeness, both of the other placements are consistent. If the dagger is in gold, then gold and silver are both true and lead is false, which agrees with lead's message being false. If the dagger is in silver, then gold and silver are both false and lead is true, and exactly one message — lead's — is true, as lead claims.)
