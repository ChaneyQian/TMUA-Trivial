## 题目
**For ALL APPLICANTS.**

The equation
$$
(2 + x - x^{2})^{2} = 16
$$
has:
(a) no real roots,
(b) one real root,
(c) two real roots,
(d) three real roots.

## 备注

### 我的备注

### AI备注


## 答案
C

## 解析
Taking the square root of both sides of the equation $(2 + x - x^{2})^{2} = 16$, we get two quadratic equations:
$2 + x - x^{2} = 4 \quad \text{or} \quad 2 + x - x^{2} = -4$

Let us analyze each equation separately:
1. $2 + x - x^{2} = 4 \implies x^{2} - x + 2 = 0$. The discriminant is $\Delta = (-1)^{2} - 4(1)(2) = 1 - 8 = -7$. Since $\Delta < 0$, there are no real roots for this case.
2. $2 + x - x^{2} = -4 \implies x^{2} - x - 6 = 0$. We can factor this as $(x - 3)(x + 2) = 0$. The roots are $x = 3$ and $x = -2$.

Thus, there are exactly two real roots. The answer is (c).
