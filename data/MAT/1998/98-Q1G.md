## 题目
The number of solutions to the equation $\sin \frac{x}{2} + \cos x = 1$ in the range $0 \leqslant x \leqslant 2\pi$ is

(i) $0$, (ii) $1$, (iii) $2$, (iv) $3$, (v) none of these.

## 备注

### 我的备注

### AI备注


## 答案
(v)

## 解析
Using the double angle identity $\cos x = 1 - 2\sin^2\frac{x}{2}$, the equation becomes

$$\sin\frac{x}{2} + 1 - 2\sin^2\frac{x}{2} = 1 \implies \sin\frac{x}{2}\left(1 - 2\sin\frac{x}{2}\right) = 0$$

This gives $\sin\frac{x}{2} = 0$ or $\sin\frac{x}{2} = \frac{1}{2}$. For $x \in [0, 2\pi]$, we have $\frac{x}{2} \in [0, \pi]$. The equation $\sin\frac{x}{2} = 0$ yields $\frac{x}{2} = 0, \pi \implies x = 0, 2\pi$. The equation $\sin\frac{x}{2} = \frac{1}{2}$ yields $\frac{x}{2} = \frac{\pi}{6}, \frac{5\pi}{6} \implies x = \frac{\pi}{3}, \frac{5\pi}{3}$. There are exactly $4$ solutions, which corresponds to none of the first four options. The answer is (v).
