## 题目
Let $P$ and $Q$ be the points with co-ordinates $(7, 1)$ and $(11, 2)$.

**(a)**

The *mirror image* of the point $P$ in the $x$-axis is the point $R$ with co-ordinates $(7, -1)$. Mark the points $P$, $Q$ and $R$ on the grid provided.

**(b)**

Consider paths from $P$ to $Q$ each of which consists of two straight line segments $PX$ and $XQ$ where $X$ is a point on the $x$-axis. Find the length of the shortest such path, giving clear reasoning for your answer. (You may refer to the diagram to help your explanation, if you wish.)

**(c)**

Sketch in the line $\ell$ with equation $y = x$. Find the co-ordinates of $S$, the mirror image in the line $\ell$ of the point $Q$, and mark in the point $S$.

**(d)**

Consider paths from $P$ to $Q$ each of which consists of three straight line segments $PY$, $YZ$ and $ZQ$, where $Y$ is on the $x$-axis and $Z$ is on the line $\ell$. Find the length of the shortest such path, giving clear reasoning for your answer.

## 解析
**(a)** $P = (7,1)$ and $Q = (11,2)$ lie above the $x$-axis; $R = (7,-1)$ is directly below $P$, at the same distance from the axis.

**(b)** For any point $X$ on the $x$-axis, reflection in that axis fixes $X$ and sends $P$ to $R$, so

$$PX = RX \qquad \text{and hence} \qquad PX + XQ = RX + XQ.$$

By the triangle inequality $RX + XQ \geqslant RQ$, with equality exactly when $X$ lies on the segment $RQ$. Since $R$ is below the axis and $Q$ above it, that segment does cross the axis, so the minimum is attained and equals

$$RQ = \sqrt{(11-7)^2 + \left(2-(-1)\right)^2} = \sqrt{16+9} = 5.$$

The shortest path has length $5$.

**(c)** Reflection in $\ell : y = x$ interchanges the coordinates, so the mirror image of $Q = (11,2)$ is

$$S = (2,11).$$

**(d)** Reflect the first leg in the $x$-axis and the last leg in $\ell$: for $Y$ on the $x$-axis and $Z$ on $\ell$,

$$PY = RY \qquad \text{and} \qquad ZQ = ZS,$$

so the total length is

$$PY + YZ + ZQ = RY + YZ + ZS \geqslant RS,$$

with equality when $Y$ and $Z$ both lie on the straight segment $RS$, in that order. The segment from $R=(7,-1)$ to $S=(2,11)$ does meet the $x$-axis first and then $\ell$: parametrising as $(7,-1) + t(-5,12)$, it crosses $y=0$ at $t = \tfrac{1}{12}$ and $y=x$ at $t = \tfrac{8}{17}$, and $\tfrac{1}{12} < \tfrac{8}{17}$. So the minimum is attained, and equals

$$RS = \sqrt{(7-2)^2 + (-1-11)^2} = \sqrt{25 + 144} = \sqrt{169} = 13.$$

The shortest such path has length $13$.
