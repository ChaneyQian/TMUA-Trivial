## 题目
With an unlimited supply of black pebbles and white pebbles, there are $4$ ways in which you can put two of them in a row: $BB$, $BW$, $WB$ and $WW$.

**(a)**

Write down the $8$ different ways in which you can put three of the pebbles in a row. In how many different ways can you put $N$ of the pebbles in a row?

**(b)**

Suppose now that you are not allowed to put black pebbles next to each other: with two pebbles there are now only $3$ ways of putting them in a row, because $BB$ is forbidden.

Write down the $5$ different ways that are still allowed for three pebbles.

Now let $r_N$ be the number of possible arrangements for $N$ pebbles in a row, still under the no-two-black-together restriction, so that $r_2 = 3$ and $r_3 = 5$.

**(c)**

Show that for $N \geqslant 4$ we have $r_N = r_{N-1} + r_{N-2}$. [Hint: Consider separately the two possible cases for the colour of the last pebble.]

**(d)**

Finally, suppose that we impose the further restriction that the first pebble and the last pebble cannot both be black. For $N$ pebbles call the number of such arrangements $w_N$, so that for example $w_3 = 4$ (although $r_3 = 5$, the arrangement $BWB$ is now forbidden).

When $N \geqslant 5$, write down a formula for $w_N$ in terms of the numbers $r_i$, and explain why it is correct.

## 解析
**(a)** The eight rows of three are

$$BBB,\; BBW,\; BWB,\; BWW,\; WBB,\; WBW,\; WWB,\; WWW.$$

Each of the $N$ positions is filled independently in $2$ ways, so there are $2^N$ ways of putting $N$ pebbles in a row.

**(b)** Discarding the three rows above that contain $BB$ leaves

$$BWB,\; BWW,\; WBW,\; WWB,\; WWW,$$

so $r_3 = 5$.

**(c)** Split the allowed arrangements of $N$ pebbles according to the colour of the last pebble.

* If the last pebble is white, the first $N-1$ pebbles may be any allowed arrangement of $N-1$ pebbles, and appending a white one never creates a $BB$. This gives $r_{N-1}$ arrangements.
* If the last pebble is black, the pebble before it must be white. The first $N-2$ pebbles then form any allowed arrangement of $N-2$ pebbles, since the $(N-2)$nd pebble is followed by a white one and so is unconstrained. This gives $r_{N-2}$ arrangements.

The two cases are mutually exclusive and cover everything, so for $N \geqslant 4$

$$r_N = r_{N-1} + r_{N-2}.$$

(With $r_2 = 3$ and $r_3 = 5$ this gives $r_4 = 8$, $r_5 = 13$, $r_6 = 21$, and so on.)

**(d)** For $N \geqslant 5$,

$$w_N = r_N - r_{N-4}.$$

The reasoning is that $w_N$ counts the allowed arrangements minus those in which the first and last pebbles are *both* black. In such an arrangement the second and the $(N-1)$st pebbles are forced to be white, so it looks like

$$B\,W\,\underbrace{\ast\cdots\ast}_{N-4}\,W\,B,$$

and the middle $N-4$ pebbles may be any allowed arrangement of that many pebbles — no new $BB$ can be created at either join, because each end of the middle block is adjacent to a white pebble. So the number of forbidden arrangements is exactly $r_{N-4}$, and subtracting gives the formula.

(Check: $w_5 = r_5 - r_1 = 13 - 2 = 11$ and $w_6 = r_6 - r_2 = 21 - 3 = 18$.)
