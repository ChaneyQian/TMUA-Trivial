## 题目
**(a)**

For what values of the constant $k$ does the quadratic equation
$$
x^{2} - 2x - 1 = k
$$
have:
(i) no real solutions;
(ii) one real solution;
(iii) two real solutions.

**(b)**

Showing your working, express $(x^{2} - 2x - 1)^{2}$ as a polynomial of degree $4$ in $x$.

**(c)**

Show that the quartic equation
$$
x^{4} - 4x^{3} + 2x^{2} + 4x + 1 = h
$$
has exactly two real solutions if either $h = 0$ or $h > 4$.

Show that there is no value of $h$ such that the above quartic equation has just one real solution.

## 解析
**(a)** Rearranging, $x^2 - 2x - (1+k) = 0$, whose discriminant is

$$4 + 4(1+k) = 4(2+k).$$

So the equation has

* **(i)** no real solutions when $k < -2$;
* **(ii)** exactly one real solution when $k = -2$;
* **(iii)** two real solutions when $k > -2$.

**(b)** Squaring the trinomial term by term, with $A = x^2$, $B = -2x$, $C = -1$:

$$A^2 = x^4, \quad B^2 = 4x^2, \quad C^2 = 1, \quad 2AB = -4x^3, \quad 2AC = -2x^2, \quad 2BC = 4x.$$

Adding these,

$$\left(x^2-2x-1\right)^2 = x^4 - 4x^3 + 2x^2 + 4x + 1.$$

**(c)** By (b) the quartic equation is exactly

$$\left(x^2-2x-1\right)^2 = h.$$

Put $u = x^2 - 2x - 1$, so the equation becomes $u^2 = h$. By part (a), for a given value $u = k$ the equation $x^2-2x-1 = k$ has two real solutions if $k > -2$, one if $k = -2$, and none if $k < -2$.

*Case $h = 0$.* Then $u = 0$ is the only possibility, and since $0 > -2$ this gives exactly **two** real solutions of the quartic (namely $x = 1 \pm \sqrt2$).

*Case $h > 4$.* Then $u = \sqrt h$ or $u = -\sqrt h$, and $\sqrt h > 2$. The value $u = \sqrt h > 0 > -2$ contributes two real solutions; the value $u = -\sqrt h < -2$ contributes none. So there are exactly **two** real solutions.

This proves the first assertion. For the second, run through the remaining values of $h$:

* $h < 0$: $u^2 = h$ is impossible, so there are no real solutions;
* $0 < h < 4$: then $\sqrt h < 2$, so $u = \sqrt h$ gives two solutions and $u = -\sqrt h > -2$ gives two more — four in all, and they are distinct because the two values of $u$ differ;
* $h = 4$: $u = 2$ gives $x^2-2x-3 = 0$, i.e. $x = 3$ or $x = -1$, while $u = -2$ gives $x^2-2x+1 = 0$, i.e. $x = 1$ — three solutions in all.

So the number of real solutions is always $0$, $2$, $3$ or $4$, and there is no value of $h$ for which the quartic has just one real solution.
