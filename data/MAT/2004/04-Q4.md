## 题目
![[Image/04-Q4-fig1.jpg]]

The triangle $ABC$, drawn above, has sides $BC$, $CA$ and $AB$ of length $a$, $b$ and $c$ respectively, and the angles at $A$, $B$ and $C$ are $\alpha$, $\beta$ and $\gamma$.

**(a)**

Show that the area of $ABC$ equals $\dfrac{1}{2} bc \sin \alpha$.

Deduce the sine rule
$$
\dfrac{a}{\sin \alpha} = \dfrac{b}{\sin \beta} = \dfrac{c}{\sin \gamma}.
$$

**(b)**

In the triangle above, let $P$, $Q$ and $R$ respectively be the feet of the perpendiculars from $A$ to $BC$, $B$ to $CA$, and $C$ to $AB$, as shown.

Prove that
$$
\text{Area of } PQR = \bigl(1 - \cos^{2}\alpha - \cos^{2}\beta - \cos^{2}\gamma\bigr) \times (\text{Area of } ABC).
$$

For what triangles $ABC$, with angles $\alpha$, $\beta$, $\gamma$, does the equation
$$
\cos^{2}\alpha + \cos^{2}\beta + \cos^{2}\gamma = 1
$$
hold?

## 解析
**(a)** Take $AB$ as the base, of length $c$. The perpendicular from $C$ to the line $AB$ has length $b\sin\alpha$, since in the right-angled triangle formed with $A$ the hypotenuse is $CA = b$ and the angle at $A$ is $\alpha$. Hence

$$\text{Area of } ABC = \frac12 \times c \times b\sin\alpha = \frac12 bc\sin\alpha.$$

By the same argument with the other two sides as base,

$$\text{Area} = \frac12 bc\sin\alpha = \frac12 ca \sin\beta = \frac12 ab\sin\gamma.$$

Dividing all three expressions by $\tfrac12 abc$ gives

$$\frac{\sin\alpha}{a} = \frac{\sin\beta}{b} = \frac{\sin\gamma}{c},$$

and taking reciprocals gives the sine rule $\dfrac{a}{\sin\alpha} = \dfrac{b}{\sin\beta} = \dfrac{c}{\sin\gamma}$.

**(b)** Consider the corner triangle $AQR$. In the right-angled triangle $ABQ$ the right angle is at $Q$, the hypotenuse is $AB = c$ and the angle at $A$ is $\alpha$, so

$$AQ = c\cos\alpha.$$

Likewise in the right-angled triangle $ACR$ the right angle is at $R$, the hypotenuse is $AC = b$ and the angle at $A$ is $\alpha$, so $AR = b\cos\alpha$. The triangle $AQR$ has the angle $\alpha$ between these two sides, so by part (a)

$$\text{Area of } AQR = \frac12 \, AQ \cdot AR \, \sin\alpha = \frac12 \left(c\cos\alpha\right)\left(b\cos\alpha\right)\sin\alpha = \cos^2\alpha \times \text{Area of } ABC.$$

By the same computation at the other two vertices,

$$\text{Area of } BRP = \cos^2\beta \times \text{Area of } ABC, \qquad \text{Area of } CPQ = \cos^2\gamma \times \text{Area of } ABC.$$

For the acute triangle drawn, $P$, $Q$, $R$ lie on the sides, and the three corner triangles together with $PQR$ make up the whole of $ABC$ without overlap. Subtracting,

$$\text{Area of } PQR = \left(1 - \cos^2\alpha - \cos^2\beta - \cos^2\gamma\right)\times \text{Area of } ABC.$$

For the final part, use the identity

$$\cos^2\alpha + \cos^2\beta + \cos^2\gamma + 2\cos\alpha\cos\beta\cos\gamma = 1,$$

valid in any triangle. To see it, substitute $\gamma = \pi - \alpha - \beta$, so $\cos\gamma = \sin\alpha\sin\beta - \cos\alpha\cos\beta$; then

$$\cos^2\gamma + 2\cos\alpha\cos\beta\cos\gamma = \cos\gamma\left(\cos\gamma + 2\cos\alpha\cos\beta\right) = \sin^2\alpha\sin^2\beta - \cos^2\alpha\cos^2\beta,$$

since the two brackets are $\sin\alpha\sin\beta \mp \cos\alpha\cos\beta$. Adding $\cos^2\alpha + \cos^2\beta$ gives

$$\cos^2\alpha\left(1-\cos^2\beta\right) + \sin^2\alpha\sin^2\beta + \cos^2\beta = \sin^2\beta\left(\cos^2\alpha + \sin^2\alpha\right) + \cos^2\beta = 1.$$

Consequently

$$\cos^2\alpha+\cos^2\beta+\cos^2\gamma = 1 \iff \cos\alpha\cos\beta\cos\gamma = 0,$$

which happens exactly when one of the angles is $\tfrac{\pi}{2}$. So the equation holds precisely for the **right-angled** triangles. (This is consistent with (b): if, say, $\gamma = \tfrac\pi2$ then the feet $P$ and $Q$ both coincide with $C$, so $PQR$ collapses to a segment of zero area.)
