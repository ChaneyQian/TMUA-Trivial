## 题目
Let
$$
f(x) = \begin{cases}
x + 1 & \text{for } 0 \leqslant x \leqslant 1; \\
2x^{2} - 6x + 6 & \text{for } 1 \leqslant x \leqslant 2.
\end{cases}
$$

**(a)**

On the axes provided below, sketch a graph of $y = f(x)$ for $0 \leqslant x \leqslant 2$, labelling any turning points and the values attained at $x = 0, 1, 2$.
![[Image/04-Q3-fig1.jpg]]

**(b)**

For $1 \leqslant t \leqslant 2$, define
$$
g(t) = \int_{t-1}^{t} f(x) \, \mathrm{d}x.
$$
Express $g(t)$ as a cubic in $t$.

**(c)**

Calculate and factorize $g'(t)$.

**(d)**

What are the minimum and maximum values of $g(t)$ for $t$ in the range $1 \leqslant t \leqslant 2$?

## 解析
**(a)** On $0 \leqslant x \leqslant 1$ the graph is the straight line from $(0,1)$ to $(1,2)$. On $1 \leqslant x \leqslant 2$ it is part of the parabola $y = 2x^2-6x+6 = 2\left(x-\tfrac32\right)^2 + \tfrac32$, with a minimum turning point at $\left(\tfrac32, \tfrac32\right)$ and endpoints $(1,2)$ and $(2,2)$. The two pieces agree at $x=1$, where both give $y=2$, so the graph is continuous. The values at $x = 0, 1, 2$ are $1$, $2$, $2$.

**(b)** For $1 \leqslant t \leqslant 2$ the interval of integration $[t-1,\,t]$ straddles the join at $x=1$, since $0 \leqslant t-1 \leqslant 1 \leqslant t$. So

$$g(t) = \int_{t-1}^{1}(x+1)\,\mathrm{d}x + \int_{1}^{t}\left(2x^2-6x+6\right)\mathrm{d}x.$$

The first integral is

$$\left[\frac{x^2}{2}+x\right]_{t-1}^{1} = \frac32 - \frac{(t-1)^2}{2} - (t-1) = 2 - \frac{t^2}{2},$$

and the second is

$$\left[\frac{2x^3}{3} - 3x^2 + 6x\right]_{1}^{t} = \frac{2t^3}{3} - 3t^2 + 6t - \frac{11}{3}.$$

Adding,

$$g(t) = \frac{2}{3}t^3 - \frac{7}{2}t^2 + 6t - \frac{5}{3}.$$

(As a check, $g(1) = \tfrac32 = \int_0^1 (x+1)\,\mathrm{d}x$ and $g(2) = \tfrac53 = \int_1^2\left(2x^2-6x+6\right)\mathrm{d}x$.)

**(c)** Differentiating,

$$g'(t) = 2t^2 - 7t + 6 = (2t-3)(t-2).$$

(This agrees with $g'(t) = f(t) - f(t-1) = \left(2t^2-6t+6\right) - t$.)

**(d)** On $1 \leqslant t \leqslant 2$ the factor $t-2$ is $\leqslant 0$, so $g'(t) \geqslant 0$ for $t < \tfrac32$ and $g'(t) \leqslant 0$ for $t > \tfrac32$. Hence $g$ increases up to $t = \tfrac32$ and decreases afterwards, so the maximum is

$$g\left(\tfrac32\right) = \frac{9}{4} - \frac{63}{8} + 9 - \frac53 = \frac{41}{24},$$

and the minimum is the smaller of the two endpoint values

$$g(1) = \frac32 = \frac{36}{24}, \qquad g(2) = \frac53 = \frac{40}{24}.$$

So on $1 \leqslant t \leqslant 2$ the minimum value of $g$ is $\dfrac{3}{2}$ (at $t=1$) and the maximum value is $\dfrac{41}{24}$ (at $t = \tfrac32$).
