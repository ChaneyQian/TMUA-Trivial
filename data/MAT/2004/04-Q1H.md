## 解析
We can rewrite the equation $x^{2}y^{2}(x + y) = 1$ as:

$$x + y = \frac{1}{x^{2}y^{2}}$$

Since $x^{2}y^{2}$ is always strictly positive for non-zero $x$ and $y$, the quantity $\frac{1}{x^{2}y^{2}}$ must also be strictly positive. This implies that for every point on the curve, we must have:

$$x + y > 0$$

This inequality means that the entire curve must lie strictly in the region above the line $y = -x$. It cannot have any points in the third quadrant (where $x<0, y<0$) or the regions of the second and fourth quadrants where $x+y \leqslant 0$. 
Furthermore, the curve has asymptotes at $x = 0$, $y = 0$, and $x + y = 0$. The sketch that accurately reflects these conditions is (c). The answer is (c).
