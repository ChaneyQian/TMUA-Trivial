## 题目
**(a)**

Show that the line $y = mx + c$ passes through the point $(1, 1)$ if $c = 1 - m$.

**(b)**

Let $L$ be a line with gradient $m > 0$, which passes through $(1, 1)$. Find the equation of the line $L'$ which is perpendicular to $L$, and which passes through the point $(1, a)$, given $a \neq 1$.

**(c)**

Find the area of the triangle which has $(1, 1)$ and $(1, a)$ as two of its vertices and the intersection of $L$ and $L'$ as the third vertex.

**(d)**

For what value of $m$ is the triangle isosceles (two sides of equal length)?

## 解析
**(a)** Putting $x = 1$ into $y = mx + c$ gives $y = m + c$. This equals $1$ exactly when $c = 1 - m$, so the line passes through $(1,1)$ if $c = 1-m$.

**(b)** By (a), $L$ has equation $y = mx + 1 - m$. A line perpendicular to it has gradient $-\dfrac1m$ (recall $m>0$, so this is defined), and passing through $(1,a)$ it is

$$y - a = -\frac{1}{m}(x-1), \qquad \text{that is} \qquad L' : \; y = a - \frac{x-1}{m}.$$

**(c)** Let $C$ be the intersection of $L$ and $L'$. Equating the two expressions for $y$ and multiplying by $m$,

$$m^2 x + m - m^2 = am - x + 1 \implies \left(m^2+1\right)x = m^2 - m + am + 1,$$

so that

$$x_C - 1 = \frac{m^2 - m + am + 1 - \left(m^2+1\right)}{m^2+1} = \frac{m(a-1)}{m^2+1}.$$

The other two vertices $(1,1)$ and $(1,a)$ both lie on the vertical line $x = 1$, so take that side as the base. Its length is $|a-1|$, and the corresponding height is the horizontal distance from $C$ to the line $x=1$, namely $|x_C - 1|$. Hence

$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}\,|a-1| \cdot \frac{m\,|a-1|}{m^2+1} = \frac{m(a-1)^2}{2\left(m^2+1\right)}.$$

**(d)** Write $P = (1,1)$ and $Q = (1,a)$. Since $L \perp L'$, the triangle has a right angle at $C$, so $PQ$ is the hypotenuse and is strictly the longest side. The triangle can therefore only be isosceles by having its two legs equal, $CP = CQ$.

Using $x_C - 1 = \dfrac{m(a-1)}{m^2+1}$ and the fact that $C$ lies on $L$ (gradient $m$) and on $L'$ (gradient $-1/m$),

$$y_C - 1 = m\left(x_C-1\right) = \frac{m^2(a-1)}{m^2+1}, \qquad y_C - a = -\frac{x_C-1}{m} = -\frac{a-1}{m^2+1}.$$

Therefore

$$CP^2 = \left(x_C-1\right)^2\left(1+m^2\right) = \frac{m^2(a-1)^2}{m^2+1}, \qquad CQ^2 = \left(x_C-1\right)^2 + \left(y_C-a\right)^2 = \frac{(a-1)^2}{m^2+1}.$$

(As a check, $CP^2 + CQ^2 = (a-1)^2 = PQ^2$, as Pythagoras requires.) Setting $CP^2 = CQ^2$ gives $m^2 = 1$, and since $m > 0$,

$$m = 1.$$
