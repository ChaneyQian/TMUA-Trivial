## 题目
The derivative of $x\mathrm{e}^{-x^{2}}\cos\!\left(\dfrac{1}{x}\right)$ is

(a) $-\dfrac{1}{x}\mathrm{e}^{-x^{2}}\sin\!\left(\dfrac{1}{x}\right) - 2x^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-x^{2}}\cos\!\left(\dfrac{1}{x}\right) + \mathrm{e}^{-x^{2}}\cos\!\left(\dfrac{1}{x}\right)$

(b) $\dfrac{1}{x}\mathrm{e}^{-x^{2}}\sin\!\left(\dfrac{1}{x}\right) - 2x^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-x^{2}}\cos\!\left(\dfrac{1}{x}\right) + \mathrm{e}^{-x^{2}}\cos\!\left(\dfrac{1}{x}\right)$

(c) $\dfrac{1}{x}\mathrm{e}^{-x^{2}}\sin\!\left(\dfrac{1}{x}\right) + 2x^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-x^{2}}\cos\!\left(\dfrac{1}{x}\right) + \mathrm{e}^{-x^{2}}\cos\!\left(\dfrac{1}{x}\right)$

(d) $\dfrac{1}{x}\mathrm{e}^{-x^{2}}\cos\!\left(\dfrac{1}{x}\right) - 2x^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-x^{2}}\cos\!\left(\dfrac{1}{x}\right) + \mathrm{e}^{-x^{2}}\cos\!\left(\dfrac{1}{x}\right)$.

## 解析
We differentiate $x \mathrm{e}^{-x^2} \cos\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)$ using the product rule on $u = x \mathrm{e}^{-x^2}$ and $v = \cos\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)$.
First, $u' = \mathrm{e}^{-x^2} - 2x^2 \mathrm{e}^{-x^2}$.
Next, using the chain rule, $v' = -\sin\left(\frac{1}{x}\right) \cdot \left(-\frac{1}{x^2}\right) = \frac{1}{x^2} \sin\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)$.
The derivative is $u'v + uv'$, which is

$$\left(\mathrm{e}^{-x^2} - 2x^2 \mathrm{e}^{-x^2}\right) \cos\left(\frac{1}{x}\right) + x \mathrm{e}^{-x^2} \cdot \frac{1}{x^2} \sin\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)$$

Expanding this gives exactly option (b). The answer is (b).
