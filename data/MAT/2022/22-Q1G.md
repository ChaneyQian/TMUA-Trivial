## 题目
For all real $n$ , it is the case that $n^4 + 1 = (n^2 + \sqrt{2} n + 1)(n^2 - \sqrt{2} n + 1)$ . From this we may deduce that $n^4 + 4$ is

(a) never a prime number for any positive whole number $n$ .

(b) a prime number for exactly one positive whole number $n$ .

(c) a prime number for exactly two positive whole numbers $n$ .

(d) a prime number for exactly three positive whole numbers $n$ .

(e) a prime number for exactly four positive whole numbers $n$ .

### 我的备注

### AI备注

## 答案
B

## 解析
This expression is true for all real $n ,$ so write $n = m / \sqrt { 2 }$ and multiply through by 4 to get

$$
m^{4} + 4 = (m^{2} + 2 m + 2) (m^{2} - 2 m + 2)
$$

Relabelling m as n gives us an interesting way to factorise $n^{4} + 4 ,$ , so this expression is not prime unless at least one of $m^{2} \pm 2 m + 2 = 1$ . Completing the square(s) gives $( m \pm 1 ) ^{2} + 1 = 1$ so $m = \pm 1$ . We want positive whole numbers, so we just need to check that $1 + 4 = 5$ is prime to find the only example of a prime number of this form.

The answer is (b).
