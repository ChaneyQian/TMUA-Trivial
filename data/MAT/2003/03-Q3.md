## 题目
Let $O$, $P$, $P_{1}$, $P_{2}$ be the points in the $(x, y)$-plane with coordinates $(0, 0)$, $(s, 1/s)$, $(s_{1}, 1/s_{1})$, $(s_{2}, 1/s_{2})$ respectively.

**(a)**

Using the axes below, sketch the curve traced out by $P$ as $s$ varies over non-zero real values, and find an equation for the curve in the form $y = f(x)$.
![[Image/03-Q3-fig1.jpg]]

**(b)**

Write down the equation of the straight line $PP_{1}$ joining $P$ to $P_{1}$, giving your answer in the form $y = m_{1}x + c_{1}$.

**(c)**

Show that the line $PP_{1}$ is perpendicular to $PP_{2}$ if, and only if, $s_{1}s_{2} = -1/s^{2}$.

**(d)**

Let $m_{1}$, $m_{2}$, $n_{1}$, $n_{2}$ be the gradients of the lines $PP_{1}$, $PP_{2}$, $OP_{1}$, $OP_{2}$ respectively. Show that
$$
\left(\dfrac{m_{1}}{m_{2}}\right)^{2} = \dfrac{n_{1}}{n_{2}}.
$$

## 解析
**(a)** Writing $x = s$ and $y = 1/s$ gives $xy = 1$, so $P$ traces the rectangular hyperbola

$$y = \frac{1}{x}.$$

As $s$ runs over all non-zero reals both branches are obtained: the branch in the first quadrant for $s>0$ and the branch in the third quadrant for $s<0$. Each branch approaches the two axes asymptotically.

**(b)** The gradient of $PP_1$ is

$$m_1 = \frac{\dfrac1s - \dfrac{1}{s_1}}{s - s_1} = \frac{\dfrac{s_1 - s}{ss_1}}{s-s_1} = -\frac{1}{ss_1}.$$

Using the point $P = \left(s, \tfrac1s\right)$,

$$y - \frac1s = -\frac{1}{ss_1}(x - s) \implies y = -\frac{1}{ss_1}x + \frac{1}{s_1} + \frac{1}{s},$$

so

$$m_1 = -\frac{1}{ss_1}, \qquad c_1 = \frac{1}{s} + \frac{1}{s_1}.$$

**(c)** By the same calculation the gradient of $PP_2$ is $m_2 = -\dfrac{1}{ss_2}$. Two lines are perpendicular exactly when the product of their gradients is $-1$, so $PP_1 \perp PP_2$ if and only if

$$\left(-\frac{1}{ss_1}\right)\left(-\frac{1}{ss_2}\right) = -1 \iff \frac{1}{s^2 s_1 s_2} = -1 \iff s_1 s_2 = -\frac{1}{s^2},$$

as required.

**(d)** The line $OP_1$ joins $(0,0)$ to $\left(s_1, \tfrac{1}{s_1}\right)$, so

$$n_1 = \frac{1/s_1}{s_1} = \frac{1}{s_1^{2}}, \qquad \text{and similarly} \qquad n_2 = \frac{1}{s_2^{2}}.$$

From (b) and (c),

$$\frac{m_1}{m_2} = \frac{-1/(ss_1)}{-1/(ss_2)} = \frac{s_2}{s_1}, \qquad \text{so} \qquad \left(\frac{m_1}{m_2}\right)^2 = \frac{s_2^{2}}{s_1^{2}}.$$

On the other hand

$$\frac{n_1}{n_2} = \frac{1/s_1^{2}}{1/s_2^{2}} = \frac{s_2^{2}}{s_1^{2}},$$

so the two are equal:

$$\left(\frac{m_1}{m_2}\right)^{2} = \frac{n_1}{n_2}.$$

(Note this holds for all choices of $s, s_1, s_2$, not only in the perpendicular case of (c).)
