## 题目
For which real numbers $x$ does the inequality
$$
\dfrac{x}{x^{2} + 1} \leqslant \dfrac{1}{2}
$$
hold?
(a) for all real numbers $x$;
(b) for real numbers $x \leqslant \dfrac{1}{2}$ and no others;
(c) for real numbers $x \leqslant 1$ and no others;
(d) none of the above.

## 备注

### 我的备注

### AI备注


## 答案
A

## 解析
Since $x^2 + 1 \geqslant 1 > 0$ for all real numbers $x$, we can safely multiply both sides of the inequality by $2(x^2 + 1)$ without flipping the inequality sign:

$$\frac{x}{x^2 + 1} \leqslant \frac{1}{2} \implies 2x \leqslant x^2 + 1$$

Rearranging the terms gives:

$$x^2 - 2x + 1 \geqslant 0 \implies (x - 1)^2 \geqslant 0$$

Since the square of any real number is always non-negative, this inequality holds for all real numbers $x$. The answer is (a).
