## 题目
There are real numbers $x$, $y$ such that precisely one of the statements (a), (b), (c), (d) is true. Which is the true statement?
(a) $x \geqslant 0$
(b) $x < y$
(c) $x^{2} > y^{2}$
(d) $|x| \leqslant |y|$

## 答案
D

## 解析
We are given four statements:
(a) $x \geqslant 0$
(b) $x < y$
(c) $x^2 > y^2$
(d) $|x| \leqslant |y|$

Notice that $x^2 > y^2$ is algebraically equivalent to $|x| > |y|$. 
Therefore, statements (c) and (d) are exact opposites; for any real numbers $x$ and $y$, exactly one of (c) or (d) must be true. 
Since we are given that precisely one of the four statements is true overall, it must be either (c) or (d). This also means that statements (a) and (b) must both be false.
- Since (a) is false, we have $x < 0$.
- Since (b) is false, we have $x \geqslant y$.

Combining these, we have $y \leqslant x < 0$. Because both $x$ and $y$ are negative and $y \leqslant x$, the absolute value of $y$ is greater than or equal to the absolute value of $x$. That is, $|y| \geqslant |x|$, which is statement (d).
Since (d) is true, (c) must be false. Thus, (d) is the unique true statement. The answer is (d).
