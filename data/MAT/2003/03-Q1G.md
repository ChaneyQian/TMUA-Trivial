## 题目
For which of the following do we have
$$
\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} = 2y \ln y?
$$
(a) $y = \mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{e}^{2x}}$
(b) $y = \mathrm{e}^{2\mathrm{e}^{x}}$
(c) $y = \mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{e}^{x^{2}}}$
(d) $y = 2\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{e}^{x}}$

## 备注

### 我的备注

### AI备注


## 答案
A

## 解析
We can test the options by finding $\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}$ using the chain rule. 
For option (a): $y = \mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{e}^{2x}}$. 
Taking the natural logarithm gives $\ln y = \mathrm{e}^{2x}$. 
Differentiating $y = \mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{e}^{2x}}$ with respect to $x$:

$$\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} = \mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{e}^{2x}} \cdot \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}(\mathrm{e}^{2x}) = \mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{e}^{2x}} \cdot 2\mathrm{e}^{2x}$$

Since $y = \mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{e}^{2x}}$ and $\ln y = \mathrm{e}^{2x}$, we can substitute these back into the derivative:

$$\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} = y \cdot 2(\ln y) = 2y\ln y$$

This matches the given differential equation. The answer is (a).
