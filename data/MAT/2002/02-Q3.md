## 题目
Let
$$
f(x) = \left(c - \dfrac{1}{c} - x\right)(4 - 3x^{2}),
$$
where $c$ is a positive constant and $x$ varies over the real numbers.

**(a)**

Show that $f(x)$ has one maximum and one minimum.

**(b)**

Show that the difference between the values of $f(x)$ at its turning points is
$$
\dfrac{4}{9}\left(c + \dfrac{1}{c}\right)^{3}.
$$

**(c)**

What is the least value that the difference in (b) can have for $c > 0$?

## 解析
Write $k = c - \dfrac1c$, so that

$$f(x) = (k-x)\left(4-3x^2\right) = 3x^3 - 3kx^2 - 4x + 4k.$$

**(a)** Differentiating,

$$f'(x) = 9x^2 - 6kx - 4 = \left(3x + \frac{2}{c}\right)\left(3x - 2c\right),$$

as expanding confirms:

$$\left(3x+\frac2c\right)\left(3x-2c\right) = 9x^2 - 6cx + \frac{6}{c}x - 4 = 9x^2 - 6\left(c-\frac1c\right)x - 4.$$

Because $c > 0$, the two stationary points

$$x_1 = -\frac{2}{3c} < 0 < x_2 = \frac{2c}{3}$$

are real and distinct. The leading coefficient of the cubic is $3 > 0$, so $f'$ is positive, then negative, then positive: $f$ has a maximum at $x_1$ and a minimum at $x_2$, one of each.

**(b)** Evaluate $f$ at the two stationary points. At $x_2 = \dfrac{2c}{3}$,

$$k - x_2 = c - \frac1c - \frac{2c}{3} = \frac{c^2-3}{3c}, \qquad 4 - 3x_2^2 = 4 - \frac{4c^2}{3} = \frac{4\left(3-c^2\right)}{3},$$

so $f(x_2) = -\dfrac{4\left(3-c^2\right)^2}{9c}$. At $x_1 = -\dfrac{2}{3c}$,

$$k - x_1 = c - \frac{1}{3c} = \frac{3c^2-1}{3c}, \qquad 4 - 3x_1^2 = 4 - \frac{4}{3c^2} = \frac{4\left(3c^2-1\right)}{3c^2},$$

so $f(x_1) = \dfrac{4\left(3c^2-1\right)^2}{9c^3}$. Subtracting,

$$f(x_1) - f(x_2) = \frac{4}{9c^3}\Bigl[\left(3c^2-1\right)^2 + c^2\left(3-c^2\right)^2\Bigr].$$

The bracket expands to

$$\left(9c^4 - 6c^2 + 1\right) + \left(9c^2 - 6c^4 + c^6\right) = c^6 + 3c^4 + 3c^2 + 1 = \left(c^2+1\right)^3.$$

Hence the difference between the values at the turning points is

$$\frac{4\left(c^2+1\right)^3}{9c^3} = \frac{4}{9}\left(\frac{c^2+1}{c}\right)^3 = \frac{4}{9}\left(c + \frac1c\right)^3.$$

**(c)** For $c > 0$, the AM–GM inequality (or $\left(\sqrt c - 1/\sqrt c\right)^2 \geqslant 0$) gives

$$c + \frac1c \geqslant 2,$$

with equality exactly when $c = 1$. Since $t \mapsto \tfrac49 t^3$ is increasing, the least value of the difference is

$$\frac{4}{9}\times 2^3 = \frac{32}{9},$$

attained at $c = 1$.
