## 题目
For **APPLICANTS IN** $\left\{\begin{array}{l}\textbf{MATHEMATICS} \\ \textbf{MATHEMATICS \& STATISTICS} \\ \textbf{MATHEMATICS \& PHILOSOPHY} \\ \textbf{MATHEMATICS \& COMPUTER SCIENCE}\end{array}\right\}$ **ONLY**.

For a positive whole number n, the function $f_{n} \left( x \right)$ is defined by

$$
f_{n} (x) = \left(x^{2n-1} - 1\right) ^{2}.
$$

(i) On the axes provided opposite, sketch the graph of $y = f_{2} \left( x \right)$ labelling where the graph meets the axes.

(ii) On the same axes sketch the graph of $y = f_{n} \left( x \right)$ where n is a large positive integer.

(iii) Determine

$$
\int_{0} ^{1} f_{n} (x) \mathrm{d} x.
$$

(iv) The positive constants A and B are such that

$$
\int_{0} ^{1} f_{n} (x) \mathrm{d} x \leqslant 1 - \frac {A}{n + B} \text { for   all } n \geqslant 1.
$$

Show that

$$
(3 n - 1) (n + B) \geqslant A (4 n - 1) n,
$$

and explain why $A \leqslant 3 / 4$

(v) When $A = 3 / 4$ , what is the smallest possible value of $B ?$

## 解析
3. (i) [4 marks] We have $f_{2} \left( x \right) = \left( x^{3} - 1 \right) ^{2}$ . Note that $f_{2} \left( x \right) = 0$ only when $x = 1$ and $f_{2} \left( 0 \right) = 1$ ; so the graph crosses the axes only at (1, 0) and $( 0 , 1 )$

Now differentiating $f_{2} \left( x \right) = x^{6} - 2 x^{3} + 1$ gives

$$
f_{2} ^{\prime} (x) = 6 x^{5} - 6 x^{2} = 6 x^{2} \left(x^{3} - 1\right)
$$

is zero only at $x = 0$ and $x = 1$ . As $f_{2} \left( x \right)$ is large for large positive and negative values, and as $f_{2} \left( x \right) \geqslant 0 = f_{2} \left( 1 \right)$ then $f_{2} \left( x \right)$ has a minimum at $x = 1$ and an inflexion point at $x = 0$

The graph of $y = f_{2} ( x )$ is shown with a solid line below.

![[Image/09-Q3-sol1.jpg]]

(ii) [2 marks] Consider $f_{n} \left( x \right) = \left( x^{2n-1} - 1 \right) ^{2}$ when n is large and positive. Then $f_{n} \left( x \right)$ still crosses the axes at $( 1 , 0 )$ and $( 0 , 1 )$ only, is large for large negative and positive values and takes only nonnegative values. For $- 1 < x < 1$ we have that $x^{2n-1} - 1$ is very close to −1 and so $f_{n} \left( x \right)$ is close to 1 in the range $- 1 < x < 1$ . For large values of x it is now the case that $f_{n} \left( x \right)$ increases even more rapidly than $f_{2} \left( x \right)$ does. The graph of $y = f_{9} ( x )$ is shown with a dashed line above.

(iii) [2 marks] Note

$$
\int_{0} ^{1} \left(x^{2n-1} - 1\right) ^{2} \mathrm{d} x = \int_{0} ^{1} \left(x^{4n-2} - 2 x^{2n-1} + 1\right) \mathrm{d} x = \frac {1}{4 n - 1} - \frac {1}{n} + 1.
$$

(iv) [4 marks] So

$$
\int_{0} ^{1} f_{n} (x) \mathrm{d} x \leqslant \frac {1}{4 n - 1} - \frac {1}{n} + 1 \leqslant 1 - \frac {A}{n + B} \iff \frac {3 n - 1}{(4 n - 1) n} \geqslant \frac {A}{n + B}
$$

Rearranging this gives

$$
\begin{array}{r c l} (3 n - 1) (n + B) & \geqslant & A (4 n - 1) n. \\ 3 n^{2} - n - B + 3 B n & \geqslant & 4 A n^{2} - A n \end{array}
$$

Comparing coefficients of $n^{2}$ (the dominant term) we need $3 \geqslant 4 A$ so that $A \leqslant 3 / 4$ . Or one might expand the brackets to get

$$
(3 - 4 A) n^{2} + (3 B + A - 1) n - B \geqslant 0 \quad \text { for } n \geqslant 1;
$$

if $4 A > 3$ the LHS is an "upside-down" parabola and so negative for large n which is a contradiction. Hence $A \leqslant 3 / 4$

(v) [3 marks] When $A = 3 / 4$ , we need

$$
\begin{array}{r c l} 4 (3 n - 1) (n + B) & \geqslant & 3 (4 n - 1) n \qquad \text {for} n \geqslant 1; \\ 12 n^{2} + 12 n B - 4 n - 4 B & \geqslant & 12 n^{2} - 3 n \qquad \text {for} n \geqslant 1; \\ (12 B - 1) n - 4 B & \geqslant & 0 \qquad \text {for} n \geqslant 1. \end{array}
$$

The graph of the LHS against n is a line with gradient $12 B - 1 ;$ so we need $12 B - 1 \geqslant 0$ for a non-decreasing line and also $( 12 B - 1 ) - 4 B \geqslant 0$ for the first $n = 1$ case to hold. So $B \geqslant 1 / 8$ and $1 / 8$ is the smallest value.
