## 题目
The shortest distance between the parabola $y = 3 - x^2$ and the line $x + y = 4$ is

$$\mathbf{A} \quad \frac{3\sqrt{2}}{8}$$

$$\mathbf{B} \quad 1$$

$$\mathbf{C} \quad \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$$

$$\mathbf{D} \quad \frac{2\sqrt{2}}{2}$$

$$\mathbf{E} \quad \frac{3\sqrt{2}}{4}$$

## 备注

## 答案
A

## 解析
A quick sketch shows that the line and parabola do not intersect. Let the closest points on the line and parabola be $P$ and $Q$ respectively. The line $PQ$ is perpendicular to both the line and the parabola, hence the tangent to the parabola at $Q$ is parallel to the line. The line has gradient $- 1$ and the gradient of the parabola is given by $\frac{dy}{dx} = - 2x$, so $Q(\frac{1}{2},\ \frac{11}{4})$. The normal to the parabola at Q has equation $y = x + \frac{9}{4}$ which intersects the line $y = 4 - x$ at $P(\frac{7}{8},\ \frac{25}{8})$. The length $PQ = \sqrt{\left( \frac{7}{8} - \frac{1}{2} \right)^{2} + \left( \frac{25}{8} - \frac{11}{4} \right)^{2}} = \frac{3\sqrt{2}}{8}$. The correct answer is A.
