## 题目
Let $x$ and $y$ be integers. How many integer pairs $(x, y)$ satisfy the inequalities
$$ xy(x - 1)(y - 4) \le 2y(y - 4) - 3(x^2 - x - 2), \quad -2 < x < 3 \quad \text{and} \quad 0 < y < 4? $$

(a) $12$
(b) $16$
(c) $20$
(d) $24$
(e) $28$

## 答案
A

## 解析
This is a question that is worded to make you think that you cannot do it. There is no immediately obvious method to apply to these inequalities, so you have to consider that you may not need to!
The key piece of information that you are only asked to find integer pairs. There are only $3$ integer values of $x$ in $- 2 < x < 3$ ($- 1,\ 0,\ 1$ and $2$) and $3$ integer values of $y$ in $0 < y < 4$ ($1,\ 2$ and $3$). Therefore, there are only $12$ integer pairs to be considered in the first place. The correct answer has to be less than or equal to $12$. The conclusion is that the correct answer is (a) simply because it cannot be (b), (c), (d) or (e).
