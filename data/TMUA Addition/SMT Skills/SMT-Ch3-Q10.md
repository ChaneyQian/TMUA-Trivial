## 题目
Find the value of the expression
$$ \sqrt{16 + 8\sqrt{3}} + 3 - \sqrt{12 - 4\sqrt{3}} + 1 $$

$$\mathbf{A} \quad \sqrt{6} + 12\sqrt{3}$$

$$\mathbf{B} \quad \sqrt{3} - 5$$

$$\mathbf{C} \quad 3$$

$$\mathbf{D} \quad 5 - \sqrt{3}$$

$$\mathbf{E} \quad -3$$

$$\mathbf{F} \quad \sqrt{6} - 12\sqrt{3}$$

$$\mathbf{G} \quad 1$$

## 答案
D

## 解析
Although there are other, much longer, available methods for answering this question, the examiners do drop a hint that there must be an easier approach on this occasion. In particular, for example, why do they write $16 + 8\sqrt{3} + 3$ instead of $19 + 8\sqrt{3}$? This must be done for a reason. If you can quickly spot a value that squares to give $16 + 8\sqrt{3} + 3$, then square rooting simply reverses the process. Considering $(a + b)^{2} \equiv a^{2} + 2ab + b^{2}$:

$16 = 4^{2}$ and $3 = \left( \sqrt{3} \right)^{2}$, so it is worth checking the expansion of $\left( 4 \pm \sqrt{3} \right)^{2} \equiv 16 \pm 8\sqrt{3} + 3$… you are in luck!
$12 = \left( 2\sqrt{3} \right)^{2}$ and $1 = 1^{2}$ , so it is worth checking the expansion of $\left( 2\sqrt{3} \pm 1 \right)^{2} \equiv 12 \pm 4\sqrt{3} + 1$… you are in luck, again, and grateful not to have ‘piled in’ to this question!

Therefore, $\sqrt{16 + 8\sqrt{3} + 3} - \sqrt{12 - 4\sqrt{3} + 1} = \sqrt{\left( 4 + \sqrt{3} \right)^{2}} - \sqrt{\left( 2\sqrt{3} - 1 \right)^{2}} = 4 + \sqrt{3} - \left( 2\sqrt{3} - 1 \right) = 5 - \sqrt{3}$.

The answer is D.
