## 题目
The inequality $x^2(y^2 - 9) < 4(y^2 - 9)$ is satisfied when

(a) $-3 < y < 3$
(b) $-2 < x < 2 \text{ AND } -3 < y < 3$
(c) $\left(-2 < x < 2 \text{ AND } (y < -3 \text{ OR } y > 3)\right) \text{ OR } \left((x < -2 \text{ OR } x > 2) \text{ AND } (-3 < y < 3)\right)$
(d) $\left(-2 < x < 2 \text{ AND } -3 < y < 3\right) \text{ OR } \left((x < -2 \text{ OR } x > 2) \text{ AND } (y < -3 \text{ OR } y > 3)\right)$
(e) $x > 2 \text{ AND } y > 3$

## 答案
C

## 解析
Realising that there is a common factor on both sides of the inequality is key:
$x^{2}\left( y^{2} - 9 \right) < 4\left( y^{2} - 9 \right) \Leftrightarrow x^{2}\left( y^{2} - 9 \right) - 4\left( y^{2} - 9 \right) < 0 \Leftrightarrow \left( x^{2} - 4 \right)\left( y^{2} - 9 \right) < 0$.
So either $x^{2} - 4 \equiv (x - 2)(x + 2) < 0$ and $y^{2} - 9 \equiv (y - 3)(y + 3) > 0$ and $x^{2} - 4 \equiv (x - 2)(x + 2) > 0$ and $y^{2} - 9 \equiv (y - 3)(y + 3) < 0$.
$x^{2} - 4 \equiv (x - 2)(x + 2) < 0$ and $y^{2} - 9 \equiv (y - 3)(y + 3) > 0$ gives $- 2 < x < 2$ and either $y < - 3$ or $y > 3$.

$x^{2} - 4 \equiv (x - 2)(x + 2) > 0$ and $y^{2} - 9 \equiv (y - 3)(y + 3) < 0$ gives $- 3 < y < 3$ and either $x < - 2$ or $x > 2$.
This is option (c), and no other options fully describe the above solution.
