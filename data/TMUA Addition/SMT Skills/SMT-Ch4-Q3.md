## 题目
The maximum distance between a point on the circle $x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 4y + 4 = 0$ and a point on the circle $x^2 + y^2 + 6x + 2y + 6 = 0$ is

$$\mathbf{A} \quad 5 + \sqrt{7}$$

$$\mathbf{B} \quad 13$$

$$\mathbf{C} \quad 5 + \sqrt{37}$$

$$\mathbf{D} \quad \sqrt{45} - 5$$

$$\mathbf{E} \quad 14$$

## 备注

### 我的备注

### AI备注

## 答案
C

## 解析
The centre-radius forms for the circles are $(x - 3)^{2} + (y + 2)^{2} = 9$ and $(x + 3)^{2} + (y + 1)^{2} = 4$. Neither circle contains the centre of the other and so the points that are maximum distance apart lie on the line passing through the centres. The distance between the centres is $\sqrt{6^{2} + 1^{2}} = \sqrt{37}$, and since the circles have radius 3 and 2 respectively, the furthest points are distance $5 + \sqrt{37}$ apart. The correct answer is C.
