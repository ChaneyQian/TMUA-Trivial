## 题目
A train arriving at Edinburgh has 12 passengers.

The passengers got on the train at three different stations:

- 5 at Peterborough
- 4 at Newark
- 3 at York

The passengers leave the train one at a time in a random order.

What is the probability that the first three to leave did **not** all get on the train at the same station?

$$\mathbf{A} \quad \dfrac{3}{11}$$

$$\mathbf{B} \quad \dfrac{41}{44}$$

$$\mathbf{C} \quad \dfrac{103}{110}$$

$$\mathbf{D} \quad \dfrac{19}{20}$$

$$\mathbf{E} \quad \dfrac{21}{22}$$

$$\mathbf{F} \quad \dfrac{43}{44}$$

$$\mathbf{G} \quad \dfrac{54}{55}$$

$$\mathbf{H} \quad \dfrac{219}{220}$$

## 答案
B

## 解析
Work with the complementary event: the first three to leave all boarded at the same station.

The number of ways of choosing three passengers from one station is

$$\binom{5}{3} + \binom{4}{3} + \binom{3}{3} = 10 + 4 + 1 = 15$$

The total number of ways of choosing the first three leavers is $\binom{12}{3} = 220$, so

$$P(\text{all same station}) = \dfrac{15}{220} = \dfrac{3}{44}$$

The required probability is

$$1 - \dfrac{3}{44} = \dfrac{41}{44}$$

which is option B.
