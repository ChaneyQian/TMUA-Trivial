## 题目
The graph of $y = f(x)$ intersects the $x$-axis at exactly two distinct points.

Consider the graphs of the following:

$$y = f(x) + 2$$

$$y = f(x+2)$$

$$y = 2f(x)$$

$$y = 2 - f(x)$$

$$y = f(-2x)$$

How many of these graphs **necessarily** intersect the $x$-axis at exactly two distinct points?

$$\mathbf{A} \quad 0$$

$$\mathbf{B} \quad 1$$

$$\mathbf{C} \quad 2$$

$$\mathbf{D} \quad 3$$

$$\mathbf{E} \quad 4$$

$$\mathbf{F} \quad 5$$

## 答案
D

## 解析
Let $a$ and $b$ be the two distinct roots of $f$, so $f(a) = f(b) = 0$ and these are the only roots.

$y = f(x) + 2$: the roots are the solutions of $f(x) = -2$, which need not number two. Not necessarily.

$y = f(x+2)$: a horizontal translation. The roots are $x = a-2$ and $x = b-2$, still exactly two. Necessarily.

$y = 2f(x)$: $2f(x) = 0 \iff f(x) = 0$, so the roots are unchanged. Necessarily.

$y = 2 - f(x)$: the roots are the solutions of $f(x) = 2$, which need not number two. Not necessarily.

$y = f(-2x)$: $x \mapsto -2x$ is a bijection, so the roots are $x = -\tfrac{a}{2}$ and $x = -\tfrac{b}{2}$, still exactly two. Necessarily.

Three of the five necessarily have exactly two roots, which is option D.
