## 题目
A list of $n$ numbers has mean $m$ and a unique mode $d$.

Two numbers are removed from the list.

The remaining list of numbers also has a unique mode, but this unique mode is not equal to $d$.

The mean of the remaining $n - 2$ numbers is $m + 2$.

What was the unique mode, $d$, of the original list?

$$\mathbf{A} \quad n - m + 2$$

$$\mathbf{B} \quad n - m - 2$$

$$\mathbf{C} \quad n + m - 2$$

$$\mathbf{D} \quad m + n + 2$$

$$\mathbf{E} \quad m - n + 2$$

$$\mathbf{F} \quad m - n - 2$$

## 备注

### 我的备注

### AI备注


## 答案
E

## 解析
Removing two numbers cannot reduce how often $d$ occurs unless the numbers removed are themselves equal to $d$. If either removed number were different from $d$, the count of $d$ would be unchanged while every other count could only fall, so $d$ would remain the unique mode.

Since the mode does change, both removed numbers equal $d$.

Comparing totals before and after:

$$nm - (n-2)(m+2) = 2d$$

$$nm - \bigl(nm + 2n - 2m - 4\bigr) = 2d$$

$$2m - 2n + 4 = 2d$$

Hence $d = m - n + 2$, which is option E.
