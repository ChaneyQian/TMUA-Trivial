## 题目
A triangle is to be drawn with sides that are integer lengths in centimetres, and a total perimeter of $12\ \text{cm}$.

How many different (non-congruent) triangles can be drawn?

$$\mathbf{A} \quad 1$$

$$\mathbf{B} \quad 2$$

$$\mathbf{C} \quad 3$$

$$\mathbf{D} \quad 10$$

$$\mathbf{E} \quad 12$$

## 答案
C

## 解析
If the three (integer) sides are equal there is the 4-4-4 (equilateral) triangle.

If the largest side is $5\ \text{cm}$, then we have two essentially different (non-congruent) triangles possible:

a (right-angled) 3-4-5 triangle (noting that the order of sides is unimportant); and a 2-5-5 (isosceles) triangle.

However, in every other combination of numbers the longest side is exceeded in length by the sum of the two shorter sides. If you imagine constructing the 'triangle' by drawing the long side first and then the shorter sides, one at each end of the longest one, then the shorter sides would not meet. So there cannot be a triangle with a side greater than or equal to 6.
