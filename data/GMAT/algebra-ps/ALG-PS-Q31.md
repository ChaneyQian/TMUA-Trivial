## 题目
If $[x]$ is the greatest integer less than or equal to $x$, and $[\sqrt{x}] = 5$ and $[\sqrt{y}] = 6$, where $x$ and $y$ are positive integers, what is the greatest possible value of $x + y$ ?

## 解析
**Official Solution:**

Some function rounds DOWN a number to the nearest integer. For example:

$[2.7] = 2$ because 2 the largest integer less than or equal to 2.7;

$[3] = 3$ because 3 the largest integer less than or equal to 3;

$[-1.7] = -2$ because -2 the largest integer less than or equal to -1.7.

So, $[\sqrt{x}] = 5$ means that 5 is the largest integer less than or equal to $\sqrt{x}$, which implies that $5 \leq \sqrt{x} < 6$. For any number from that range, the largest integer less than or equal that number will be 5. Square: $25 \leq x < 36$. The greatest integer value of $x$ is 35.

Similarly, $[\sqrt{y}] = 6$ means that 6 is the largest integer less than or equal to $\sqrt{y}$, which implies that $6 \leq \sqrt{y} < 7$. For any number from that range, the largest integer less than or equal that number will be 6. Square: $36 \leq y < 49$. The greatest integer value of $y$ is 48.

The greatest integer value of $x+y=35+48=83$

Answer: D
